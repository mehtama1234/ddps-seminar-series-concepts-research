Talk 119 · Google DeepMind

Yasaman Bahri: A First-Principles Approach to Understanding Deep Learning

This lecture uses statistical physics (how large groups of interacting parts create patterns), exactly solvable models, mean-field theory (averaging effects across many units), and nonlinear dynamics to explain how deep learning works from the ground up.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Deep networks work well in science and AI, yet treating them as unexplainable black boxes stops us from predicting what they'll do, controlling them, and trusting them. The field needs basic principles that answer: why do certain network designs, training methods, and learning behavior actually work?

First-Principles Model

The lecture uses a physics method: find simple regions where the model can be solved exactly, solve them completely, then apply those solutions to understand real networks. When neural networks grow very wide, they connect to Gaussian processes (a way to think about probability distributions over functions) and kernels (similarity measures that let you do learning in hidden spaces). Mean-field and statistical-physics tools describe what happens as networks train and build their internal representations. Nonlinear dynamics provides the language for how the path an optimizer takes unfolds over time.

Key Claims

  • Understanding deep learning is a major unsolved problem for AI and science.
  • Physics gives you tools to derive true principles, not just measure how well something works.
  • When neural networks are very wide, they relate to Gaussian processes and kernel methods.
  • Models simple enough to solve exactly help isolate what actually causes behavior.
  • Mean-field theories can describe the combined behavior of millions of neurons.
  • Nonlinear dynamics helps track how training and internal representations evolve.

Important Terms

Statistical Physics

How macroscopic patterns arise from many tiny interacting components—like how heat emerges from jiggling molecules.

Gaussian Process

A way to assign probabilities to functions (rather than just numbers), connected to neural networks when they become infinitely wide.

Kernel

A function that measures similarity between two things, allowing linear learning to happen in a hidden space of features.

Mean-Field Theory

A method that studies the average combined effects in systems where many units interact, instead of tracking every individual.

Nonlinear Dynamics

The study of systems where what happens next depends on current conditions in a way that isn't just multiplication by a constant.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk directly addresses the study system's focus on first-principles understanding of AI.
  • It builds on discussions of how algorithms improve and how data quality matters by asking what deep learning mechanisms are actually understood.
  • It also connects scientific machine learning to broader learning theory through Gaussian processes, kernels, and training dynamics.

Practical Caution

Exactly solvable limits reveal mechanisms but may leave out finite width (networks that aren't infinitely wide), finite data (real training sets), specific optimizers (like Adam), and design choices that often dominate what happens in practice.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that deep learning theory moves forward by finding simple regions where mechanisms can be solved exactly, not just watched and measured.

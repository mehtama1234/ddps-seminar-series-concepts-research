Talk 119 · Google DeepMind

Yasaman Bahri: A First-Principles Approach to Understanding Deep Learning

This lecture uses statistical physics, exactly solvable models, mean-field theory, and nonlinear dynamics to explain deep learning from first principles.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Deep networks are powerful in science and AI, but treating them as black boxes limits our ability to predict, control, and trust them. The field needs fundamental principles explaining why network architectures, training dynamics, and generalization behavior work.

First-Principles Model

The lecture approaches deep learning using physics-style simplification: identify regimes where the model becomes analytically tractable, solve those regimes, and use the solution to understand real networks. Large-width neural networks connect to Gaussian processes and kernels. Mean-field and statistical-physics tools describe training and representation behavior. Nonlinear dynamics provides language for how optimization trajectories evolve.

Key Claims

  • Understanding deep learning is a grand challenge for scientific AI.
  • Physics provides tools for deriving principles rather than only measuring performance.
  • Large-width neural networks have connections to Gaussian processes and kernels.
  • Exactly solvable models help isolate mechanisms in otherwise complex systems.
  • Mean-field theories can describe collective behavior in large neural networks.
  • Nonlinear dynamics helps analyze training and representation evolution.

Important Terms

Statistical Physics

A field that explains macroscopic behavior from many interacting microscopic components.

Gaussian Process

A distribution over functions, connected to certain infinite-width neural-network limits.

Kernel

A similarity function that defines linear learning in an implicit feature space.

Mean-Field Theory

An approximation that studies average collective effects in large interacting systems.

Nonlinear Dynamics

The study of systems whose evolution is governed by nonlinear interactions.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects directly to the study system's first-principles AI thread.
  • It complements algorithmic-progress and data-progress discussions by asking what deep learning mechanisms are actually understood.
  • It also links scientific ML to broader ML theory through kernels, GP limits, and training dynamics.

Practical Caution

Exactly solvable limits can explain mechanisms but may omit finite-width, finite-data, optimizer, and architecture details that dominate real systems.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that deep learning theory advances by finding simplified regimes where mechanisms can be solved rather than merely observed.

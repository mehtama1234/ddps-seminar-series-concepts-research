Talk 128 · Georgia Institute of Technology

Sung Ha Kang: Identifying Differential Equations from Single Observation with Numerical Methods

This lecture presents numerical methods for identifying governing differential equations from one noisy time-dependent observation.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Equation discovery usually benefits from many trajectories or clean derivative data. In practice, one may have only a single noisy observation, making differentiation, feature selection, and coefficient recovery ill-conditioned.

First-Principles Model

The governing equation is assumed to be a linear combination of candidate linear and nonlinear differential terms. This turns identification into a linear system whose feature matrix contains candidate terms and whose coefficient vector selects the equation. The talk surveys methods including IDENT, successively denoised differentiation, subspace pursuit, weak-form recovery, and group subspace pursuit to improve robustness under noise and limited observations.

Key Claims

  • The setting is differential-equation identification from a single noisy time-dependent dataset.
  • Candidate differential terms define a feature matrix.
  • The unknown equation coefficients are recovered through sparse or structured linear-system methods.
  • Successively denoised differentiation helps stabilize derivative estimates.
  • Weak-form methods improve robustness to high noise and higher-order derivatives.
  • Group subspace pursuit extends sparse recovery to structured term selection.

Important Terms

Equation Identification

Recovering the governing differential equation from observed data.

Feature Matrix

A matrix whose columns are candidate terms evaluated on data.

Subspace Pursuit

A sparse recovery algorithm for selecting a small set of explanatory features.

Weak Form

An integral equation formulation that avoids unstable pointwise derivatives.

Denoised Differentiation

Estimating derivatives after reducing noise in the observed data.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects symbolic regression, weak-form discovery, sparse recovery, and numerical differentiation.
  • It complements the WSINDy/WENDy lecture by focusing on the single-observation regime.
  • It also links to first-principles model discovery where interpretability and data scarcity both matter.

Practical Caution

Single-observation identification is highly sensitive to the candidate library and noise model. Recovering a sparse equation does not prove it is the true governing law without validation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that equation discovery from one trajectory becomes possible only by adding strong structure: sparse libraries, denoising, and weak formulations.

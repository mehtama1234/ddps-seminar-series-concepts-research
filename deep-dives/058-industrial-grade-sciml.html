Talk 58 · RocketML

Santi Adavani: Industrial Grade Scientific Machine Learning

This lecture explains why scientific machine learning in production must solve ambiguity, scalability, and deployability problems before it can replace or work alongside traditional PDE solvers (equations that model how systems change over time) in real applications.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

SciML research has produced many papers and open-source tools, but industrial practitioners still face domain-specific constraints: unclear requirements, integrating with existing solvers, scaling to real problem sizes, deployment infrastructure, validation, and uncertainty over when a neural network approach is better than a traditional forward solver (one that predicts outcomes from known inputs) or inverse solver (one that finds unknown inputs from observed outcomes).

First-Principles Model

An industrial solver is not just an algorithm; it is a production artifact embedded in workflows, hardware, data pipelines, domain constraints, and verification practices. SciML must therefore be evaluated along three axes: whether the problem formulation is unambiguous, whether the method scales to industrial sizes and compute environments, and whether it can be deployed, monitored, and maintained.

Key Claims

  • SciML applies modern deep learning to engineered systems represented by PDEs (equations that model how systems change over space and time).
  • There is strong academic and industrial interest, but practical adoption faces major barriers.
  • Industrial users need a clear path to move from traditional forward and inverse PDE solvers to SciML-based solvers.
  • Ambiguity, scalability, and deployability are central industrial challenges.
  • Open-source packages and publications are not enough for domain-specific production use.
  • The research community can contribute by addressing real deployment-facing constraints, not only method novelty.

Important Terms

Industrial Grade SciML

Scientific machine learning designed for production reliability, scale, integration with existing systems, and maintainability.

Forward PDE Solver

A solver that predicts what a system will do given known starting conditions, geometry, and material properties.

Inverse PDE Solver

A solver that figures out unknown starting conditions or material properties by working backward from what was observed.

Deployability

The ability to install, run, check on, and maintain a model inside a real operational workflow without breaking.

Scalability

The ability of a method to work with realistic industrial problem sizes, data volumes, and available computing power.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture complements Dirk Hartmann's industrial digital-twin talk by focusing on the obstacles to turning SciML into production software.
  • It connects to every discussion in the series about replacing traditional solvers: accuracy alone is not enough for people to adopt it.
  • It also provides a practical lens for evaluating PINNs (physics-informed neural networks), neural operators, and differentiable solvers.

Practical Caution

Do not mistake a research prototype for an industrial solver. Industry needs clear problem definitions, scaling behavior, deployment plans, and validation procedures before moving away from trusted numerical workflows.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that industrial SciML is judged by how well it fits into an existing system. A method becomes useful when it can be specified, scaled, deployed, and trusted.

Talk 58 · RocketML

Santi Adavani: Industrial Grade Scientific Machine Learning

This lecture explains why industrial SciML must solve ambiguity, scalability, and deployability problems before it can replace or augment traditional PDE solvers in real applications.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

SciML research has produced many papers and open-source tools, but industrial practitioners still face domain-specific constraints: messy requirements, solver integration, scaling, deployment infrastructure, validation, and uncertainty over when a neural method is preferable to a traditional forward or inverse PDE solver.

First-Principles Model

An industrial solver is not just an algorithm; it is a production artifact embedded in workflows, hardware, data pipelines, domain constraints, and verification practices. SciML must therefore be evaluated along three axes: whether the problem formulation is unambiguous, whether the method scales to industrial sizes and compute environments, and whether it can be deployed, monitored, and maintained.

Key Claims

  • SciML applies modern deep learning to engineered systems represented by PDEs.
  • There is strong academic and industrial interest, but practical adoption faces major barriers.
  • Industrial users need a transition path from traditional forward and inverse PDE solvers to SciML-based solvers.
  • Ambiguity, scalability, and deployability are central industrial challenges.
  • Open-source packages and publications are not enough for domain-specific production use.
  • The research community can contribute by addressing deployment-facing constraints, not only method novelty.

Important Terms

Industrial Grade SciML

Scientific machine learning designed for production reliability, scale, integration, and maintainability.

Forward PDE Solver

A solver that predicts system behavior from known parameters, geometry, and conditions.

Inverse PDE Solver

A solver that estimates unknown parameters or inputs from observed outputs.

Deployability

The ability to package, run, monitor, and maintain a model inside a real operational workflow.

Scalability

The ability of a method to handle realistic industrial problem sizes, data volumes, and compute constraints.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture complements Dirk Hartmann's industrial digital-twin talk by focusing on SciML productization barriers.
  • It connects to every solver-replacement discussion in the series: accuracy alone is insufficient for adoption.
  • It also provides a practical lens for evaluating PINNs, neural operators, and differentiable solvers.

Practical Caution

Do not mistake a research prototype for an industrial solver. Industry needs clear problem definitions, scaling behavior, deployment plans, and validation procedures before replacing trusted numerical workflows.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that industrial SciML is judged by system fit. A method becomes useful when it can be specified, scaled, deployed, and trusted.

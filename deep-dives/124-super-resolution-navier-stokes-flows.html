Talk 124 · IBM T.J. Watson Research Center

Kyongmin Yeo: Reducing Data Resolution for Better Reconstruction: Super-Resolution of Navier-Stokes Flows

This talk shows why deliberately coarsening data can improve reconstruction of fluid flows from noisy measurements.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

When we want to recover a high-resolution fluid state from low-resolution measurements, noisy data creates a painful choice: keeping every fine-grained observation preserves the nominal resolution but also keeps all the noise. The specific challenge is recovering the true state of a 2D fluid governed by the Navier-Stokes equations from measurements corrupted by random error.

First-Principles Model

The method starts by averaging measurements on a coarse grid, intentionally sacrificing resolution to filter out noise. It then uses a dynamic observer — a system that watches measurements and evolves them forward in time using the physics model — to reconstruct the fine-scale flow. The core insight is that reconstruction quality is not monotone in measurement resolution when observations are noisy; a coarser but cleaner signal can actually be better input to a physics-aware observer than a fine but noisy signal.

Key Claims

  • Super-resolution reconstructs high-resolution fluid states from low-resolution data.
  • For physical systems, empirical results need theoretical understanding.
  • This work studies noisy measurements of 2D Navier-Stokes flows.
  • Averaging over a coarse grid reduces noise at the cost of losing fine resolution.
  • A dynamic observer then reconstructs the full flow state.
  • The key tradeoff is resolution against noise reduction.

Important Terms

Super-Resolution

Recovering high-resolution information from lower-resolution measurements.

Dynamic Observer

A system that combines measurements with a physics model to estimate the true hidden state over time.

Navier-Stokes Flows

Fluid motion governed by the Navier-Stokes equations, which describe how fluids move under forces and viscosity.

Coarse-Grid Averaging

Averaging measurements over larger spatial regions to reduce noise and resolution together.

Noisy Observation

A measurement corrupted by random or systematic error.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects data assimilation (combining measurements with models), observer design (inferring hidden state), super-resolution, and fluid reconstruction.
  • It complements sensor-placement and state-estimation work by showing that how you preprocess measurements — before inference — can improve the final result.
  • It also relates to weak-form methods and denoising approaches because handling noise is central to scientific machine learning.

Practical Caution

Averaging only helps if the lost fine details can be recovered by the physics dynamics and the observer. Averaging too aggressively can erase features that the model cannot rebuild from the coarse information.

Takeaway

Better reconstruction can come from cleaner measurements, not simply from measuring at higher resolution.

Talk 15 · ENSAM Institute of Technology / ESI Group

Francisco Chinesta: Empowering Hybrid Twins From Physics-Informed Artificial Intelligence

This lecture describes hybrid twins as the combination of physics-based models with learned gap models that correct what the physics model misses.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

A classical simulator is only a model of reality. Industrial users increasingly care about real system performance under real loading histories, so the twin must represent both known physics and the unmodeled deviation between simulation and measurement. Pure physics may be incomplete; pure data may be unstructured and unreliable.

First-Principles Model

The lecture's central equation is reality equals known physics plus ignorance. A hybrid twin keeps the physics-based model for the known part and learns a data-driven model of the gap between predictions and measurements. When the physics model and deviation model are combined, the virtual twin becomes closer to the real system and can support diagnosis and prognosis.

Key Claims

  • Industrial modeling is shifting from selling products to guaranteeing performance under real usage.
  • A physics model is an approximation, so mismatch between prediction and measurement should be modeled explicitly.
  • Hybrid twins combine physics-based simulation with a data-driven model of the gap.
  • The learned gap model should respect physical consistency, including thermodynamic constraints when relevant.
  • Once the twin matches reality better, it can support diagnosis from data and prognosis about future behavior.
  • Model order reduction, regression, and machine learning can be viewed as related approximation tools in this hybrid paradigm.

Important Terms

Hybrid Twin

A digital twin that combines mechanistic simulation with learned correction or gap models.

Model Gap

The difference between what a physics model predicts and what the real system measures.

Diagnosis

Using current observations to infer the present state or defect of a system.

Prognosis

Predicting future system behavior or failure based on the updated twin.

Thermodynamic Consistency

A constraint that learned material or process models should not violate basic thermodynamic laws.

Connections Across The Series

  • This is the clearest DDPS framing for hybrid physics-ML systems: learn the missing part, not the whole world from scratch.
  • It connects to robust ROMs because fast reduced physics models often form the core of a real-time twin.
  • It also connects to uncertainty and control because diagnosis and prognosis require the twin to stay aligned with real observations.

Practical Caution

A digital twin is not just a dashboard or a trained surrogate. If it does not separate known physics from learned deviation, it becomes hard to know when it is extrapolating beyond its evidence.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that hybrid twins are gap models around physics: keep what you know, learn what you do not, and update the twin against reality.

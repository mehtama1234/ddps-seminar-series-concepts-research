Talk 68 · Georgia Institute of Technology

Spencer Bryngelson: Competitive Physics-Informed Networks

This lecture introduces competitive PINNs, an adversarial formulation where a discriminator learns to emphasize PINN errors and can achieve much higher PDE-solution accuracy than standard residual-squared PINNs.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Standard PINNs train by minimizing a squared PDE residual, but they often plateau around modest accuracy even on benign PDEs. One suspected reason is conditioning: squaring residuals can effectively square already-large condition numbers from PDE discretization, making high-accuracy optimization difficult.

First-Principles Model

CPINNs turn PDE solving into a minimax game. The solution network tries to minimize PDE violation, while a discriminator is rewarded for finding where the solution network makes mistakes. The exact PDE solution is an optimal strategy of the zero-sum game. This adversarial weighting changes how errors are penalized and avoids some conditioning pathologies of residual-squared training.

Key Claims

  • PINNs use PDE residuals as loss functions but typically struggle to produce high-accuracy solutions.
  • Competitive PINNs introduce an adversarial discriminator trained to predict mistakes made by the PINN.
  • The discriminator and PINN form a zero-sum game whose optimal strategy is the exact PDE solution.
  • The method avoids squaring large PDE-discretization condition numbers.
  • On a Poisson problem, CPINNs achieved errors four orders of magnitude smaller than strong PINN baselines.
  • The approach produced relative errors near single-precision accuracy with consistent epoch-by-epoch decrease.

Important Terms

Physics-Informed Neural Network

A neural network trained to satisfy data and governing differential-equation residuals.

Competitive PINN

An adversarial PINN formulation where a discriminator emphasizes solution errors.

Discriminator

In CPINNs, a learned function rewarded for identifying where the solution network is wrong.

Zero-Sum Game

An optimization setting where one player's gain is the other player's loss.

Condition Number

A measure of numerical sensitivity; large values indicate that solving or optimizing may be unstable.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk directly extends the early PINN-failure lectures with a proposed adversarial remedy.
  • It connects to optimization conditioning as a central explanation for why physics losses can fail.
  • It also pairs with Neural Galerkin and structure-preserving closure talks as alternative ways to make neural PDE solvers more numerically serious.

Practical Caution

Adversarial training can introduce its own stability and tuning challenges. CPINNs should be judged by robust convergence across PDEs, not only by a single favorable benchmark.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that the form of the physics loss matters. If residual-squared training creates bad conditioning, changing the game can change trainability.

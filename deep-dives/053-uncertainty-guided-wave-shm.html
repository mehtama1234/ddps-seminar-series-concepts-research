Talk 53 · University of Florida

Ishan Khurjekar: Uncertainty-Aware Guided Wave Structural Health Monitoring

This lecture explains how ensemble learning and probabilistic methods can make guided-wave structural health monitoring more reliable when temperature, loading, and model errors introduce uncertainty.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Guided-wave SHM systems detect and locate damage by comparing acoustic wave signals across sensor arrays. Real structures experience temperature, loading, and environmental shifts that change how waves travel, so damage-like signal changes can appear even when the structure is healthy. Standard neural networks often become overconfident under these shifts.

First-Principles Model

A guided-wave monitoring system sends sound waves through a structure and records what comes back. Damage changes those signals, but non-damage factors like temperature also change them. Uncertainty-aware learning treats detection and localization as probabilistic inference: ensembles, variational autoencoders (neural networks that learn a probability distribution over compressed signals), adversarial perturbations, and anomaly detection estimate both predictions and confidence when conditions vary.

Key Claims

  • Structural health monitoring is essential for buildings, bridges, aircraft, oil rigs, and other structures that need inspection.
  • Guided waves work well for damage detection and localization because sensor arrays can send and receive sound waves.
  • Temperature and loading shifts create uncertainty by changing how waves travel, even when there is no damage.
  • Deep learning methods need to handle uncertainty because ordinary networks can fail or become overconfident when conditions change.
  • Ensembles of variational autoencoders can improve reliability when temperature varies.
  • Training on simulated perturbations can improve reliability when sound-wave properties are uncertain.

Important Terms

Structural Health Monitoring

Sensing and analysis methods for detecting, locating, and describing damage in structures.

Guided Wave

An acoustic wave trapped and moved by the structure itself, often sent through it to look for damage over distance.

Damage Localization

Estimating where damage is located in a structure.

Ensemble Learning

Combining multiple models to improve reliability and estimate how confident we should be in predictions.

Variational Autoencoder

A neural network that learns a probability distribution over compressed versions of data, and can spot when new data looks different from training data.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects to uncertainty propagation and reliable surrogate modeling through engineering decision-making and risk.
  • It also links to active learning and data augmentation because environmental variation must be included in training data.
  • It is a real-world example of why uncertainty estimates matter for actual engineering use.

Practical Caution

Damage detection is safety-critical. A model must tell the difference between damage and environmental shift, and must say when it is uncertain about inputs outside what it saw during training.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that monitoring is not just classification. It is inference under environmental variation, and uncertainty must be part of what the system outputs.

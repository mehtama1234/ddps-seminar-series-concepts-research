Talk 139 · Johns Hopkins University

Michael Shields: The Nexus of Machine Learning, Physics-Based Modeling, and Uncertainty Quantification

This lecture explains how uncertainty quantification and machine learning interact inside physics-based modeling and scientific foundation models.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Physics-based models contain uncertainties in inputs, parameters, excitations, and sometimes model form. Scientific ML adds further uncertainty from data, training, approximation, and generalization. High-consequence scientific prediction needs both ML and UQ rather than treating uncertainty as an afterthought.

First-Principles Model

UQ characterizes epistemic uncertainty from lack of knowledge and aleatory uncertainty from inherent variability. In SciML, UQ for ML asks how uncertain the learned model is; ML for UQ uses learning to accelerate or improve uncertainty propagation, surrogate modeling, sensitivity analysis, and inverse problems. The lecture frames these roles as increasingly important for broad scientific foundation models.

Key Claims

  • UQ and ML are increasingly important in physics-based computational modeling.
  • Physics-based models are rife with epistemic and aleatory uncertainties.
  • SciML introduces additional uncertainties beyond the original physical model.
  • UQ has become integral to physics-based modeling.
  • The talk distinguishes UQ for ML from ML for UQ.
  • Broadly generalizable scientific foundation models require uncertainty-aware methods.

Important Terms

Uncertainty Quantification

The study and computation of uncertainty in model inputs, parameters, structure, and predictions.

Epistemic Uncertainty

Uncertainty due to limited knowledge that could in principle be reduced with more information.

Aleatory Uncertainty

Uncertainty due to inherent randomness or variability.

UQ for ML

Quantifying uncertainty in machine-learning models and predictions.

ML for UQ

Using machine learning to accelerate or improve uncertainty-quantification workflows.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects trustworthy AI, Bayesian neural operators, multi-fidelity learning, and scientific foundation models.
  • It complements all deployment-facing DDPS lectures by making uncertainty a first-class output.
  • It also links to the user's evaluation discussions: reliability requires knowing when a model may be wrong.

Practical Caution

Uncertainty estimates can be miscalibrated. UQ should be validated with coverage tests, out-of-distribution cases, and sensitivity to model-form assumptions.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that scientific ML is incomplete unless it says how uncertain its predictions are and why.

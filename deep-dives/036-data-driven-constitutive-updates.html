Talk 36 · Columbia University

Steve Sun: Data-Driven Constitutive Updates From Model-Free Poroelasticity To Level Set Plasticity

This lecture shows how to build material models from data, starting with fluids flowing through solids, and moving to interpreting how materials yield and deform.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Simulations of materials need equations that connect stretching and squeezing (strains), internal forces (stresses), fluid pressure, and flow speed. For complex materials, these equations may be unknown, cost too much to measure, or have only sparse experimental data to back them up. Using data directly, without a prebuilt equation, can work, but you need the result to be understandable and to respect the physics.

First-Principles Model

A constitutive update (the local rule for how a piece of material responds) sits inside a larger mechanics solver. In poroelasticity (the mix of solid deformation and fluid flow through rock or soil), data can tell you how strain connects to effective stress, and how pressure gradient connects to Darcy flow velocity. A model-free approach searches through the data points to find a physically sensible response; neural networks or level-set functions (defined surfaces that track transitions) can approximate yield surfaces or plastic deformation when data are scarce.

Key Claims

  • Data-driven material modeling using neural networks goes back decades.
  • The goal is to make material laws readable and to work with the limited data you actually have.
  • Simulations of fluids in solids need both solid-deformation and flow equations.
  • You can switch between data-driven and equation-based updates depending on how much data exists.
  • Fast search methods (like KD-trees, which divide space to speed up lookups) can make data-driven updates efficient.
  • Level-set plasticity (surfaces that track yield boundaries) can learn simplified plasticity models by learning from higher-resolution simulations.

Important Terms

Constitutive Law

An equation or model connecting local mechanical variables: stress (internal force), strain (deformation), pressure, and flow.

Model-Free Mechanics

Simulation that uses measured material data directly, instead of writing down an explicit equation ahead of time.

Poroelasticity

The simultaneous deformation of solid material and flow of fluid through its pores (as in water moving through soil or oil through rock).

Constitutive Manifold

The set of all physically allowed local material states, either measured in experiments or represented by a learned model.

Level-Set Plasticity

A way to describe yielding (when material starts to permanently deform) using a learned or designed surface that marks the boundary between elastic and plastic behavior.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture adds material modeling to the data-driven physics stack: the learned object is often the material law, not the entire PDE.
  • It connects to hybrid twins because combining data and physics equations is most natural at the material-response level.
  • It also connects to model-constrained learning because the full PDE solver limits which learned local responses are actually admissible.

Practical Caution

A material model learned from data can break the global solver if it violates admissibility (physics-allowed states), consistency (the math stays sound step-to-step), or energy conservation. The learned local material model must be tested inside the full coupled simulation, not just on isolated plots of stress versus strain.

Takeaway

The deeper lesson is that data-driven mechanics often does its work at the constitutive update—the local material response. Learn the material behavior from data, but keep it compatible with the overall physics.

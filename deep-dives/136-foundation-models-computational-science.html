Talk 136 · Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory

Youngsoo Choi: Defining Foundation Models for Computational Science: Toward Clarity and Rigor

This lecture asks what should rigorously count as a foundation model for computational science and proposes DD-FEM as one concrete path.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Foundation models work well in language and vision, but in computational science the term risks becoming a marketing word. Without a shared definition, models for fluids, materials, climate, design, and uncertainty quantification may be labeled foundation models without meeting domain-specific standards for generalization, reuse, scalability, and scientific rigor.

First-Principles Model

A computational-science foundation model should be reusable across tasks, scalable across problem families, and grounded in the actual structure of scientific computation. The lecture frames necessary and desirable properties and introduces the Data-Driven Finite Element Method as a concrete route: keep the modularity and mathematical discipline of finite-element numerical methods while adding data-driven machine-learning components that adapt automatically.

Key Claims

  • The foundation-model idea is spreading from language and vision into computational science.
  • The domain needs a shared definition to stop the term from becoming empty.
  • Generalization, reusability, and scalability are central criteria.
  • Scientific foundation models must be judged against computational-science needs, not only machine-learning benchmarks.
  • DD-FEM combines classical numerical-method structure with modern data-driven adaptability.
  • The goal is clarity and rigor rather than picking one single fixed architecture.

Important Terms

Foundation Model

A broadly reusable model trained or designed to handle many different downstream tasks.

Computational Science

Scientific investigation using mathematical models, simulation, and numerical computation to solve real-world problems.

Data-Driven Finite Element Method

A method that combines finite-element structure (which divides space into small regions and solves equations locally) with machine-learning models that adapt to data.

Reusability

The ability of a model or component to handle multiple tasks or systems without needing complete retraining from scratch.

Scalability

The ability to handle larger problems, richer domains, or more demanding calculations without breaking down.

Practical Caution

A large model is not automatically a foundation model for computational science. The label should demand evidence of reusable scientific structure and proven generalization across real problems.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that a scientific foundation model must be defined by reusable scientific capability, not by analogy to large language models alone.

Talk 94 · University of Michigan

Elnaz Rezaian: Data-Driven Balancing Transformation For Predictive Model Order Reduction

This lecture reviews balanced truncation and data-driven ERA-style ROMs for predictive control-oriented model reduction, emphasizing error bounds and bottlenecks in large input-output systems.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Projection-based ROMs can be fast but may fail in predictive engineering scenarios if they do not preserve input-output behavior or come with error guarantees. Balanced truncation offers system-theoretic guarantees, but analytical and data-driven versions can be expensive for complex systems with many inputs and outputs.

First-Principles Model

Balanced truncation identifies states that are both reachable from inputs and observable in outputs, then truncates states that contribute little to input-output behavior. Data-driven variants such as ERA build reduced models from impulse responses or Markov parameters. The resulting ROMs are tailored to control objectives and can have theoretical error bounds, but scaling requires tangential interpolation, output decomposition, and gappy POD-style approximations.

Key Claims

  • Balanced truncation originates from system-theoretic principles and is tailored for control objectives.
  • Data-driven balancing methods use empirical system Gramians and minimum realization theory.
  • Theoretical error bounds allow balanced ROMs to operate in truly predictive scenarios.
  • The eigensystem realization algorithm is a key data-driven balanced truncation method.
  • ERA can show predictive performance in stiff reactive flow and aero-acoustic systems.
  • Many input-output channels create computational overhead, motivating tangential interpolation, output decomposition, and low-fidelity gappy POD.

Important Terms

Balanced Truncation

A model reduction method that removes states with low combined reachability and observability.

System Gramian

A matrix measuring reachability or observability properties of a dynamical system.

Eigensystem Realization Algorithm

A data-driven method for constructing reduced state-space models from impulse-response data.

Markov Parameter

A sequence of impulse-response coefficients used in system realization.

Gappy POD

A method for reconstructing or approximating fields from partial observations using POD bases.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture adds a control-theoretic ROM perspective to the broader model reduction sequence.
  • It connects to predictive ROMs, inverse design, and digital twins because error bounds matter for deployment.
  • It also complements CUR and adaptive ROM talks by focusing on input-output balancing rather than only state compression.

Practical Caution

A ROM can be low-dimensional and still poor for prediction if it removes states important for outputs or control. Reduction should respect the input-output task, not just state variance.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that predictive ROMs should preserve what the system can be driven to and what can be observed, not merely what has high energy.

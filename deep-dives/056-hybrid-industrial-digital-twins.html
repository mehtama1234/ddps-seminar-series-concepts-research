Talk 56 · Siemens Technology

Dirk Hartmann: Machine Learning And Physics-Based Simulations For Industrial Digital Twins

This lecture frames industrial digital twins as hybrid systems where machine learning and physics-based simulation must be tightly integrated to achieve usable accuracy, speed, and automation.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Industrial assets, products, systems, and infrastructure need digital models that are accurate enough for decisions and fast enough for design, engineering, and operations. Pure high-fidelity simulation is often too slow and expert-intensive, while pure ML can be unreliable outside the data regime. Scaling digital twins also runs into the cost of manual expert modeling effort.

First-Principles Model

A digital twin is a computational representation tied to a real asset or process. In industry, it must answer operational questions under constraints: speed, accuracy, maintainability, and deployment cost. Physics-based models provide structure and extrapolation discipline; machine learning supplies fast surrogates, calibration, automation, and data adaptation. The useful object is the hybrid workflow, not either component alone.

Key Claims

  • Combining the digital and real world requires sufficiently accurate and fast digital models.
  • The required speed and fidelity will only be achieved by tightly integrating ML and physics-based methods.
  • Hybrid digital twins are needed across design, engineering, and operations of complex industrial systems.
  • Industrial deployment requires the right accuracy-speed tradeoff rather than maximum fidelity everywhere.
  • Manual expert effort is a major limiting factor for producing more digital twins.
  • Research needs should be judged from the full industrial lifecycle, not only academic benchmark performance.

Important Terms

Digital Twin

A computational representation of a real asset, product, system, or infrastructure used for analysis, prediction, or operation.

Hybrid Digital Twin

A digital twin that combines physics-based simulation with machine learning components.

Industrial Deployment

Using a method reliably in production workflows with constraints on cost, speed, maintenance, and accountability.

Accuracy-Speed Tradeoff

The practical balance between model fidelity and computational cost.

Expert-Effort Bottleneck

The limitation caused by requiring scarce domain experts to manually build, calibrate, or maintain models.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk turns the series' hybrid physics-ML theme into an industrial systems problem.
  • It connects to surrogate modeling, ROMs, active learning, and uncertainty because digital twins require all of them in deployment.
  • It also clarifies why interpretability and automation matter: they affect whether many twins can be built and maintained.

Practical Caution

An impressive demo twin is not automatically an industrial digital twin. Industrial use requires lifecycle integration, validation against real assets, update mechanisms, and clear responsibility when model predictions drive decisions.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that industrial SciML is constrained engineering. The winning model is the one that fits the decision workflow with enough physics, enough data, and enough automation.

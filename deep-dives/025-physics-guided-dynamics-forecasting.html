Talk 25 · UC San Diego

Rui Wang: Physics-Guided Deep Learning For Dynamics Forecasting

This lecture shows how adding physics knowledge directly into deep learning models improves spatiotemporal dynamics forecasting by building in numerical methods, constraints, and symmetry.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Dynamics forecasting is hard because physical fields evolve across different scales, conditions change between systems, and tiny errors compound over time. A purely data-driven model might fit large patterns while missing small eddies, incompressibility, or quantities that should stay constant.

First-Principles Model

A forecasting model maps recent states of a dynamical system to future states. Physics-guided learning modifies that map by adding physics bias into the neural network: numerical-method-inspired components, symmetry that stays correct under transformations, divergence or conservation losses, and multiscale decompositions that force the model to represent both large and small features.

Key Claims

  • Dynamics forecasting benefits from combining deep learning with prior physics knowledge.
  • TF-Net marries numerical techniques with custom neural-network design for physical fields.
  • For incompressible flow, divergence should be small and can be used as a physical measure or loss.
  • Symmetry and equivariance can improve generalization across transformations such as rotation, translation, uniform motion, or scaling.
  • Mean-squared-error training can overemphasize large scales and ignore small eddies.
  • Robust forecasting still needs better theory for nonstationary and chaotic dynamics when conditions differ from training data.

Important Terms

Dynamics Forecasting

Predicting future states of a time-evolving physical system from current or past states.

Physics-Guided Deep Learning

Deep learning that incorporates known physical constraints, symmetries, or numerical methods.

Equivariance

A property where transforming the input transforms the output in the corresponding physically meaningful way.

Divergence Loss

A penalty that encourages incompressible flow predictions to have near-zero divergence.

Distribution Shift

A mismatch between training and test conditions that often degrades forecasting performance.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects graph simulators, differentiable physics, and fluid reduced-order models around the issue of long-rollout physical consistency.
  • It complements PINNs by using physics to guide both the architecture and the losses for forecasting, rather than only fitting PDE equations.
  • It also reinforces the series-wide warning that generalization across different regimes is harder than interpolation within one regime.

Practical Caution

Do not judge a dynamics model only by pixelwise or fieldwise short-horizon error. Check physical diagnostics such as divergence, spectra, conserved quantities, and how well it performs when conditions differ from training.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that forecasting is not just extrapolating frames. The model must preserve the physical structure that makes future states plausible.

Talk 25 · UC San Diego

Rui Wang: Physics-Guided Deep Learning For Dynamics Forecasting

This lecture explains how physics-guided deep learning can improve spatiotemporal dynamics forecasting by building numerical structure, constraints, and symmetry into neural models.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Dynamics forecasting is hard because physical fields evolve across scales, distributions shift across systems, and small errors compound over time. A purely data-driven video-prediction model may fit large-scale patterns while missing small eddies, incompressibility, or conservation-related structure.

First-Principles Model

A forecasting model maps recent states of a dynamical system to future states. Physics-guided learning modifies that map by adding physical inductive bias: numerical-method-inspired components, equivariance to symmetry transformations, divergence or conservation losses, and multiscale decompositions that force the model to represent both large and small features.

Key Claims

  • Dynamics forecasting benefits from combining deep learning with prior physics knowledge.
  • TF-Net marries numerical techniques with custom neural-network design for physical fields.
  • For incompressible flow, divergence should be small and can be used as a physical metric or loss.
  • Symmetry and equivariance can improve generalization across transformations such as rotation, translation, uniform motion, or scaling.
  • Mean-squared-error training can overemphasize large scales and ignore small eddies.
  • Robust forecasting still needs better theory for nonstationary and chaotic dynamics under distribution shift.

Important Terms

Dynamics Forecasting

Predicting future states of a time-evolving physical system from current or past states.

Physics-Guided Deep Learning

Deep learning that incorporates known physical constraints, symmetries, or numerical structure.

Equivariance

A property where transforming the input transforms the output in the corresponding physically meaningful way.

Divergence Loss

A penalty that encourages incompressible flow predictions to have near-zero divergence.

Distribution Shift

A mismatch between training and test regimes that often degrades forecasting performance.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects graph simulators, differentiable physics, and fluid ROMs around the issue of long-rollout physical consistency.
  • It complements PINNs by using physics as architecture and loss guidance for forecasting rather than only PDE residual fitting.
  • It also reinforces the series-wide warning that generalization across regimes is harder than interpolation.

Practical Caution

Do not judge a dynamics model only by pixelwise or fieldwise short-horizon error. Check physical diagnostics such as divergence, spectra, conserved quantities, and performance under distribution shift.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that forecasting is not just extrapolating frames. The model must preserve the physical structure that makes future states plausible.

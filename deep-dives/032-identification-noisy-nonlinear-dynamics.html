Talk 32 · Max Planck Institute for Dynamics of Complex Technical Systems

Peter Benner: Identification Of Nonlinear Dynamical Systems From Noisy Measurements

This lecture studies how to identify nonlinear differential equations from noisy and sparse measurements by combining sparse identification (which picks the most active terms from a list of candidate equations) with numerical time-integration methods that step through time in steps rather than differentiating the data directly.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

System identification tries to recover dynamical equations from observed trajectories. For nonlinear systems, sparse observations and measurement noise create hard challenges: computing derivatives from noisy data gives unreliable results, the choice of candidate terms in the dictionary matters greatly, and spurious terms can be selected when noise is high.

First-Principles Model

A dynamical system defines time derivatives as a function of the current state. Sparse identification assumes that this function can be represented by only a few active terms selected from a candidate library. Instead of differentiating noisy data directly, numerical integration methods such as Runge-Kutta—which evolves the state through small time steps using weighted combinations of derivatives—can connect states across time and make the identification problem more stable.

Key Claims

  • Discovering dynamical models from measurements is essential for analysis, optimization, and control.
  • Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics popularized the idea of selecting only active terms from a large library of candidates.
  • Noise and sparse sampling remain central obstacles when identifying nonlinear systems.
  • Integrating the equations through time rather than differentiating the data directly can make identification more stable.
  • Choosing the candidate dictionary automatically remains an open problem.
  • For wave or transport equations, the time-stepping method should match the dynamics being identified.

Important Terms

System Identification

Inferring a mathematical dynamical model by fitting equations to observed input-output or trajectory data.

SINDy

Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics: a method that selects only the most important terms from a library of candidate nonlinear functions.

Dictionary

A candidate library of functions—such as powers, products, or sines—from which the actual terms of the dynamical system are selected.

Runge-Kutta Scheme

A time-integration method that evolves the state forward by computing weighted averages of derivatives at intermediate time points within each time step.

Noisy Measurements

Observed data corrupted by measurement error, which distorts derivative estimates and can mislead term selection.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk extends the Brunton interpretability lecture by focusing on the numerical-analysis details of equation discovery.
  • It connects to Koopman and Green's function talks because all three seek dynamical structure from data.
  • It also connects to control because an identified model is often needed before designing optimal or feedback-based strategies.

Practical Caution

Sparse discovery can return elegant equations that actually reflect noise patterns or limitations of the candidate dictionary rather than true underlying physics. The dictionary, integration method, and noise model are all assumptions built into the identification approach and must be chosen carefully.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that equation discovery requires more than just fitting numbers to data. It requires combining regression with time-evolution rules, noise treatment, and a good choice of candidate physics.

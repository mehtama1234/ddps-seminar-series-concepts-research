Talk 9 · ETH Zurich / Disney Research Studios

Byungsoo Kim: Data-Driven Methods For Fluid Simulations In Computer Graphics

This lecture explains how graphics-oriented fluid simulation uses data-driven methods to trade some physical exactness for speed, visual quality, controllability, and artist direction.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Computer graphics needs fluid simulations that look plausible, run fast enough for production workflows, and can be controlled by artists. Scientific accuracy is valuable, but the primary objective is often visual appearance and directability rather than strict numerical fidelity.

First-Principles Model

A physics-based graphics simulator generates motion for water, smoke, splashes, or related materials. Data-driven methods can replace expensive solver pieces, enhance coarse simulation detail, operate in latent space, stylize flow fields, or optimize controls through differentiable simulation. The governing constraint is not only physics; it is the production objective of plausible, controllable visual output.

Key Claims

  • Physics-based animation has matured, but production workflows still need efficient and controllable simulation tools.
  • Graphics can sometimes sacrifice exact accuracy for performance, controllability, and visual appearance.
  • Deep learning can accelerate particle or grid-based fluid solvers and pressure solves.
  • Latent-space fluid solvers use autoencoders and sequence models to integrate lower-dimensional states.
  • Data-driven methods can enhance visual detail through turbulence synthesis, splash augmentation, and super-resolution.
  • Differentiable physics gives a natural interface for optimizing controllers or initial conditions toward a target state.

Important Terms

Art-Directable Simulation

A simulation workflow that lets artists control visual outcomes while retaining physical plausibility.

Fluid Stylization

Applying style-transfer ideas to fluid volumes, such as smoke motifs or colorization.

Super-Resolution For Flow

Learning to add high-resolution, temporally coherent detail to coarse fluid simulations.

Latent-Space Fluid Solver

A solver that evolves compressed latent representations instead of full-resolution fields.

Differentiable Physics Control

Using gradients through a simulator to optimize motion controllers or initial states.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk clarifies that DDPS objectives vary by domain: graphics values controllability and appearance differently from engineering verification.
  • It connects to differentiable physics by showing a concrete creative-control use case for simulator gradients.
  • It also links to latent-space ROMs and super-resolution as practical examples of learned simulation acceleration.

Practical Caution

A graphics surrogate should not be judged by engineering accuracy standards alone, but the reverse is also true: a visually plausible flow model may be unacceptable for scientific prediction or safety-critical design.

Takeaway

The lecture's first-principles lesson is that the evaluation target defines the simulator: graphics DDPS optimizes plausible controllable appearance, while engineering DDPS optimizes predictive reliability.

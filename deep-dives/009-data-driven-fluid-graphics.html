Talk 9 · ETH Zurich / Disney Research Studios

Byungsoo Kim: Data-Driven Methods For Fluid Simulations In Computer Graphics

This lecture explains how computer graphics simulates fluids using data-driven methods to speed up calculation while keeping the look convincing, giving artists control, and letting animators direct the result.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Computer graphics needs fluid simulations that look convincing, run fast enough to use in production, and respond to artist control. Real-world accuracy matters, but the main goal is usually how the motion looks and whether artists can shape it, not whether every number exactly matches physics equations.

First-Principles Model

A physics-based graphics simulator creates motion for water, smoke, splashes, or similar materials. Data-driven methods—trained models learned from example data—can replace slow parts of the solver, add visual detail to coarse simulations, work in compressed space, stylize flow patterns, or optimize control settings through differentiable simulation (where you can take gradients of the simulator to guide optimization). The key constraint is not only matching physics; it is creating visually convincing, controllable output for production.

Key Claims

  • Physics-based animation has developed, but production work still needs fast and controllable simulation tools.
  • Graphics can accept some loss of exact accuracy in exchange for speed, control, and visual quality.
  • Neural networks can speed up particle or grid solvers and the pressure calculation step.
  • Latent-space fluid solvers use autoencoders and sequence models to evolve smaller, compressed representations instead of full-resolution fields.
  • Data-driven methods can enhance visual detail through turbulence synthesis, splash generation, and super-resolution upscaling.
  • Differentiable physics provides a way to optimize controls or starting states by taking gradients through the simulator toward a target result.

Important Terms

Art-Directable Simulation

A simulation that lets artists control what the motion looks like while still staying physically plausible.

Fluid Stylization

Applying artistic style ideas to fluid volumes, such as artistic smoke patterns or color changes.

Super-Resolution For Flow

Training a neural network to add sharp, smooth high-resolution detail to low-resolution fluid simulations.

Latent-Space Fluid Solver

A solver that evolves compressed representations of the flow instead of the full-size field.

Differentiable Physics Control

Using gradients computed through a simulator to optimize motion controllers or initial starting states.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk shows that data-driven simulation goals differ by field: graphics values control and appearance in ways engineering verification does not.
  • It connects to differentiable physics by showing a concrete use case where simulator gradients enable creative control.
  • It also links to compressed-space approximations and super-resolution as practical examples of trained simulation speedup.

Practical Caution

A graphics shortcut should not be measured by engineering or scientific accuracy standards alone, but the opposite is also true: a visually convincing flow model may fail for scientific prediction or safety-critical design.

Takeaway

The lecture's first-principles lesson is that what you measure determines the simulator: graphics data-driven simulators optimize for convincing controllable appearance, while engineering data-driven simulators optimize for accurate prediction.

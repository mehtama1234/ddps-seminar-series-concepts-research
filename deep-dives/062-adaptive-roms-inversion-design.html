Talk 62 · Virginia Tech

Eric de Sturler: Cheap And Robust Adaptive Reduced Order Models For Nonlinear Inversion And Design

This lecture explains how randomization and adaptive reduced-order models can reduce the cost of nonlinear inverse and PDE-constrained optimization problems while monitoring surrogate accuracy.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Nonlinear inverse problems and design optimization often require repeated large linear, nonlinear, and adjoint solves. Parametric ROMs can reduce cost, but building and evaluating them can still be expensive, and they often lack cheap error bounds during optimization.

First-Principles Model

Optimization with a surrogate is safe only if the optimizer knows when the surrogate is reliable. Randomization can cheaply construct reduced bases or estimate errors and objective improvement gaps. Adaptive ROMs update the reduced model when monitoring indicates it is no longer accurate enough, and trust-region logic limits steps according to surrogate reliability.

Key Claims

  • Nonlinear inverse and PDE-constrained optimization problems require many repeated solves.
  • Newton-type methods also require adjoint or transpose-system solves.
  • Parametric model reduction and surrogate models can substantially reduce computation.
  • Online computation of reduced models can itself be expensive for these problems.
  • Parametric ROMs often lack cheap error bounds.
  • Randomization can help compute reduced models cheaply and monitor accuracy or objective improvement for trust-region methods.

Important Terms

Nonlinear Inverse Problem

A problem of estimating unknown parameters or structures from observations when the forward map is nonlinear.

PDE-Constrained Optimization

Optimization where the variables must satisfy a PDE model.

Parametric Reduced-Order Model

A reduced model designed to approximate a family of full models across parameter values.

Randomization

Using randomized numerical methods to reduce cost, estimate error, or sample informative subspaces.

Trust-Region Method

An optimization method that restricts steps to regions where the model is believed to be reliable.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects ROMs to inverse design and optimization rather than only forward simulation.
  • It also links to uncertainty and error monitoring because surrogate use requires reliability estimates.
  • It complements industrial SciML by asking how reduced models can be made cheap and robust enough for repeated design loops.

Practical Caution

A reduced model can accelerate optimization while steering it in the wrong direction if its error is not monitored. Trust-region or randomized error checks are part of the algorithm, not optional diagnostics.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that optimization needs trustworthy cheap models, not just cheap models. The surrogate must know when it is safe to guide the next step.

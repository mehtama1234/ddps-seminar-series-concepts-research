Talk 99 · University of California, San Francisco

Jan Christoph: Predicting Heart Rhythm Disorders from Spatio-Temporal Imaging Data Using AI

This lecture explains how AI can infer hidden electrical waves in the heart from observable images of tissue motion.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Heart rhythm disorders come from abnormal electrical waves moving through cardiac tissue. Current imaging cannot easily resolve the full three-dimensional pattern of electrical activity inside the heart wall. Doctors and researchers need a way to infer dangerous hidden electrical patterns from measurements that are easier to obtain, like motion or deformation images.

First-Principles Model

Cardiac cells are electrically excitable nonlinear oscillators (cells that generate electrical pulses and respond by changing state). Their coordinated electrical pulses create reaction-diffusion waves (patterns that spread through tissue via local reactions and diffusion), and these electrical waves trigger mechanical contraction and deformation. If electrical activity leaves a measurable mechanical signature, then a model can learn the inverse map (work backward) from high-speed fluorescence or ultrasound imaging of deformation back to electrical dynamics. Ex vivo isolated-heart experiments (hearts removed from the body and kept functioning in controlled conditions) provide paired training data where both the hidden electrical state and visible mechanical motion can be measured.

Key Claims

  • Heart rhythm disorders can be understood as abnormal nonlinear wave activity in cardiac tissue.
  • Electrical excitation and mechanical deformation are coupled, so motion images can contain information about electrical activity.
  • AI models can learn to predict electrical dynamics from spatiotemporal imaging data.
  • High-speed fluorescence and ultrasound imaging provide complementary data sources.
  • Ex vivo isolated-heart training enables supervised learning with paired electrical and mechanical measurements.
  • The goal is to better predict and understand the mechanisms of arrhythmia.

Important Terms

Electrophysiology

The study of electrical activity in biological tissue, specifically in the heart.

Reaction-Diffusion System

A system where local chemical or electrical reactions and spatial spreading combine to create propagating waves or patterns.

Spatiotemporal Imaging

Imaging that captures both spatial structure and how it changes over time.

Ultrasound Imaging

A medical imaging technique that uses sound waves to observe tissue motion and structure.

Ex Vivo Training

Collection of training data using organs or tissue outside the living body in controlled experimental conditions.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects physics-informed learning, inverse problems, and biomedical imaging.
  • It exemplifies a broader DDPS theme: inferring hidden states from observable proxy measurements.
  • It links nonlinear waves and reaction-diffusion dynamics to real clinical prediction problems.

Practical Caution

A model trained ex vivo may not transfer directly to in vivo clinical settings. Differences in tissue conditions, imaging quality, patient variability, and medical interventions must be tested and validated.

Takeaway

When two physical processes connect (like electrical waves and mechanical motion), an invisible state can become inferable from a visible proxy signal.

Talk 55 · Georgia Institute of Technology

Molei Tao: Trustworthy Learning Of Mechanical Systems And Stiefel Optimization

This lecture shows how embedding geometric structure such as symplecticity (a way energy stays constant when it should) into learned mechanical dynamics improves predictions far into the future, and connects dynamics ideas to optimization on the Stiefel manifold (the mathematical space of orthonormal frames).

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Mechanical systems preserve geometric structures: energy stays constant when it should, momentum follows conservation laws, and the flow lives on a special kind of mathematical surface called symplectic. Standard neural networks can fit short trajectories while breaking these rules, causing predictions to drift farther and farther from truth as you extend them into the future. Optimization on constrained spaces such as the Stiefel manifold faces a similar problem: algorithms should respect the curved geometry they're moving on instead of pretending the space is flat.

First-Principles Model

A mechanical system is not just a list of states; it is a flow with preserved geometric structure. Symplectic learning embeds this preservation directly into the model so the learned time evolution stays correct over long horizons. For Stiefel optimization, the variable sits on the manifold of orthonormal frames (matrices with columns that are perpendicular unit vectors), so gradient steps and momentum acceleration must be designed to stay on the manifold rather than treating it like unconstrained flat space.

Key Claims

  • Physics embedded directly into deep learning models can improve trustworthy prediction of mechanical dynamics.
  • Mechanical systems preserve structures such as energy, momentum, and symplecticity.
  • Symplecticity is an underlying geometric property of Hamiltonian dynamics.
  • Embedding symplectic structure can provably improve how far into the future you can predict accurately.
  • The connection between dynamics and machine learning also informs optimization algorithms.
  • Momentum-accelerated gradient descent can be built on curved mathematical spaces, with the Stiefel manifold as a key example.

Important Terms

Symplecticity

A geometric structure where flow preserves area and energy-like quantities; a property of Hamiltonian mechanical dynamics.

Hamiltonian Dynamics

A formulation of mechanics based on position, momentum, and an energy-like function called the Hamiltonian.

Long-Time Prediction

Forecasting a dynamical system over many time steps where small structural errors compound and grow.

Stiefel Manifold

The set of matrices whose columns are perpendicular unit vectors; the mathematical space where constrained problems sit.

Riemannian Optimization

Optimization methods designed to move on curved mathematical surfaces instead of flat spaces.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects to the structure-preserving thread across physics-informed neural networks, neural ODE solvers, and scientific machine learning.
  • It complements talks on uncertainty and trustworthiness by showing one path to safer extrapolation: preserve geometry and conservation laws.
  • It also links dynamics and optimization, a pattern that recurs in neural PDE solvers and learning on curved spaces.

Practical Caution

Embedding structure helps only when the assumed structure matches the system. If you force symplecticity on systems that lose energy through friction or get pushed by external forces, the model will preserve the wrong geometry and predictions will fail.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that trustworthy dynamics learning starts by respecting the geometry of the system. Accurate prediction over time follows from preserving the right conservation laws and symmetries.

Talk 13 · University of Muenster

Mario Ohlberger: Model Reduction With Adaptive Enrichment For Large Scale PDE-Constrained Optimization

This lecture explains adaptive enrichment as a way to use reduced models inside large-scale PDE-constrained optimization without paying the full offline cost upfront.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Classical reduced-basis workflows rely on an expensive offline stage that builds a rich reduced space before online queries become cheap. In large-scale PDE-constrained optimization, uncertainty quantification, battery degradation, and multiscale settings, the offline solves themselves can be prohibitive, and a fixed reduced space may not be accurate where the optimizer actually travels.

First-Principles Model

A PDE-constrained optimization problem repeatedly solves a PDE while changing design or control variables. A reduced basis can accelerate those solves, but the optimizer must know when the reduced model is trustworthy. Adaptive enrichment starts with a smaller model and enriches it during the optimization process, often inside a trust-region framework that limits reduced-model use to regions where error is controlled.

Key Claims

  • Adaptive enrichment goes beyond the classical offline-online splitting of reduced-basis methods.
  • The target setting is large-scale PDE-constrained optimization and related uncertainty quantification problems.
  • Classical offline construction can require very large-scale solves and become prohibitive.
  • Reduced-basis methods need efficient a posteriori error estimators to identify where the current basis is weak.
  • Trust-region ideas can control when an approximate reduced model is reliable enough for optimization steps.
  • For hyperbolic, shock-forming, or slow Kolmogorov-width problems, linear reduced spaces may need nonlinear extensions.

Important Terms

PDE-Constrained Optimization

An optimization problem whose feasible states are governed by a partial differential equation.

Adaptive Enrichment

Growing the reduced basis during the solve or optimization process when the current reduced model is insufficient.

Reduced Basis

A low-dimensional space built from representative full-order solutions to accelerate later PDE solves.

A Posteriori Error Estimator

A computable estimate of the reduced model error after a solution has been produced.

Trust Region

An optimization region where the approximate model is judged reliable enough to guide a step.

Connections Across The Series

  • This extends the ROM discussion from prediction to optimization loops, where repeated solves and reliability guarantees matter.
  • It connects to inverse problems and control because optimization requires deciding when a cheap model can replace an expensive PDE solve.
  • It also foreshadows nonlinear ROM talks by noting that linear reduced spaces cannot beat slow Kolmogorov-width decay.

Practical Caution

Do not build a giant offline reduced basis by default. If the optimizer only visits a small portion of parameter space, adaptive enrichment can spend expensive full-order solves where they are actually needed.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that ROMs in optimization must be managed dynamically: use the cheap model when valid, enrich it when needed, and let error control govern the loop.

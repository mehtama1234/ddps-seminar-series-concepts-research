Talk 84 · Vanderbilt University

Thomas Beckers: Physics-Enhanced Gaussian Processes For Electromechanical Systems

This lecture presents physics-enhanced Gaussian processes, including GP Port-Hamiltonian systems, to learn electromechanical dynamics with uncertainty while remaining physically correct by design.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Data-driven models for nonlinear electromechanical systems can be data-hungry and physically inconsistent if they ignore known principles such as energy structure. Penalty-based physics constraints can help but may still permit unphysical models or require significant tuning.

First-Principles Model

Gaussian processes provide distributions over functions and uncertainty estimates. Port-Hamiltonian systems encode energy exchange, dissipation, and interconnection structure. GP-PHS combines them by forming a Bayesian distribution over physically structured dynamical systems rather than a single black-box function. Physics-enhanced VAEs extend the idea by putting a GP prior with Green's-function structure on latent dynamics.

Key Claims

  • Data-driven electromechanical models should incorporate basic physical principles for data efficiency and correctness.
  • Physics-enhanced Gaussian processes provide nonparametric Bayesian learning with uncertainty quantification.
  • Gaussian Process Port-Hamiltonian systems are physically correct by design rather than by penalty.
  • GP-PHS uses data to form a distribution over possible Port-Hamiltonian systems.
  • Physics-enhanced VAEs use physically enhanced GP priors on latent dynamics.
  • Green's functions can encode linear dynamical prior knowledge in GP kernels.

Important Terms

Gaussian Process

A Bayesian nonparametric model that defines a distribution over functions.

Port-Hamiltonian System

A dynamics framework that represents energy storage, dissipation, and interconnection.

Physically Correct By Design

A model architecture that structurally enforces physics rather than adding physics as a soft penalty.

Green's Function

A function representing the response of a linear system to an impulse, used to encode physics in kernels.

Latent Dynamics

Dynamics occurring in a learned hidden representation, such as the latent space of a VAE.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects uncertainty-aware learning with structure-preserving dynamics.
  • It complements symplectic and Hamiltonian learning talks by adding Bayesian uncertainty.
  • It also links to physics-informed kernels and Green's-function perspectives elsewhere in the series.

Practical Caution

A physically structured prior is only appropriate if the real system follows that structure. Model mismatch can lead to confident but systematically biased predictions.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that Bayesian uncertainty and physical structure can be combined in the model class itself, not merely patched on after training.

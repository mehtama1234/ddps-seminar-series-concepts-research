Talk 48 · Princeton University / Johns Hopkins University

Yannis Kevrekidis: No Equations, No Variables, No Parameters, No Space And No Time

This lecture argues that modern data-driven modeling can sometimes bypass explicit equations, variables, parameters, space, and time by discovering useful coarse observables and computational representations directly from observations.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Classical modeling moves from observations to variables, equations, parameters, and analysis. Complex systems often resist this route because the right macroscopic variables are unknown, the microscopic simulator may be available without closed equations, and the effective geometry or time scale may be hidden inside high-dimensional data.

First-Principles Model

The first-principles move is to separate the scientific task from the traditional symbolic container. If the goal is prediction, continuation, bifurcation analysis, or control, one can sometimes build the required computational operators directly from data or short bursts of simulation. Manifold learning, diffusion maps, autoencoders, recurrent nets, neural ODEs, and operator learning can identify low-dimensional coordinates, coarse observables, or evolution rules without first writing a closed-form macroscopic equation.

Key Claims

  • Many modern neural architectures have older roots in nonlinear dynamical-system identification.
  • Equation-free modeling keeps the analysis goal but replaces explicit macroscopic equations with computational experiments on data or microscopic simulators.
  • The right variables may be learned from data rather than chosen by intuition alone.
  • Manifold learning can reveal low-dimensional curved structure inside high-dimensional observations.
  • Diffusion maps are one way to learn coordinates on data manifolds that serve as macro observables.
  • The provocative phrase no equations, variables, parameters, space, or time points to learning the representation needed for the task rather than assuming it in advance.

Important Terms

Equation-Free Modeling

A computational framework that performs macroscopic analysis using data or microscopic simulators without closed-form macroscopic equations.

Macro Observable

A reduced variable that summarizes the large-scale state of a complex system.

Manifold Learning

Methods that discover low-dimensional geometric structure inside high-dimensional data.

Diffusion Maps

A manifold-learning method that uses diffusion geometry on data to construct useful low-dimensional coordinates.

Coarse Time-Stepper

A computational operator that advances coarse variables by lifting them to microscopic states, simulating briefly, and restricting back.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk links directly to Petros Koumoutsakos' effective dynamics lecture and later data-driven dynamics talks.
  • It also connects to ROMs and neural operators because all search for lower-dimensional or operator-level descriptions.
  • It reframes scientific ML as representation discovery: first learn what variables or coordinates make the problem solvable.

Practical Caution

Learning coordinates from data is powerful only if the data cover the relevant manifold. A beautiful low-dimensional embedding can fail under new regimes, rare transitions, or unobserved parameters.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that equations are one representation of dynamics, not the only one. The deeper goal is to find the coordinates and operators that make prediction and analysis possible.

Talk 116 · Caltech

Akhil Nekkanti: Data-Driven Techniques for Analysis of Turbulent Flows

This lecture uses data-driven modal analysis, especially SPOD, to rebuild, clean up noise from, and make sense of turbulent flow data.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Turbulent flows are high-dimensional (many variables to track), nonlinear (output is not simply proportional to input), and unstable (small changes grow fast), yet they contain organized patterns that control energy and motion. Measurements and simulations are often noisy or have gaps, so analysis tools must pull meaningful patterns out of broken data.

First-Principles Model

Spectral proper orthogonal decomposition is a frequency-domain (organized by speed of oscillation) version of PCA (principal component analysis, a method that finds the main directions of variation in data) tailored to flow data. It breaks turbulent fields into organized structures sorted by energy and frequency. Extending SPOD to low-rank rebuilding, noise cleaning, and frequency-time analysis allows missing regions to be estimated from patterns in space and time, and brief-lived structures to be tracked.

Key Claims

  • Turbulent flows are hard because they have many variables to track, they are unstable, and they are nonlinear.
  • Data-driven techniques reduce complexity by extracting major flow patterns.
  • SPOD is a frequency-domain modal tool well-suited for turbulent-flow analysis.
  • Low-rank rebuilding can recover data with gaps or damage.
  • Frequency-time analysis helps study when organized structures appear and fade.
  • Organized structures matter for energy and motion even though turbulence looks chaotic.

Important Terms

Turbulence

Chaotic, multi-scale fluid motion where instability and nonlinear effects create disorder.

SPOD

Spectral Proper Orthogonal Decomposition: a method to break flow data into pieces organized by oscillation frequency.

Coherent Structure

An organized region of flow that carries meaningful energy or motion within the turbulence.

Gappy Reconstruction

Filling in missing or damaged parts of data using the patterns from the parts you can see.

Low-Rank Reconstruction

Approximating data using just a handful of the strongest patterns instead of all the pieces.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk brings together modal analysis, reduced-order models (simplified versions that capture main behavior), turbulent-flow diagnosis, and data repair.
  • It builds on POD/DMD/Koopman lectures by focusing on frequency-domain and brief-lived structure.
  • It also touches sensor placement and experiment design because missing data can be filled in using patterns discovered in the full data.

Practical Caution

Low-rank turbulent-flow rebuilds can remove noise but may also erase rare yet important dynamics. Rebuild quality should be checked against real physical events, not just average error across all values.

Takeaway

The foundational lesson is that turbulence analysis becomes doable by pulling out organized low-rank structure while keeping track of brief-lived anomalies.

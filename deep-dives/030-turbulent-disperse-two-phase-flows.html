Talk 30 · University of Michigan

Jesse Capecelatro: Turbulent Disperse Two-Phase Flows

This lecture studies turbulent gas-particle flows where particles strongly modify carrier turbulence and closure modeling must represent two-way multiphase coupling.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Dispersed two-phase flows are common in energy and environmental systems, but modeling them is difficult because particles interact with turbulence, wakes, collisions, concentration, and wall effects. Single-phase turbulence closures do not directly capture dense and dilute multiphase behavior.

First-Principles Model

A gas-particle flow has a carrier fluid and a dispersed particle phase. Particles feel drag and other forces, but they also feed back on the fluid through wakes and momentum exchange. Reynolds-averaged or filtered equations therefore require closures for unresolved correlations and interphase coupling terms. Data-driven modeling can target those closures while preserving physical statistics.

Key Claims

  • Particles can have order-one effects on background turbulence.
  • Multiphase RANS closures have often been extensions of single-phase turbulence models.
  • Those extensions can fail across dilute and dense regimes where two-way coupling matters.
  • Data-driven modeling has grown for closure modeling, uncertainty quantification, and model augmentation.
  • The talk explores alternatives to standard neural-network closures.
  • Stochastic force treatments can help preserve particle velocity variance and dispersion statistics.

Important Terms

Dispersed Two-Phase Flow

A flow where droplets or particles are suspended in a carrier fluid.

Two-Way Coupling

Interaction where the fluid affects particles and particles significantly affect the fluid.

Multiphase RANS

Reynolds-averaged modeling extended to coupled fluid and particle phases.

Closure Modeling

Approximating unresolved terms introduced by averaging or filtering equations.

Stochastic Drag/Force Model

A model that treats particle forces probabilistically to match important flow statistics.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk extends the closure theme from LES-ROMs and radiative transfer to multiphase turbulence.
  • It connects to data-driven model augmentation because the learned part targets specific closure terms rather than replacing all physics.
  • It also adds a concrete example where missing phase coupling can dominate prediction error.

Practical Caution

A closure calibrated for single-phase turbulence or one concentration regime may fail when particles strongly alter the carrier flow. Validate across dilute/dense regimes and particle statistics, not just mean flow fields.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that multiphase turbulence closure is about coupling statistics: particles are not passive decoration; they reshape the turbulent flow.

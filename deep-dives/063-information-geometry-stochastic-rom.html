Talk 63 · University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

Zorin Mitran: Data-Driven Information Geometry For Stochastic Model Reduction

This lecture extends reduced-order modeling from flat Euclidean subspaces to curved statistical manifolds, using data-driven information geometry for stochastic systems.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Standard ROMs often project data onto linear subspaces using Euclidean least squares. For stochastic phenomena, the objects of interest are probability distributions, and their natural geometry may be curved rather than flat. In non-equilibrated systems, a clean parametric distribution family may not even be known.

First-Principles Model

Information geometry treats families of probability distributions as manifolds with metrics and geodesics. Instead of projecting vectors onto a linear subspace, stochastic model reduction projects or transports distributions along curved statistical manifolds. When no parametric family is given, local normal distributions can be constructed from data using SVD, and their envelope forms a data-driven statistical manifold.

Key Claims

  • Traditional ROMs project onto subspaces and minimize Euclidean distance in flat linear spaces.
  • Stochastic phenomena can be modeled on manifolds of probability distributions.
  • Information geometry studies statistical manifolds of parametrized probability distributions.
  • For non-equilibrated systems, an explicit parametrized distribution may be unavailable.
  • A data-driven statistical manifold can be built from envelopes of local normal distributions obtained from SVD of data.
  • Reduced models are obtained by geodesic transport, where geodesics are curved rather than straight Euclidean lines.

Important Terms

Information Geometry

The study of probability distributions as geometric objects on statistical manifolds.

Statistical Manifold

A manifold whose points represent probability distributions.

Geodesic

The shortest or natural path between points on a curved manifold, generalizing straight lines.

Local Normal Distribution

A local Gaussian approximation built around data neighborhoods.

Stochastic Model Reduction

Reducing the complexity of models whose states or outputs are probabilistic.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture broadens the ROM theme from deterministic state reduction to probabilistic distribution reduction.
  • It connects to uncertainty quantification because model states are treated as distributions rather than point estimates.
  • It also links to manifold-learning talks: the reduced object may live on a curved learned geometry.

Practical Caution

A Euclidean projection can distort probabilistic structure. For stochastic systems, the choice of geometry affects the reduced model, so the manifold construction must match the distributional behavior in the data.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that stochastic reduction is geometry-sensitive. If the objects are distributions, the reduced space should respect distribution geometry.

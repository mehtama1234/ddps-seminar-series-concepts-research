Talk 54 · Stanford University

Tailin Wu: Learning To Accelerate Large-Scale Physical Simulations

This lecture presents learned simulators for large-scale fluid and plasma systems, using graph networks, U-Nets, and multi-step objectives to improve long-term rollout and multiscale behavior.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Large-scale fluid and plasma simulations can involve millions of cells and expensive long-time evolution. Deep surrogate models promise speedups, but they must handle heterogeneous geometry, local and global dynamics, multiscale coupling, and error accumulation over rollouts.

First-Principles Model

A physical simulator updates a field state over time. A learned simulator approximates that update map. For subsurface flow, local water-oil dynamics can be represented by graph neural networks over mesh cells, while pressure has more global structure suited to U-Net-like field models. Training on multi-step losses exposes the model to its own rollout errors, improving long-term stability compared with one-step imitation.

Key Claims

  • Classical numerical solvers for fluids and plasmas can require massive computational resources.
  • Deep surrogate modeling can complement or accelerate simulations but faces long-term evolution and multiscale dynamics challenges.
  • Hybrid graph network simulators combine graph networks for local dynamics with 3D U-Nets for global pressure dynamics.
  • Graph neural networks are useful for irregular or mesh-based local interactions.
  • Multi-step training objectives improve long-term rollout accuracy compared with one-step losses.
  • The method targets very large systems, scaling to millions of cells in the subsurface example.

Important Terms

Learned Simulator

A machine learning model trained to approximate the time-update behavior of a physical simulator.

Graph Neural Network

A neural architecture that propagates information along graph edges, useful for mesh or particle systems.

U-Net

An encoder-decoder convolutional architecture often used for field-to-field prediction.

Multi-Step Loss

A training objective that penalizes errors over several rollout steps rather than only the next step.

Long-Term Rollout

Repeatedly applying a learned update model to predict many future time steps.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture continues the surrogate-acceleration thread with a concrete graph-based large-scale simulator.
  • It connects to Petros Koumoutsakos' hybrid-alloy framing by combining architectures according to physical structure.
  • It also connects to neural operators and ROMs because the learned model approximates an evolution operator.

Practical Caution

A one-step accurate learned simulator may drift badly when rolled out. Long-term stability must be tested directly, especially when the surrogate is used for control or inverse design.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that learned simulation is repeated operator application. Stability over rollout is the real test, not next-step prediction alone.

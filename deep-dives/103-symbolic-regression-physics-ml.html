Talk 103 · University of Connecticut

George M. Bollas: Physics-Informed Machine Learning through Symbolic Regression

This lecture shows how symbolic regression can recover interpretable physics models from scarce and noisy data.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Many engineering systems need equations, not just predictions, especially when data are scarce, noisy, or needed for diagnosis and prognosis. PINN-style methods can fit PDE constraints, but they may be costly and may not directly reveal the governing model.

First-Principles Model

Symbolic regression searches over mathematical expressions to identify compact equations that explain observed data. When combined with automatic differentiation, it can estimate derivatives from time-varying data and search for PDE terms without repeatedly integrating candidate PDEs. Genetic programming can also augment an existing dynamic model with additional symbolic equations that expose how a fault evolves over time.

Key Claims

  • Symbolic regression is useful when the goal is model discovery rather than black-box prediction.
  • The approach is especially attractive under scarce data.
  • The presented framework is tested under high noise, including up to 50 percent noise.
  • Automatic differentiation can remove the need for repeated PDE integration during discovery.
  • Comparisons with PINNs highlight different tradeoffs between equation discovery and constrained neural fitting.
  • Fault detection and prognosis benefit from symbolic expressions that describe fault progression analytically.

Important Terms

Symbolic Regression

A search procedure that discovers mathematical expressions fitting data.

Genetic Programming

An evolutionary search method often used to evolve symbolic expressions or programs.

Automatic Differentiation

A computational method for exactly differentiating programs or neural representations through the chain rule.

PDE Discovery

The task of identifying governing partial differential equations from observed data.

Fault Prognosis

Predicting how a system fault will progress over time.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects sparse model discovery, PINNs, symbolic methods, and interpretable scientific ML.
  • It complements the broader physics-informed learning thread by emphasizing closed-form equation recovery.
  • It also links to industrial digital twins, where discovered equations can support diagnosis and prognosis.

Practical Caution

Symbolic regression can discover elegant but spurious equations if the candidate library, derivative estimates, or noise handling are poor. Interpretability does not remove the need for validation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that when decisions require mechanisms, learning should try to recover equations and not only approximate outputs.

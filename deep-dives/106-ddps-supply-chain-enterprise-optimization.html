Talk 106 · University of Connecticut

Burcu Beykal: A DDPS Engineering Approach for Supply Chain Management and Enterprise-Wide Optimization

This lecture applies data-driven process systems engineering to supply-chain and enterprise-wide optimization problems where planning and scheduling decisions depend on each other.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Modern industrial enterprises coordinate many interconnected production, planning, scheduling, and supply-chain decisions. Optimizing each layer separately can produce infeasible or brittle operations because lower-level scheduling constraints feed back into higher-level planning decisions.

First-Principles Model

Supply-chain management can be formulated as a hierarchical optimization problem. The planning layer acts like a leader choosing strategic or aggregate decisions, while scheduling layers act as followers imposing feasibility constraints from detailed operations. Bi-level programming (an optimization structure with a leader choosing first, then a follower reacting) captures this leader-follower structure, but large numbers of integer variables make the resulting mathematical programs difficult to solve. A data-driven process systems engineering approach combines data-driven modeling, process-systems structure, and optimization algorithms to make these interdependent decisions tractable.

Key Claims

  • Supply-chain and enterprise systems are multi-dimensional, multi-purpose, and multi-product systems.
  • Reliable coordination across planning and scheduling layers is essential for efficiency, resiliency, productivity, and profitability.
  • Bi-level programming is well suited because scheduling problems can constrain planning decisions.
  • Integer-heavy scheduling subproblems create major algorithmic challenges.
  • A process systems engineering perspective provides structure for otherwise burdensome enterprise optimization.
  • Data-driven methods can support practical optimization across interconnected industrial activities.

Important Terms

Enterprise-Wide Optimization

Optimization across multiple connected layers of an industrial enterprise rather than a single unit operation.

Supply Chain Management

Coordinating material, production, inventory, logistics, and market-facing decisions.

Bi-Level Programming

An optimization structure with a leader problem and a follower problem whose decisions affect each other.

Mixed-Integer Optimization

Optimization involving both continuous variables (that can be any real number) and integer variables (that must be whole numbers).

Process Systems Engineering

The field combining modeling, optimization, control, and systems thinking for chemical and industrial processes.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk extends data-driven process systems engineering beyond PDEs into operational decision systems.
  • It connects data-driven modeling with constrained optimization and control.
  • It also parallels agentic planning: local feasible actions must be coordinated with global objectives.

Practical Caution

A data-driven optimizer can recommend infeasible decisions if the lower-level constraints are simplified too aggressively. Enterprise optimization needs feasibility checks at the operational layer.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that enterprise optimization is a coupled hierarchy: decisions are only good if they remain feasible across all planning and scheduling layers.

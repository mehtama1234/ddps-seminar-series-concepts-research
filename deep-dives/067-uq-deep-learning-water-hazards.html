Talk 67 · University of Manchester

Ajay Harish: Uncertainty Quantification And Deep Learning For Water-Hazard Prediction

This lecture shows how to use uncertainty quantification and deep learning to predict storm surge and water hazards, where water flow, floating debris, building structure, and infrastructure unknowns all shape how much damage occurs.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Storm surges, tsunamis, and coastal flooding exert severe forces on urban buildings and roads. The damage depends on more than just water height; it also depends on the uncertain starting conditions, how waves and surge interact, floating debris, sediment movement, material properties of structures, and how buildings respond under load. To make sound decisions about protection, we must measure these uncertainties and their effects.

First-Principles Model

A water-hazard model takes uncertain inputs (surge start, structure properties) and computes uncertain damage outputs. Bayesian calibration (updating uncertain model numbers using measured data) estimates parameters from observations; neural networks and surrogate models (fast replacements for slow simulations) speed up repeated simulations; uncertainty propagation (tracking how input uncertainty flows through the model) produces a range of possible damage values, not just one prediction. The goal is to forecast not just a single impact, but a distribution—the full range of possible damage—so decision-makers know the true risk.

Key Claims

  • Storm surge is the rise in sea level above normal tides, caused by wind and pressure from a storm system.
  • Surge combined with waves and high tides can greatly raise average water levels and flood coastal cities.
  • Modeling urban infrastructure damage is made harder by uncertainty in both water flow and building behavior.
  • Major uncertainty sources include starting conditions, floating debris, sediment movement, and how solid materials behave under stress.
  • Bayesian calibration and neural networks can help measure and understand extreme-event impacts quantitatively.
  • Water-hazard prediction requires damage estimation, not just water-flow simulation.

Important Terms

Storm Surge

An abnormal rise in sea level caused by storm winds and low air pressure, above regular tide levels.

Water Hazard

A damaging water-driven event such as storm surge, tsunami, coastal flood, or wave impact.

Damage Assessment

Estimating the likely physical or economic harm caused by a hazard event.

Bayesian Calibration

Updating uncertain model parameters using measured data and prior assumptions.

Urban Infrastructure Risk

The exposure of buildings, roads, utilities, and coastal assets to harm from hazard events.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture is an applied uncertainty-quantification case study that builds on uncertainty propagation and structural health monitoring talks.
  • It connects to digital twins because making good infrastructure decisions needs fast calibrated hazard models that run in real time.
  • It also links to surrogate modeling: extreme-event uncertainty quantification requires many model simulations under different conditions.

Practical Caution

A single-value flood map can hide the true risk because it ignores uncertainty. Real hazard decisions must account for unknown starting conditions, random debris, structure variability, and errors in model calibration itself.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that hazard prediction is uncertainty propagation feeding into decisions. Deep learning helps when it lets us measure and communicate risk faster and more accurately.

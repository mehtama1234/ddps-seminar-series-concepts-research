Talk 79 · Texas A&M University

Eduardo Gildin: Guided Deep Learning Manifold Linearization Of Porous Media Flow

This lecture presents physics-aware manifold linearization for porous-media flow, using non-intrusive reduction, autoencoders, and Koopman-style lifting to speed up reservoir digital twins (fast computational copies used for monitoring and control).

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Reservoir simulators are expensive because nonlinear porous-media equations require iterative solves at each time step. Running fast closed-loop simulations, optimizations, and control systems in real time needs a quicker proxy—but full access to the simulator's internal equations is often unavailable or impractical.

First-Principles Model

The core idea is to learn a transformed space where nonlinear reservoir dynamics behave more like linear systems. E2CO (Embed to Control and Observe) combines data-driven model reduction and neural networks to copy both the time evolution of the state and how the system responds to inputs, without needing simulator internals. A second path augments the state space and uses an autoencoder (a neural network that compresses and decompresses data) to map reservoir equations to a lower-dimensional surface where a Koopman operator (a linear operator that describes evolution in a transformed space) approximates the dynamics, cutting down on expensive nonlinear iteration.

Key Claims

  • Reservoir digital twin operations require fast simulation to enable real-time closed-loop management, drilling decisions, and optimization.
  • Physics-aware machine learning can give proxy models properties related to material balance and conservation laws (rules about what cannot be created or destroyed).
  • E2CO uses data-driven reduction and neural networks for simultaneous matching of state trajectories and input-output behavior.
  • The non-intrusive method does not require access to the reservoir simulator's internal equations or structure.
  • POD (proper orthogonal decomposition, a data-driven compression method) can be extended by adding extra state dimensions to create a bilinear system (one where the nonlinearity comes only from interactions between state and input).
  • Autoencoders can linearize reservoir equations on a reduced manifold by treating the Koopman operator (a linear map in a transformed space) as the evolution rule.

Important Terms

Porous Media Flow

Movement of fluids (oil, gas, water) through materials that contain pores, such as rock in underground reservoirs.

Reservoir Digital Twin

A fast computational copy of a reservoir used for monitoring changes, testing decisions, and running control systems.

E2CO

Embed to Control and Observe—a non-intrusive method that combines model reduction and machine learning to match both state evolution and input-output response without accessing simulator internals.

Koopman Operator

A linear operator that describes how observables (measurable properties) of a nonlinear dynamical system evolve in time.

Manifold Linearization

Learning a transformed lower-dimensional space where the nonlinear dynamics become closer to linear evolution.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture ties together Koopman theory, autoencoder-based reduced-order models, and industrial digital twins applied to reservoirs.
  • It also complements talks on learned simulators by showing how to deploy fast models non-intrusively alongside existing simulators.
  • It reinforces the seminar's theme that the most useful learned representation is one where control and prediction become computationally efficient.

Practical Caution

A learned linear manifold only stays accurate within the operating conditions present in the training data. Real closed-loop reservoir control needs monitoring to catch when the latent linearization stops matching the simulator's behavior or actual field measurements.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that a digital twin needs a representation where the control problem becomes cheap to solve. Manifold linearization (learning a transformed space where dynamics are closer to linear) is one way to turn nonlinear reservoir physics into something operational.

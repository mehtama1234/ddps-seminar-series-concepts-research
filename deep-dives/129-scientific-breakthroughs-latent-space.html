Talk 129 · Johannes Kepler University

Johannes Brandstetter: The Next Wave of Scientific Breakthroughs Is in the Latent Space

This lecture argues that scientific models trained on broad datasets will unlock new progress by learning useful latent spaces—simplified, learned representations—for complex physical systems.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Scaling and language model methods have transformed how we handle text, but scientific and engineering domains need models that respect physical structure, multiple types of fields, geometry, and long-range dynamics. The question is where the next useful way of representing information for scientific machine learning will come from.

First-Principles Model

A latent space is a learned, compressed representation where complicated states become easier to model, compare, generate, or improve. The talk connects recent progress in weather and climate modeling, computer vision, physics-informed learning, neural operators (neural networks that learn mappings between functions), multi-physics modeling (systems where multiple physical processes interact), molecular dynamics, computational fluid dynamics (CFD: simulating how fluids flow and behave), and nuclear fusion. The claim is that scientific breakthroughs will come from latent spaces that encode the right physical variables and interactions.

Key Claims

  • Scientific machine learning must adapt the techniques that work at large scale in language to engineering and physical domains.
  • Weather and climate modeling show recent successes in machine learning for science.
  • Scientific latent spaces connect ideas from computer vision, physics-informed learning, and neural operators.
  • The next challenges involve multi-physics systems, molecular dynamics, computational fluid dynamics, and nuclear fusion.
  • Latent spaces can support building models, generating new possibilities, and scientific discovery.
  • The value of a latent space depends on whether it actually captures the important physical structure.

Important Terms

Latent Space

A learned, simpler lower-dimensional representation of data or physical states.

Scientific Foundation Model

A model trained on broad scientific data to handle multiple tasks or domains.

Multi-Physics Modeling

Modeling systems where multiple physical processes interact and affect each other.

Neural Operator

A neural network that learns mappings between functions or types of data.

Computational Fluid Dynamics

Numerical simulation and analysis of how fluids flow and behave.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects the DDPS technical arc to broader themes about models trained on large datasets.
  • It complements autoencoder, neural-operator, and materials-foundation-model lectures by making learned representation the central object.
  • It also links to earlier discussions on how AI capabilities grow with scale: what a model learns depends heavily on the representations and data distributions it encounters.

Practical Caution

A latent space can compress data without preserving why things happen (causal relationships) or the underlying physical laws. Scientific value requires checking whether it actually predicts well on unseen problems, whether you can control the system using it, and whether it helps discover new scientific insights.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that scientific machine learning progress often depends on finding the representation where the physics becomes simpler.

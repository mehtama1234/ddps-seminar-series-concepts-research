Talk 135 · ETH Zurich

Siddhartha Mishra: AI for Data-Driven Simulations in Physics

This lecture reviews neural PDE surrogates and neural operators for accelerating simulations across physics and engineering.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

PDE simulations are central to physics and engineering, but high-fidelity numerical methods can be extremely expensive. Fast AI surrogates promise lower cost, but they must learn solution operators accurately across domains, geometries, and physical conditions.

First-Principles Model

Neural operators learn the PDE solution operator from data: a map from inputs such as initial conditions, coefficients, sources, or geometry to solution fields. The talk reviews convolution- and attention-based neural operators, graph and transformer architectures for arbitrary domains, and conditional diffusion models for data-driven physics simulations.

Key Claims

  • PDEs model a wide variety of physics and engineering phenomena.
  • Classical numerical methods can be computationally expensive.
  • AI-based neural PDE surrogates aim to be fast and accurate.
  • Neural operators are a major ML paradigm for PDEs.
  • Convolution and attention architectures support state-of-the-art neural operators.
  • Graph, transformer, and diffusion-based models extend PDE learning to arbitrary domains and generative settings.

Important Terms

Neural Operator

A model that learns mappings between function spaces, often PDE input-to-solution maps.

PDE Surrogate

A learned approximation that predicts PDE solutions faster than a numerical solver.

Graph Neural Operator

A neural operator using graph structure to handle irregular domains or meshes.

Transformer Architecture

An attention-based neural architecture used here for PDE modeling and arbitrary-domain interactions.

Conditional Diffusion Model

A generative model that samples outputs conditioned on inputs through iterative denoising.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk summarizes many DDPS threads: PDE surrogates, neural operators, attention, graphs, and data-driven physics.
  • It connects to operator-network, differentiable-solver, and latent-space talks.
  • It also links to the user's broader AI scaling interests because neural operators are a domain-specific foundation-model direction for physics.

Practical Caution

Neural PDE surrogates must be validated for conservation, stability, uncertainty, and out-of-distribution conditions. Speed is not useful if rare physics is missed.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that neural operators try to learn the simulator itself: the input-to-solution map behind families of PDE problems.

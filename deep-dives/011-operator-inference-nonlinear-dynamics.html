Talk 11 · Max Planck Institute for Dynamics of Complex Technical Systems

Pawan Goyal: Physics-Informed Learning For Nonlinear Dynamical Systems

This lecture presents operator inference as a way to learn reduced dynamical models directly from data while preserving known physical structure.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Engineering design, control, optimization, and uncertainty studies need dynamical models that can be simulated repeatedly. High-fidelity discretized models can be too expensive, and legacy or commercial codes may not expose the matrices needed for classical projection-based model reduction.

First-Principles Model

A dynamical system can often be reduced to a low-dimensional state if the important behavior lies in dominant modes. Operator inference tries to learn the reduced operators directly from trajectory data, using the assumed physical structure of the governing equations so the learned model resembles the ROM that would have been obtained intrusively.

Key Claims

  • Dynamical models are needed to analyze transient behavior under changing operating conditions.
  • Two bottlenecks are discretization of complex processes and the high dimensionality of high-fidelity models.
  • The goal is to move from PDE data directly to reduced-order models without requiring access to a discretized model.
  • Operator inference learns reduced operators from data while using the known structure of the physical model.
  • If the data are informative enough, the learned operators can approximate intrusive POD-style reduced models.
  • The approach is especially relevant when the data come from legacy codes, commercial solvers, or dedicated simulation toolboxes.

Important Terms

Operator Inference

A method that learns the operators of a reduced dynamical system from state trajectory data.

High-Fidelity Model

A detailed discretized physical model with many degrees of freedom, often too expensive for repeated simulation.

Dominant Modes

Low-dimensional directions that capture the main behavior of a high-dimensional dynamical system.

Legacy Code

A simulator that can produce input-output data but does not easily expose internal discretization or operator matrices.

Physics-Structured Learning

Learning constrained by known equation structure instead of fitting an unconstrained black-box predictor.

Connections Across The Series

  • This continues the non-intrusive ROM theme but focuses on learning the reduced dynamics themselves.
  • It connects to differentiable simulation and inverse problems because a learned reduced model can become the fast inner loop for control.
  • It also reinforces the series-wide pattern: use data to infer missing operators, but keep physical structure whenever possible.

Practical Caution

Operator inference is not just curve fitting. If the assumed operator structure is wrong or the training trajectories do not excite important modes, the reduced model may look accurate on short rollouts and fail in prediction or control.

Takeaway

The lecture's key idea is to learn the small dynamical system that classical reduction would have produced, but to infer it directly from data when the full discretized model is unavailable or too costly.

Talk 11 · Max Planck Institute for Dynamics of Complex Technical Systems

Pawan Goyal: Physics-Informed Learning For Nonlinear Dynamical Systems

Operator inference learns reduced dynamical models from data while keeping the known physics structure built in.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Engineering design, control, optimization, and uncertainty studies need dynamical models you can simulate many times over. High-fidelity detailed models can take too long to run, and older or commercial simulators may not give you the internal equations needed for classical projection-based model reduction.

First-Principles Model

A dynamical system can often be captured by a small set of key directions (dominant modes—the low-dimensional patterns that describe the main behavior). Operator inference tries to learn the small reduced operators directly from trajectory data, using the known structure of the governing equations so the learned model looks like what classical reduction would have produced if you had access to all the equations.

Key Claims

  • Dynamical models are needed to analyze how systems change over time under new operating conditions.
  • Two major obstacles are turning complex processes into equations and the huge size of detailed high-fidelity models.
  • The goal is to jump from differential equation data straight to small reduced models without needing the full discretized model.
  • Operator inference learns the reduced equations from data while keeping the known structure of the physics.
  • If the data excite enough different behaviors, the learned equations can match the reduced models that classical methods would have found.
  • The approach is especially useful when data come from older codes, commercial solvers, or specialized simulation tools.

Important Terms

Operator Inference

A method that learns the equations of a reduced dynamical system by studying state trajectory data.

High-Fidelity Model

A detailed mathematical model with many degrees of freedom, often so expensive to run that you cannot simulate it repeatedly.

Dominant Modes

Low-dimensional patterns that capture the main behavior of a large dynamical system.

Legacy Code

A simulator that produces output data but does not easily give you the internal equations or matrices that make it work.

Physics-Structured Learning

Learning that follows known equation structure instead of fitting an unconstrained general-purpose predictor.

Connections Across The Series

  • This continues the non-intrusive ROM theme but focuses on learning the reduced dynamics themselves.
  • It connects to differentiable simulation and inverse problems because a learned reduced model can become the fast inner loop for control.
  • It also reinforces the series-wide pattern: use data to infer missing equations, but keep physical structure whenever possible.

Practical Caution

Operator inference is not just fitting a curve. If the assumed operator structure is wrong or the training trajectories do not exercise important modes, the reduced model may predict accurately on short runs and then fail in longer predictions or control.

Takeaway

Learn the small dynamical system that classical reduction would have produced, but infer it directly from data when the full detailed model is unavailable or too costly to use.

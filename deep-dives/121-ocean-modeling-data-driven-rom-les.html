Talk 121 · University of Houston

Annalisa Quaini: Reducing the Cost of Ocean Modeling with a Data-Driven ROM and LES

This lecture combines POD (extracting the main patterns in data), LSTM (a neural network for sequences), and LES (simulating large features while approximating small ones) to speed up two-layer quasi-geostrophic ocean simulation.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Ocean models such as the two-layer quasi-geostrophic equations need large areas and fine detail to capture turbulent motion. Running the full equations is slow, but simpler models must still keep the right potential-vorticity (a number that nearly stays constant in rotating fluids) and stream-function (the flow pattern) behavior.

First-Principles Model

The approach has two parts. First, large eddy simulation uses nonlinear spatial filters to create cheap training data that still shows how unresolved small-scale turbulence affects the larger flow. Second, a data-driven reduced model applies POD to pull out the main patterns in each field and trains LSTM networks to update the reduced numbers forward in time. Together, the filter makes training data fast to create while the reduced model makes predictions fast to run.

Key Claims

  • Two-layer quasi-geostrophic equations describe stratified wind-driven ocean flow.
  • High-resolution ocean simulation costs a lot because of large domains and the wide range of turbulent scales.
  • The proposed method joins a data-driven reduced model with data from large eddy simulation.
  • Nonlinear spatial filters are used in the large eddy simulation step.
  • POD pulls out the main modes for each field variable.
  • LSTM recurrent neural networks track how the reduced numbers evolve over time.

Important Terms

Two-Layer Quasi-Geostrophic Equations

A simplified ocean model that describes stratified rotating flow using potential vorticity (a conserved number in the flow) and stream function (the flow pattern).

Large Eddy Simulation

A way to simulate turbulence by resolving the large swirls exactly and approximating the small swirls.

POD

Proper Orthogonal Decomposition, a method that extracts the dominant patterns from simulation or measurement data.

LSTM

Long Short-Term Memory, a type of recurrent neural network (a network that processes sequences) built to remember and update information over time.

Potential Vorticity

A quantity in rotating stratified fluids that stays roughly constant and drives the dynamics.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects reduced models, turbulent geophysical flows, large eddy simulation, and recurrent neural networks.
  • It complements climate and ocean surrogate modeling by pairing physics-based filtering with learned updates.
  • It also links to multiscale modeling because large eddy simulation and reduced models each cut cost at different stages.

Practical Caution

The reduced model depends on how well the large eddy simulation captures the small-scale effects. If the filter misses important physics at the small scales, the LSTM may learn a distorted version of the dynamics.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that ocean-model cost drops when you mix physics-based coarse simulation to generate data with a learned reduced model to make fast predictions.

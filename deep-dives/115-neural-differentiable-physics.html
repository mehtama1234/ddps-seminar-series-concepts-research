Talk 115 · University of Notre Dame

Jianxun Wang: Neural Differentiable Physics

This lecture presents neural differentiable physics as a framework for blending incomplete physical solvers with trainable neural components.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

First-principles numerical models are essential for engineering prediction, but they can be incomplete, expensive, or too slow for inverse problems, optimization, uncertainty quantification, and real-time control. Pure neural models may be fast but can lose physical reliability.

First-Principles Model

Neural differentiable physics treats simulation as a differentiable program composed of known physical components and trainable neural components. The physical part supplies conservation laws, discretization structure, or partial knowledge; the neural part fills missing closures, corrections, or unknown terms. Because the whole pipeline is differentiable, data assimilation and inverse/optimization tasks can update neural and physical parameters together.

Key Claims

  • Traditional first-principles models can fail when the underlying physics is incomplete.
  • High computational cost limits repeated-query applications such as inverse design and UQ.
  • Neural differentiable physics creates a differentiable programming platform for computational physics.
  • The framework blends incomplete physics components with trainable neural architecture components.
  • Data-augmented simulation can improve predictive modeling and control.
  • The approach aims to unify numerical PDEs and deep learning.

Important Terms

Neural Differentiable Physics

A framework that embeds physics solvers and neural components in a differentiable computation pipeline.

Data-Augmented Computational Physics

Computational physics enhanced by data-trained components that correct or complete the model.

Closure Model

A model for unresolved or unknown terms needed to close a reduced or approximate physical system.

Inverse Problem

A problem where hidden parameters, inputs, or causes are inferred from observed effects.

Differentiable Programming

Programming where computations are designed so derivatives can be propagated through them.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects differentiable solvers, closure learning, PINNs, and hybrid physics-ML.
  • It complements ROM and operator-learning talks by focusing on incomplete physics rather than only fast surrogates.
  • It also links to scientific AI reliability because known physics remains inside the trainable loop.

Practical Caution

Hybrid models can hide errors in the trainable component. Validation should check whether the neural correction is physically meaningful or merely compensating for dataset artifacts.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that incomplete physics should be corrected inside a differentiable simulation loop rather than replaced wholesale by a black box.

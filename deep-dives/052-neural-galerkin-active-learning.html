Talk 52 · New York University

Benjamin Peherstorfer: Neural Galerkin Schemes With Active Learning

This lecture presents Neural Galerkin schemes that solve high-dimensional evolution equations by fitting neural-network states over time and actively choosing training samples based on where the current solution approximation fails most.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

High-dimensional time-dependent PDEs are difficult because sampling uniformly across the time-space domain wastes data, while neural networks need well-chosen training points to use their approximation power well. A fixed training set may miss moving localized structures such as waves, fronts, or concentrated features that travel across the domain.

First-Principles Model

Galerkin methods approximate a PDE solution inside a chosen trial space by measuring how much the guess violates the differential equation and reducing that error. Neural Galerkin replaces the classical trial basis with a neural-network representation whose parameters evolve sequentially in time. Instead of drawing all training points blindly, the method uses this error measurement to actively collect samples where the current approximation performs worst.

Key Claims

  • Estimating how well a model will work on new data is hard in science and engineering when informative samples are scarce.
  • Neural Galerkin schemes fit network parameters by minimizing the PDE error sequentially over time.
  • Residual-based sampling provides a self-guided active-learning mechanism.
  • Active data collection is needed to make neural approximation strengths practical in high dimensions.
  • The approach works especially well for evolution equations with moving concentrated features.
  • The method treats training data generation as part of the numerical solver rather than a separate preparation step.

Important Terms

Galerkin Method

A numerical method that approximates a solution in a chosen space and reduces how much the guess violates the governing equation.

Neural Galerkin Scheme

A Galerkin-style PDE solver where the chosen space is parameterized by a neural network.

PDE Residual

The amount by which an approximate solution violates the governing differential equation.

Active Learning

A data-selection strategy that chooses new training samples based on what would most improve the model's performance.

Evolution Equation

A differential equation describing how a system changes over time.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects to PINNs through error minimization, but adds a sequential time-stepping and active-learning view aligned with numerical solvers.
  • It also connects to ROMs and high-dimensional PDE methods because the neural trial space is an adaptive reduced representation.
  • It reinforces the series' data-generation theme: where you sample can matter as much as what architecture you use.

Practical Caution

Minimizing the equation error can hide the truth if the sampling distribution skips important regions or if the equation error stays small while the actual solution error grows large. Active learning reduces this risk but does not guarantee the answer is correct without outside checking.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that neural PDE solvers should adapt their samples to track the dynamics. The training set is part of the numerical method, not just preparation before it begins.

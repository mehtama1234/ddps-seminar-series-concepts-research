Talk 88 · ETH Zurich

Eleni Chatzi: Twinning And Model Discovery For Engineered Systems

This lecture frames structural health monitoring and digital twinning as hybrid model-discovery problems where data-driven sensing must be integrated with mechanics to assess, forecast, and act on engineered structures.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Modern engineered structures are interconnected, exposed to variable loads, and operated in harsh environments. Basic SHM—structural health monitoring, a sensing and analysis method to detect damage—can alert you to problems, but higher-level tasks such as measuring damage size, predicting how long a structure will last, and planning maintenance require more than fitting numbers to sensor data, because the monitored structure must be represented as it actually is now and under future stresses.

First-Principles Model

A structural twin is a live representation of a physical asset. Sparse sensors provide measurements, while mechanics-based models spread that information to unmeasured locations and future conditions. Model discovery identifies damage, material properties, or structural states that make the model match reality. The twin becomes actionable when it can detect damage, locate it, measure it, predict future performance, and suggest maintenance decisions.

Key Claims

  • SHM tasks form a hierarchy: first detect damage, then locate it, then measure it, then predict remaining useful life.
  • Purely data-driven methods fall short for higher-level SHM tasks such as damage measurement and remaining-life prediction.
  • Digital twins of monitored structures require physics-based representations grounded in mechanics.
  • Hybrid SHM can represent a structure as it actually is rather than only as it was designed.
  • Augmented twin representations can anticipate performance under future stressors.
  • A useful twin should recommend preventive and repair actions, not only display measurements.

Important Terms

Structural Health Monitoring

Sensing and modeling methods for detecting, locating, measuring, and predicting damage in structures.

Augmented Twin

A digital twin updated with data from sensors and built on physics-based representations of the actual structure.

Residual Life

The remaining useful time a system has before degradation or failure becomes unacceptable.

Full-Field Reconstruction

Estimating structural response across the entire object from a small number of sensor measurements.

Model Discovery

Learning model parts, states, or properties that explain the observed behavior of a structure.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects digital twins, SHM, uncertainty, and model discovery in a deployment-oriented structural setting.
  • It complements guided-wave SHM by moving from damage detection and location toward prediction and action.
  • It also links to ROM and surrogate model talks because twins need fast but mechanics-aware representations.

Practical Caution

A dashboard displaying sensor data is not a digital twin. Higher-level SHM needs mechanics, uncertainty accounting, and future-load reasoning before it can reliably support maintenance decisions.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that twinning combines three things: state estimation (what is the structure now), mechanics (how will it behave), and decision support (what should we do). Sensors alone do not create a useful twin.

Talk 51 · Harvard University

Petros Koumoutsakos: Artificial Intelligence And Scientific Computing For Fluid Mechanics

This lecture argues for forming careful alloys of AI and scientific computing for complex flows, using learned effective dynamics and multi-agent reinforcement learning while distinguishing real progress from hype.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Fluid mechanics combines nonlinear dynamics, multiscale structure, turbulence, flow-structure interaction, and control. AI methods are widely promoted as replacements for simulation, but black-box learning alone is unreliable for complex flows. The problem is how to combine data, numerical solvers, physical insight, and learning algorithms into systems that predict and control flows better than either component alone.

First-Principles Model

Scientific computing provides discretized physical laws, numerical solvers, and stability constraints. AI provides flexible approximation, representation learning, and control policies. An alloy is a hybrid in which each component does what it is structurally good at: simulators generate or constrain dynamics, learned models approximate effective low-dimensional behavior, and reinforcement learning explores control strategies under physical simulation feedback.

Key Claims

  • AI has benefited from massive hardware and data growth, but fluid-mechanics claims require careful separation of promise from hype.
  • Complex flow prediction and control should use fusions of AI and simulations rather than naive replacement.
  • Learning effective dynamics is one alloy: microscopic or high-fidelity simulations inform a reduced dynamical description.
  • The equation-free framework is an important ancestor of learned effective dynamics.
  • Multi-agent reinforcement learning can be fused with scientific computing for flow-structure interaction and control.
  • Successes and failures both matter because they reveal when physical structure is necessary.

Important Terms

Computing Alloy

A hybrid computational system that combines scientific computing, models, numerics, and machine learning.

Effective Dynamics

A reduced dynamical description that captures the behavior of a complex system at the scale of interest.

Flow-Structure Interaction

Coupled dynamics between a fluid and moving or deforming solid bodies.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

A learning setup where multiple agents learn policies through interaction with an environment and each other.

Scientific Computing

Numerical methods and computational algorithms for solving mathematical models of physical systems.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture directly extends Kevrekidis' equation-free and effective-dynamics ideas into fluid mechanics.
  • It connects to later surrogate-acceleration talks because the goal is not speed alone but faithful prediction and control.
  • It also links to RL-based scientific control, where simulation becomes the training environment.

Practical Caution

For fluids, a learned model that looks impressive on one rollout can fail under distribution shift, unstable long-time integration, or control feedback. Hybridization must include numerical and physical checks.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that AI for fluids should be engineered as a hybrid scientific-computing system. The right question is not AI or solvers, but which alloy is stable, accurate, and controllable.

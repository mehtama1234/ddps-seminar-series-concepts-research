Talk 101 · University of Hong Kong

Z. Zhang: DeepParticle: Learning Invariant Measure by a Deep Neural Network Minimizing Wasserstein Distance

This lecture presents DeepParticle, a method that learns parameterized invariant measures for high-dimensional PDE-related particle dynamics.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

High-dimensional PDEs with fronts, sharp gradients, or concentrations are difficult for mesh methods, while mesh-free interacting-particle methods can become expensive for long-time resolved computation. The challenge is to predict behavior at costly physical regimes without running the full particle computation there.

First-Principles Model

For Fisher-KPP front speeds in incompressible flows, PDE analysis reduces the target quantity to the principal eigenvalue of an advection-diffusion operator. A Feynman-Kac stochastic representation enables a genetic interacting-particle algorithm whose distribution converges to a large-time invariant measure. DeepParticle learns a family of these invariant measures, parameterized by the Peclet number, using a physically parameterized neural network and an optimal-transport/Wasserstein objective. Training at moderate Peclet numbers lets the model predict more expensive high-Peclet regimes.

Key Claims

  • DeepParticle integrates deep learning, optimal transport, and interacting particle computation.
  • The case study focuses on Fisher-KPP front speeds in incompressible flows.
  • The front-speed computation can be reduced to a principal eigenvalue problem.
  • The Feynman-Kac formula gives a stochastic representation usable by particle algorithms.
  • The learned object is a parameterized family of invariant measures.
  • The method extends toward learning stochastic particle dynamics such as Keller-Segel chemotaxis aggregation.

Important Terms

DeepParticle

A method combining neural networks, optimal transport, and interacting particles to learn invariant measures.

Invariant Measure

A probability distribution that remains unchanged under the long-time evolution of a dynamical or stochastic process.

Feynman-Kac Formula

A representation linking certain PDE quantities to expectations over stochastic processes.

Interacting Particle Method

A simulation method that represents distributions using many particles whose dynamics interact or are resampled.

Wasserstein Distance

An optimal-transport distance measuring how much mass must move to transform one distribution into another.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects stochastic dynamics, PDE analysis, optimal transport, and deep learning.
  • It complements talks on operator learning by learning invariant distributions rather than only input-output solution maps.
  • It also links to rare-event and multiscale simulation because the method targets expensive parameter regimes.

Practical Caution

Learning invariant measures at moderate parameters does not automatically guarantee accuracy at extreme parameters. Extrapolation in Peclet number must be checked against physics or higher-fidelity computation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that some high-dimensional PDE questions can be made learnable by targeting the long-time distributional object that controls the quantity of interest.

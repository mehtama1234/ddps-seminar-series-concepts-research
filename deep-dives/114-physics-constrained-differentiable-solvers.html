Talk 114 · UC Berkeley

Aditi Krishnapriyan: Bridging Numerical Methods and Deep Learning with Physics-Constrained Differentiable Solvers

This lecture shows how differentiable solvers (numerical methods you can compute gradients through) let neural networks learn PDE solutions while respecting physics constraints and classical numerical structure.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Machine-learning models for spatiotemporal systems (things that change over space and time) need to make accurate predictions even with limited training data, but purely neural solvers can skip the physics rules while purely classical solvers cannot learn new behavior from data. The goal is to build models that combine the best of both: learned patterns from data and reliable physics-based computation.

First-Principles Model

A differentiable solver turns a numerical method into a trainable layer so gradients can flow backward through it. Instead of solving a PDE separately from training, you embed the solve—whether spectral (expanding in basis functions), finite-difference, or optimization—as part of the neural network graph. The talk covers self-supervised learning that shifts what basis functions the network learns in, plus simulation-in-the-loop approaches where you fold PDE-constrained optimization (finding the best solution subject to the physics equations) directly into the network.

Key Claims

  • Machine learning is becoming essential for modeling systems that change over space and time.
  • Learning from small datasets requires strategies that guarantee predictions stay physically valid.
  • Self-supervised learning can retrain spectral methods to learn in more useful basis functions.
  • You can build learning loops where simulation and optimization happen inside training.
  • Combining numerical methods and neural networks as one trainable system is possible and useful.
  • General learning strategies that work across many different systems and architectures are needed.

Important Terms

Differentiable Solver

A numerical method whose calculations can output gradients, allowing it to be trained as part of a neural network.

Physics-Constrained Learning

Learning where physical laws or conservation rules are baked into the model or training, not just added as a penalty term.

PDE-Constrained Optimization

Finding the best solution to a problem where all candidates must satisfy a differential equation.

Simulation-in-the-Loop

A training method that runs the simulator or solver step-by-step during training or predictions, not just at the end.

Spectral Method

A numerical approach that represents unknown quantities as sums of basis functions (like sine waves) rather than grid values.

Connections Across The Series

  • This work bridges neural PDE solvers, physics-informed neural networks, differentiable programming, and classical numerical analysis.
  • It extends physics-informed learning by embedding the solver structure itself rather than only adding loss-term penalties.
  • It also connects to inverse design and control systems because you can compute gradients backward through the solver to optimize inputs.

Practical Caution

A differentiable solver inherits all the strengths and weaknesses of the underlying numerical method: its stability properties (how errors grow), discretization errors (approximation from continuous to discrete), and modeling assumptions. Making it differentiable does not automatically make the physics correct or robust.

Takeaway

The core insight is that solver structure—not just the loss function—can become part of the neural architecture, so learning respects physical laws while still tuning predictions to fit data.

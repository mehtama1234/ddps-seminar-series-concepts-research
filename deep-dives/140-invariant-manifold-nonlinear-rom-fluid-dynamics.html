Talk 140 · Stanford University

Balint Kaszas: Invariant Manifold-Based Nonlinear Model Reduction for Fluid Dynamics

This lecture uses spectral submanifolds to build nonlinear reduced-order models for fluid dynamics.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Fluid flows are high-dimensional and nonlinear, and linear reduced-order models can miss transitions, multiple steady states, or chaotic dynamics. A better reduction should capture the low-dimensional nonlinear geometry that attracts the dynamics.

First-Principles Model

Dynamical-systems theory identifies invariant manifolds that organize trajectories. Spectral submanifolds are nonlinear continuations of spectral subspaces of the linearized system near stationary states. If the high-dimensional flow rapidly approaches or evolves near such a manifold, the dynamics can be restricted to it, giving a low-dimensional nonlinear ROM that captures transitions, coexisting steady states, and chaos.

Key Claims

  • Model reduction for nonlinear systems can be based on invariant manifolds.
  • Spectral submanifolds are nonlinear continuations of linear spectral subspaces.
  • These manifolds can act as low-dimensional attractors.
  • Invariant manifold models capture essential fluid dynamics.
  • The approach can model transitions between coexisting steady states and chaotic dynamics.
  • Examples include wall-bounded shear flows and fluid-structure interaction measurements.

Important Terms

Invariant Manifold

A geometric set that trajectories starting on it remain on under the dynamics.

Spectral Submanifold

A nonlinear invariant manifold continuing a spectral subspace of a linearized system.

Nonlinear Model Reduction

Reducing dynamics to a low-dimensional nonlinear manifold rather than a linear subspace.

Attractor

A set toward which trajectories evolve over time.

Wall-Bounded Shear Flow

A fluid flow constrained by walls with velocity shear, often central in turbulence studies.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk closes the ROM arc by connecting nonlinear manifolds, dynamical systems, and fluid dynamics.
  • It complements autoencoder, charting, and oblique-projection lectures by giving a dynamical-systems definition of the reduced manifold.
  • It also links to Koopman and modal-decomposition talks through spectral structure.

Practical Caution

Invariant-manifold ROMs depend on whether the identified manifold actually attracts the observed dynamics over the intended regime. Transient excursions away from it can break predictions.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that nonlinear ROMs should reduce dynamics onto the invariant geometric objects that organize motion.

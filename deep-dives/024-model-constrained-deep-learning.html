Talk 24 · University of Texas at Austin

Tan Bui-Thanh: Model-Constrained Deep Learning For Inference, Control And UQ

This lecture explains how to embed scientific forward models (mathematical rules that predict observations from parameters) into learning systems for inference, control, and uncertainty quantification.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Inverse problems and control tasks cannot rely on unconstrained prediction alone. Measurements may be sparse, noisy, high-dimensional, and indirect—meaning you get few readings, they contain errors, and each reading depends on many hidden causes. The unknown parameter space can also be high-dimensional. Bayesian or optimization-based inference may need to solve the forward model thousands of times, making computation prohibitively expensive.

First-Principles Model

A forward model takes parameters or shapes and predicts observations by solving a PDE (partial differential equation—an equation describing how something changes in space and time). An inverse problem tries to recover the hidden parameters from observations. In Bayesian inversion, the solution is a posterior distribution (a probability distribution over possible parameter values, updated after seeing the data). Model-constrained deep learning places the mathematical model inside the neural network or variational approximation so learning respects the physics while reducing the cost of exploring the posterior or control space.

Key Claims

  • Scientific machine learning combines scientific computing, which uses laws and equations from physics, with learning, which finds patterns in data.
  • Forward problems solve equations from known parameters; inverse problems work backward to find hidden causes from limited measurements.
  • High-dimensional parameters and high-dimensional data create severe computational challenges because there are too many possibilities to explore.
  • Bayesian inverse problems are expensive because finding the posterior distribution repeatedly calls the forward model.
  • Variational approaches can approximate the posterior by replacing it with a simpler, fast-to-compute distribution.
  • The key idea is to put the underlying mathematical model inside the variational or deep-learning architecture so the learning respects the physics.

Important Terms

Model-Constrained Deep Learning

A learning approach where predictions or latent variables are forced to obey a governing scientific model.

Forward Problem

Computing observations or states from known parameters by solving the physical model.

Inverse Problem

Inferring hidden parameters, shapes, or sources from observations.

Posterior Distribution

A probability distribution over unknowns after combining prior assumptions with observed data.

Variational Inference

Approximating a difficult posterior distribution with a simpler, parameterized distribution that is fast to compute.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk bridges differentiable simulation (running physics models backward through neural networks), implicit layers (neural networks that embed equations), and uncertainty-aware scientific machine learning.
  • It connects to digital twins because real twins need inference and uncertainty quantification, not only forward prediction.
  • It also connects to control because decision quality depends on model-constrained inference of the current state and its uncertainty.

Practical Caution

A model-constrained neural method is only as reliable as both pieces: the forward model must represent the relevant physics accurately, and the variational or neural approximation must capture the posterior uncertainty well enough for the downstream decision to be sound.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that inference and control need learning systems that respect the forward physics while making uncertainty computation fast enough to use.

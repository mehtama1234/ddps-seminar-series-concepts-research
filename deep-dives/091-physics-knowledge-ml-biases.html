Talk 91 · University of Cambridge

Alice Cicirello: Integrating Physics Knowledge In Machine Learning Strategies

This lecture organizes physics-enhanced machine learning around four biases: observational, inductive, learning, and discrepancy, using friction-force identification as a case study.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

ML models trained only from data can generalize poorly, extrapolate badly, and produce physically impossible predictions. Engineering problems often have partial physics knowledge, incomplete equations, noisy measurements, and limited data, so the question is how to insert the right physics knowledge into the right part of the learning strategy.

First-Principles Model

Physics knowledge can enter ML in different places. Observational bias changes the data by adding or choosing measurements informed by physics. Inductive bias embeds constraints, symmetries, boundaries, or conservation laws into architecture. Learning bias modifies losses, constraints, or algorithms. Discrepancy bias models known gaps between partial equations and observed behavior. Friction identification shows how sparse system ID and GPs can use these biases.

Key Claims

  • Physics knowledge improves small- and medium-data generalization and can enforce consistency with large data.
  • Data-only ML can suffer observational bias and physically inconsistent predictions.
  • Observational bias uses physics-informed data sources or augmentation.
  • Inductive bias embeds prior assumptions such as boundaries, conservation laws, and symmetries into the learning architecture.
  • Learning bias changes the loss, constraints, or algorithmic procedure.
  • Discrepancy bias uses partial physics knowledge or known model-form gaps, demonstrated through friction-force identification.

Important Terms

Observational Bias

Physics-informed choices about what data are collected, augmented, or emphasized.

Inductive Bias

A prior restriction built into a model architecture or representation.

Learning Bias

Physics information introduced through losses, constraints, or optimization choices.

Discrepancy Bias

A modeled correction for known gaps between an imperfect physics model and observations.

Friction Identification

Estimating friction forces or laws in a dynamical system from data and physics constraints.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture provides a useful taxonomy for many earlier talks on physics-informed, physics-guided, and physics-enhanced ML.
  • It connects to PINNs, sparse system identification, Gaussian-process physics priors, and discrepancy modeling.
  • It also gives language for deciding where physics should enter a model rather than saying only 'add physics'.

Practical Caution

Adding physics knowledge in the wrong bias channel can be ineffective or misleading. For example, a penalty loss may be weaker than an architectural constraint when the physical law must never be violated.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that physics can enter data, architecture, loss, or model discrepancy. Good SciML chooses the insertion point deliberately.

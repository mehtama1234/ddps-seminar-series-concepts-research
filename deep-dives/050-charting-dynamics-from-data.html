Talk 50 · University of Houston

Daniel Floryan: Charting Dynamics From Data

This lecture uses maps of curved surfaces (manifolds) and overlapping local coordinate systems (atlases and charts) to build small dynamical models directly from time-series data.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Many systems are high-dimensional, nonlinear, and hard to write down as first-principles equations, yet their trajectories often live on lower-dimensional surfaces. A single global coordinate system may twist that surface or fail to work, so the modeling problem is to find local coordinate systems and dynamical laws that together cover the entire state space.

First-Principles Model

A curved surface (manifold) can be described by an atlas: overlapping local coordinate systems with maps between local coordinates and the ambient state. From time-series data, you can cluster trajectories into patches, learn coordinates on each patch, learn local dynamics, and stitch the patches into a global dynamical description. The result is a minimal model in the true dimensionality of the system rather than the dimensionality of the measured state.

Key Claims

  • Data-driven model discovery is motivated by unknown equations and high-dimensional equations we do know.
  • Natural systems often evolve on low-dimensional surfaces inside high-dimensional state spaces.
  • An atlas of coordinate systems provides local ways to cover a curved surface.
  • Time-series data can be clustered into overlapping patches that become coordinate domains.
  • Learning local coordinate systems can produce dynamical models in the lowest useful dimension.
  • Local coordinate systems can separate the true shape of state space from coordinate artifacts and improve prediction or control.

Important Terms

Manifold

A space that may be curved overall but looks locally like ordinary flat space.

Atlas

A set of overlapping local coordinate systems that together describe a curved surface.

Chart

A local coordinate system describing one patch of a curved surface.

Intrinsic Dimension

The true number of degrees of freedom needed to describe the system's state.

Time-Series Data

Sequential observations of a system used to learn its shape and how it changes over time.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture complements Kevrekidis' work on discovering structure in data with a concrete geometric method.
  • It also connects to reduced-order models because both aim to find low-dimensional descriptions of how systems evolve.
  • It bridges manifold learning, nonlinear dynamics, and control-focused model discovery.

Practical Caution

Local coordinate systems require good coverage and overlap. If the data miss parts of the surface or transitions between patches, the learned atlas can produce jumps or misleading dynamics.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that dimension reduction is not just compression. For dynamical systems, the learned coordinates must support a valid evolution rule.

Talk 50 · University of Houston

Daniel Floryan: Charting Dynamics From Data

This lecture uses the geometry of manifolds and atlases of charts to learn minimal dynamical models directly from time-series data.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Many systems are high-dimensional, nonlinear, and difficult to derive first-principles equations for, yet their trajectories often live on lower-dimensional structures. A single global coordinate system may distort that structure or fail entirely, so the modeling problem is to find local coordinates and dynamics that together cover the state space.

First-Principles Model

A manifold can be represented by an atlas: overlapping local coordinate charts with maps between local coordinates and the ambient state. From time-series data, one can cluster trajectories into patches, learn coordinates on each patch, learn local dynamics, and stitch the patches into a global dynamical description. The result is a minimal model in the intrinsic dimension of the system rather than the dimension of the measured state.

Key Claims

  • Data-driven model discovery is motivated by unknown governing equations and high-dimensional known equations.
  • Natural systems often evolve on low-dimensional manifolds inside high-dimensional state spaces.
  • An atlas of charts provides local coordinate systems for covering a manifold.
  • Time-series data can be clustered into overlapping patches that become coordinate domains.
  • Learning local charts can produce dynamical models in the lowest useful dimension.
  • Local charting can separate state-space geometry from coordinate artifacts and improve prediction or control.

Important Terms

Manifold

A space that may be globally curved but looks locally like a low-dimensional Euclidean space.

Atlas

A collection of overlapping charts that together describe a manifold.

Chart

A local coordinate representation of one patch of a manifold.

Intrinsic Dimension

The true low-dimensional degrees of freedom needed to describe the system's state.

Time-Series Data

Sequential observations of a system used to infer its geometry and dynamics.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture complements Kevrekidis' representation-discovery theme with a concrete geometric construction.
  • It also connects to ROMs because both seek low-dimensional dynamical descriptions.
  • It is a bridge between manifold learning, nonlinear dynamics, and control-oriented model discovery.

Practical Caution

Local charts require good coverage and overlap. If the data miss parts of the manifold or transitions between patches, the learned atlas can produce discontinuous or misleading dynamics.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that dimension reduction is not just compression. For dynamical systems, the learned coordinates must support a valid evolution law.

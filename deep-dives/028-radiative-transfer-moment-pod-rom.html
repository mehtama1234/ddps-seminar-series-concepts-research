Talk 28 · North Carolina State University

Dmitriy Anistratov: ROMs For Thermal Radiative Transfer Based On Moment Equations And POD

This lecture builds reduced-order models for nonlinear thermal radiative transfer by projecting the Boltzmann transport equation into moment equations and compressing closure quantities.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Thermal radiative transfer is high-dimensional, nonlinear, multiscale, and strongly coupled. Directly solving the transport equation is expensive because the unknown depends on space, time, angle, and energy. Classical low-order models such as diffusion or P1 can be fast but may lose accuracy.

First-Principles Model

The method uses nonlinear projection to derive a hierarchy of low-order moment equations from the Boltzmann transport equation. The hard part is closure: the lower-order moment system needs quantities such as the Eddington tensor to represent angular transport effects. POD or DMD compresses these closure quantities from available high-fidelity data.

Key Claims

  • Thermal radiative transfer is a useful test platform for high-energy-density and radiative-hydrodynamics problems.
  • The main computational burden is solving the high-dimensional transport equation.
  • Nonlinear projection can produce low-order moment equations for radiation intensity.
  • The Eddington tensor provides closure for the moment system.
  • POD and DMD can approximate closure data over phase space and time.
  • Removing the transport equation from online calculations can bring cost closer to diffusion or P1-type models while retaining controlled quality.

Important Terms

Thermal Radiative Transfer

Modeling radiation energy transport coupled with material energy exchange.

Boltzmann Transport Equation

A high-dimensional equation for particle or radiation intensity across space, direction, energy, and time.

Moment Equation

A lower-dimensional equation obtained by integrating or projecting a transport equation over some variables.

Eddington Tensor

A closure quantity representing angular information needed by low-order radiative-transfer moment equations.

POD/DMD Closure

Using proper orthogonal decomposition or dynamic mode decomposition to approximate closure terms from data.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk adds a transport-heavy ROM example beyond fluids and mechanics.
  • It connects to LES-ROM closure: both reduce a high-dimensional physics model and then must model the effect of removed variables.
  • It also illustrates hybrid physics-data modeling where the equations define the low-order structure and data compresses the closure.

Practical Caution

A low-order radiative transfer model is only as good as its closure. Compressing the Eddington tensor with POD or DMD requires representative data over the regimes where the ROM will be used.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that moment ROMs reduce transport by keeping the low-order physics and learning or compressing the closure that the projection creates.

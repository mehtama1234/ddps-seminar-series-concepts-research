Talk 116 · Caltech

Akhil Nekkanti: Data-Driven Techniques for Analysis of Turbulent Flows

This lecture uses data-driven modal analysis, especially SPOD, to reconstruct, denoise, and interpret turbulent flow data.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Turbulent flows are high-dimensional, nonlinear, and unstable, yet they contain coherent structures that drive energy and dynamics. Measurements and simulations are often noisy or incomplete, so analysis tools must extract meaningful structure from compromised data.

First-Principles Model

Spectral proper orthogonal decomposition is a frequency-domain version of PCA tailored to flow data. It decomposes turbulent fields into coherent structures organized by energy and frequency. Extending SPOD to low-rank reconstruction, denoising, and frequency-time analysis allows missing regions to be estimated from spatial-temporal correlations and intermittent structures to be tracked.

Key Claims

  • Turbulent flows are challenging because of high dimensionality, instability, and nonlinearity.
  • Data-driven techniques reduce complexity by extracting key flow features.
  • SPOD is a powerful frequency-domain modal tool for turbulent-flow analysis.
  • Low-rank reconstruction can recover gappy or compromised data.
  • Frequency-time analysis helps study intermittency of coherent structures.
  • Coherent structures are dynamically and energetically important despite turbulent chaos.

Important Terms

Turbulence

Irregular, multi-scale fluid motion characterized by instability and nonlinear interactions.

SPOD

Spectral Proper Orthogonal Decomposition, a frequency-domain modal decomposition for flow data.

Coherent Structure

An organized flow pattern that carries important energy or dynamics inside turbulence.

Gappy Reconstruction

Estimating missing or corrupted parts of a field from the observed portions.

Low-Rank Reconstruction

Approximating data using a small number of dominant modes or components.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects modal decomposition, ROMs, turbulent-flow diagnostics, and data repair.
  • It complements POD/DMD/Koopman lectures by emphasizing spectral and intermittent structure.
  • It also links to sensor placement and experimental design because missing data can be reconstructed from correlations.

Practical Caution

Low-rank turbulent-flow reconstructions can remove noise while also removing rare but important dynamics. Reconstruction quality should be tested against physically meaningful events, not just average error.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that turbulence analysis becomes tractable by extracting coherent low-rank structure without forgetting intermittency.

Talk 74 · Brown University

Ameya Jagtap: Scientific Machine Learning Through The Lens Of PINNs

This lecture surveys physics-informed neural networks as a SciML framework, emphasizing their capabilities, limitations, scalable variants, and causal time-decomposition strategies.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Traditional scientific computing requires known physics, boundary and initial conditions, meshes, and long simulations. PINNs offer mesh-light integration of physics and data, but vanilla PINNs face scaling and trainability limits for big data, large domains, long time horizons, and parameterized PDEs.

First-Principles Model

A PINN represents a solution with a neural network and trains it with data and PDE residual constraints. Scalable PINN variants decompose the problem: conservative PINNs enforce conservation across subdomains, extended PINNs split large domains, and causal or temporal sweeping trains time-dependent problems in an order that respects physical causality rather than treating all time points equally.

Key Claims

  • PIML has emerged as an alternative to workflows requiring precise laws, meshes, and long simulations.
  • PINNs are a central PIML method for solving forward and inverse problems with noisy data.
  • PINNs have useful capabilities but also important limitations.
  • Conservative PINNs and extended PINNs are scalable extensions for big data or large models.
  • Temporal decompositions help scale PINNs over long time horizons.
  • Causal sweeping provides a unified strategy for training time-dependent PINN problems in physically meaningful order.

Important Terms

Physics-Informed Machine Learning

Machine learning that incorporates physical laws, constraints, or data from scientific systems.

Physics-Informed Neural Network

A neural network trained to satisfy differential-equation residuals and data constraints.

Conservative PINN

A PINN variant designed to respect conservation laws across decomposed domains.

Extended PINN

A domain-decomposition PINN method for scaling to larger or more complex domains.

Causal Sweeping

A training strategy for temporal problems that prioritizes earlier times before later times.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture synthesizes many early PINN-focused talks: vanilla PINN promise, failure modes, and scalable repairs.
  • It connects to CPINNs because both try to address limitations of standard residual training.
  • It also links to operator learning and Neural Galerkin schemes as alternative responses to PINN scaling problems.

Practical Caution

PINNs can be effective in selected applications, but the training strategy matters. Large time-domain problems can fail if trained without decomposition, causality, or conservation structure.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that PINNs are a framework, not one algorithm. Scaling them requires decomposing physics, space, time, and training dynamics.

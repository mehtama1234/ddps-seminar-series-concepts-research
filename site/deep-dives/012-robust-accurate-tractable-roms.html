Talk 12 · University of Michigan

Karthik Duraisamy: Towards Robust, Accurate And Tractable Reduced-Order Models

This lecture frames practical ROM development around three simultaneous requirements: robustness, accuracy, and tractability for multiscale multiphysics systems.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Projection ROMs are attractive because they replace a huge full-order system with a lower-dimensional model, but complex flows such as combustion dynamics combine stiffness, transport, acoustics, heat release, changing topology, and nonlinear residuals. A ROM that is accurate on one condition may become unstable, inconsistent, or too expensive when the physics or parameters change.

First-Principles Model

A ROM approximates the governing equations on a lower-dimensional manifold. For nonlinear systems, the reduced coordinates do not automatically make the residual cheap, stable, or conservative. Robust ROM design therefore needs structure-preserving variables and discretely consistent residual minimization for stability, adaptivity for parameter changes, and hyper-reduction for reducing residual evaluation cost.

Key Claims

  • Reduced-order modeling means solving the same governing equations on a lower-dimensional manifold.
  • Complex multiphysics flows create ROM challenges through stiffness, multiscale structure, transport, and topology changes.
  • Basic Galerkin ROMs can be appealing theoretically but may be unstable in difficult regimes.
  • Least-squares Petrov-Galerkin-style formulations can improve discrete consistency and robustness.
  • Hyper-reduction is required because nonlinear residual evaluation can still scale with the full-order model size.
  • Tractability matters most when full-order simulations are so expensive that even generating training data becomes difficult.

Important Terms

Robust ROM

A reduced model that remains stable and physically consistent under challenging dynamics or parameter changes.

Least-Squares Petrov-Galerkin

A projection approach that minimizes a discrete residual over a trial space rather than only enforcing a standard Galerkin condition.

Structure-Preserving Transformation

A change of variables designed to preserve conservation, symmetry, conditioning, or other important mathematical properties.

Hyper-Reduction

Reducing online cost by evaluating nonlinear residuals or operators at selected samples instead of all full-order degrees of freedom.

Component ROM

A reduced model built for a subsystem that can be coupled with other reduced or full-order components.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture synthesizes the earlier ROM talks into an engineering checklist: accuracy alone is not enough.
  • It connects to hybrid twins because a deployable digital twin needs stable reduced components, not just fast surrogates.
  • It also links to entropy-stable schemes and differentiable simulation by emphasizing consistency between numerical structure and learned/reduced models.

Practical Caution

A ROM can fail even when the basis is good if the residual is inconsistent, the nonlinear terms are not hyper-reduced, or the operating condition changes the flow topology. Always evaluate stability, cost, and parameter adaptivity together.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that reduced dimension is not the same as reduced difficulty. Practical ROMs must preserve structure, adapt to changing physics, and reduce nonlinear evaluation cost.

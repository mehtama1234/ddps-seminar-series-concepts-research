Talk 59 · University of Pittsburgh

Ben Erichson: Continuous Networks For Sequential Predictions

This lecture views recurrent neural networks for time-series prediction as discretized dynamical systems and shows how numerical integration, SDEs, and multiscale ODEs improve robustness and long-term sequential modeling.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Sequential prediction models are often deployed in scientific settings such as fluids and climate where robustness matters. Standard recurrent units can be sensitive to perturbations and may not reflect the continuous-time structure of the underlying system.

First-Principles Model

A recurrent network can be interpreted as a time-discretized dynamical system. Once viewed this way, numerical analysis becomes architecture design: forward Euler resembles a simple residual update, higher-order integrators such as explicit midpoint can improve accuracy, SDE formulations add stochastic robustness, and multiscale ODEs can represent long-term dependencies at multiple time scales.

Key Claims

  • Time-series neural networks can be analyzed through the lens of dynamical systems.
  • Out-of-the-box deep networks may not be suitable for mission-critical scientific applications because of adversarial sensitivity.
  • Continuous-time recurrent neural networks can be more robust and accurate than traditional recurrent units.
  • Classical numerical time integrators can improve neural sequential prediction.
  • Explicit midpoint integration can outperform simpler forward Euler discretization.
  • Connections to stochastic differential equations and multiscale ODEs support robust long-term sequence modeling.

Important Terms

Continuous-Time Recurrent Network

A recurrent architecture modeled as an underlying continuous-time dynamical system before discretization.

Forward Euler

A first-order numerical time integration scheme that advances a state with one slope evaluation.

Explicit Midpoint

A higher-order time integration method that uses a midpoint slope to improve accuracy.

Stochastic Differential Equation

A differential equation with random forcing or diffusion terms.

Multiscale ODE

An ordinary differential equation representation with dynamics operating across multiple time scales.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects neural architecture design to numerical methods, a recurring theme in PINNs and neural ODE talks.
  • It also complements structure-preserving mechanics and differentiable programming by treating learning models as dynamical systems.
  • It gives a robustness-oriented alternative to purely empirical sequence-model design.

Practical Caution

A recurrent model's discretization is part of the model. Changing the time step, integrator, or noise regime can change stability and robustness, so sequential models need numerical validation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that sequence models are numerical dynamical systems. Better scientific architectures can come from better discretization and stability thinking.

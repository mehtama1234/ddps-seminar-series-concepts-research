Talk 45 · Stanford University

Lexing Ying: Prony's Method, Analytic Continuation, And Quantum Signal Processing

This lecture uses Prony's method—a technique for extracting exponential frequencies and amplitudes from signal samples—to unify three problems: noisy analytic continuation and stable factorization in quantum signal processing.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Many scientific computing problems ask us to recover hidden spectral or exponential structure from limited samples. This is hard because noise, finite sampling, and ill-conditioning (where tiny data errors cause huge errors in the answer) can make frequency identification and analytic continuation unstable. Quantum signal processing adds a related factorization problem where stable phase recovery is essential.

First-Principles Model

Prony's method starts from the assumption that a sampled signal is a sum of exponentials. If that model is right, the frequencies and amplitudes are encoded in algebraic relationships among equally spaced samples. The broader lesson is to exploit latent analytic structure: convert a hard inverse problem into identifying the parameters of a structured representation, while respecting noise and conditioning.

Key Claims

  • Prony's method identifies frequencies and amplitudes from equally spaced samples of exponential sums.
  • The method is old but underappreciated relative to its usefulness in scientific computing.
  • Noisy analytic continuation can be approached using ideas from Prony-style structured signal recovery.
  • Quantum signal processing requires stable factorization of phase factors.
  • The same algebraic insight can connect classical signal processing, complex analytic continuation, and quantum algorithms.
  • Stability is the central practical issue because the inverse problems are sensitive to noise.

Important Terms

Prony's Method

A classical algorithm for recovering frequencies and amplitudes in a signal modeled as a finite sum of exponentials.

Analytic Continuation

Extending a function beyond the region where it is directly known, often an ill-posed problem when data are noisy.

Quantum Signal Processing

A technique for transforming spectra of quantum operators through sequences of controlled phase factors.

Frequency Recovery

Estimating the exponential or oscillatory components that generate observed samples.

Ill-Conditioning

A situation where small data errors can cause large errors in the recovered solution.

Connections Across The Series

Practical Caution

Structured recovery can yield accurate answers, but if the assumed exponential model is wrong or the data are too noisy, Prony-style methods can amplify errors rather than reveal structure.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that good algorithms exploit structure before optimizing. Prony's method works because it turns samples into a parametric spectral model.

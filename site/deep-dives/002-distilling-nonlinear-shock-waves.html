Talk 2 · Courant Institute / NYU

Donsub Rim: Distilling Nonlinear Shock Waves

This lecture explains why classical linear reduced-order models struggle on transport-dominated problems and how nonlinear representations can capture moving shocks and interfaces more efficiently.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Classical ROMs assume a solution manifold can be well represented by a low-dimensional linear space. Transport-dominated problems break that assumption: a pulse or shock moving across a domain can generate many nearly orthogonal snapshots, so linear rank grows even when the underlying motion is simple.

First-Principles Model

For transport, the low-dimensional object is often not the field values themselves but the map that moves information through space. If a feature is translated, the set of snapshots may be high-rank, while the characteristic flow or deformation map may be low-dimensional. Nonlinear model reduction tries to represent that moving geometry directly.

Key Claims

  • Transport-dominated and moving-interface problems are hard for traditional reduced models.
  • Kolmogorov n-width explains why a linear basis can need many modes even for simple moving discontinuities.
  • The solution manifold may be high-rank while the characteristic map that transports the solution is low-dimensional.
  • Nonlinear reduced representations can be built by composing reduced bases with learned or constructed maps.
  • Shock formation creates additional difficulty because the characteristic representation can break or become non-smooth.
  • Deep networks can be interpreted as a way to distill a large solution representation into a smaller nonlinear reduced network.

Important Terms

Transport-Dominated Problem

A PDE problem where motion, advection, shocks, or interfaces dominate the dynamics.

Kolmogorov n-width

A measure of how well a solution manifold can be approximated by an n-dimensional linear subspace.

Characteristic Map

A map describing how points or features move through the domain under transport dynamics.

Nonlinear Reduced Model

A reduced model that approximates a solution manifold using nonlinear coordinates or transformations instead of only a linear basis.

Reduced Deep Network

A specialized compact neural representation intended to approximate a solution family more efficiently than a large generic network.

Connections Across The Series

  • This connects directly to the reduced-order modeling concept page and gives the first major exception to linear ROM intuition.
  • It prepares later lectures on nonlinear manifolds, registration-based reduction, and latent-space simulation.
  • It also explains why data-driven simulation cannot ignore geometry: a simple translation can look high-dimensional in raw coordinates.

Practical Caution

If a ROM fails on shocks or advection, the problem may not be lack of data. The coordinate system may be wrong: the low-dimensional structure may live in transport maps, not in linear combinations of snapshots.

Takeaway

The lecture's key move is to distinguish low-dimensional physics from low-rank snapshots. Moving waves can be simple in the right coordinates and expensive in the wrong ones.

Talk 93 · JuliaHub / Pumas AI / MIT Julia Lab

Christopher Rackauckas: Generalizing SciML And Differentiable Simulation Beyond Continuous Models

This lecture argues that SciML and differentiable simulation should extend beyond ODEs and PDEs to jump processes, stochastic simulations, agent-based models, particle filters, and differentiable Bayesian computation.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Much SciML progress has focused on continuous models such as ODEs and PDEs. But scientific simulation also includes discontinuities, stochastic events, agents, particle filters, MCMC, and hybrid model forms. Differentiating these systems introduces mathematical, numerical stability, and implementation challenges that differ from continuous adjoint methods.

First-Principles Model

Differentiable simulation means making a simulator trainable with respect to unknown functions or parameters. If a scientific model has unknown components, those functions can be replaced with neural networks or trainable models, provided one can differentiate through the simulation procedure. Generalization beyond continuous models requires differentiating through randomness, events, discrete choices, chaotic trajectories, and inference algorithms.

Key Claims

  • SciML has incorporated ODEs and PDEs into deep learning through differentiable simulation.
  • Scientific simulation also includes jump diffusions, agent-based models, and other non-continuous forms.
  • Differentiable simulation can be generalized to chaotic simulations and stochastic simulations.
  • Particle filters and agent-based models require special differentiation approaches.
  • Bayesian inverse problems can require differentiating MCMC methods.
  • As differentiable programming spreads, numerical stability and implementation issues become central.

Important Terms

Differentiable Simulation

A simulation implemented so gradients can be computed through it for learning or optimization.

Jump Diffusion

A stochastic process combining continuous diffusion with discrete jump events.

Agent-Based Model

A simulation of many interacting agents following local rules.

Particle Filter

A sequential Monte Carlo method for estimating hidden states in stochastic systems.

Differentiable MCMC

Gradient-based treatment of Markov Chain Monte Carlo algorithms or their outputs.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture broadens the automatic differentiation and differentiable programming thread.
  • It connects to FP-IRL, stochastic generative models, and Bayesian inverse problems because they require differentiating nonstandard simulation loops.
  • It also provides a software-infrastructure perspective on making SciML general rather than ODE-only.

Practical Caution

A gradient through a stochastic or discrete simulator can be biased, unstable, or implementation-dependent. Differentiability must be matched to the mathematical object being optimized.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that SciML must differentiate scientific computation in all its forms, not just smooth ODE/PDE solvers.

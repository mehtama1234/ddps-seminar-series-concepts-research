Talk 57 · KTH Royal Institute of Technology

Ricardo Vinuesa: Modeling And Controlling Turbulent Flows Through Deep Learning

This lecture surveys deep learning for turbulent flows, including wall-based sensing, transfer learning, nonlinear reduced models, GAN super-resolution, and reinforcement-learning flow control.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Turbulent flows are high-dimensional, nonlinear, multiscale, and expensive to measure or simulate. For wall-bounded turbulence, the flow away from the wall is hard to observe without disturbing it, while control requires fast and reliable estimates of coherent structures.

First-Principles Model

A turbulent flow contains hidden state distributed through space. Sensors, simulations, and neural networks provide partial windows into that state. CNNs can map wall measurements to off-wall velocity fields; transfer learning can reuse structure across Reynolds numbers; autoencoders can build nonlinear ROMs; GANs can reconstruct high-resolution flow from sparse or low-resolution data; reinforcement learning can search for control policies in simulation.

Key Claims

  • Deep learning is increasingly used in CFD and turbulent-flow analysis.
  • Non-intrusive sensing can predict flow away from the wall using wall measurements, avoiding probes that disturb the flow.
  • CNNs can outperform traditional linear models for turbulent-flow prediction.
  • Transfer learning can move learned representations between related Reynolds-number regimes.
  • Autoencoders enable nonlinear reduced-order models for turbulent flows.
  • GANs and reinforcement learning support super-resolution and control, but must be evaluated carefully.

Important Terms

Wall-Bounded Turbulence

Turbulent flow shaped by the presence of solid boundaries, such as channels, wings, or urban surfaces.

Non-Intrusive Sensing

Inferring flow quantities without placing sensors that disturb the flow field.

Transfer Learning

Reusing a model trained on one task or regime to improve performance on a related regime.

Autoencoder ROM

A nonlinear reduced-order model built by encoding high-dimensional flow fields into latent variables and decoding them back.

Flow Control

Actively modifying a flow to achieve goals such as drag reduction or mixing enhancement.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects to Petros Koumoutsakos' AI-fluid-mechanics discussion by showing concrete neural tools for prediction and control.
  • It also connects to ROM talks because autoencoders offer nonlinear alternatives to POD.
  • It reinforces the sensor-inference theme: physical measurements are partial, and models infer the hidden state.

Practical Caution

Turbulent-flow ML can overfit to a Reynolds number, geometry, or simulation setup. Transfer, control, and super-resolution claims should be checked under physical and operational shifts.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that turbulence learning is hidden-state inference plus control under multiscale dynamics. Deep learning helps when its representation matches the measurement and control problem.

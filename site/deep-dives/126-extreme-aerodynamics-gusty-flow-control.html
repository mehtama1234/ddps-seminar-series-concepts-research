Talk 126 · UCLA

Kunihiko Taira: Extreme Aerodynamics: Flow Analysis and Control for Highly Gusty Conditions

This lecture studies how to model and control aircraft in extreme unsteady aerodynamic conditions such as gusts, wakes, and urban-canyon flows.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Small and medium air vehicles can encounter atmospheric disturbances whose sudden forces are far larger than those assumed in traditional aerodynamics. When disturbances are this large, flight control can become difficult or impossible unless the flow is analyzed, modeled, and controlled in real time.

First-Principles Model

Extreme aerodynamics treats unsteadiness as a dominant effect rather than a small variation. Large gusts, wakes, and instabilities interact with the vehicle on time scales comparable to when the flow becomes unstable. The high-dimensional flow must be reduced into control-relevant structures so that computational and data-driven methods can predict sudden forces and design control actions.

Key Claims

  • Adverse weather and urban or mountainous wakes create strong large-scale disturbances.
  • Extreme aerodynamic conditions produce enormous sudden forces.
  • Traditional aerodynamic assumptions are insufficient when unsteadiness is very large.
  • The study focuses on analysis, modeling, and control of extreme aerodynamic flows.
  • High dimensionality and unsteadiness make data-driven and reduced-order approaches valuable.
  • Operations of fixed and rotating wing aircraft can be limited by these flow phenomena.

Important Terms

Extreme Aerodynamics

Aerodynamic regimes with large unsteady disturbances and sudden forces.

Gust

A sudden change in incoming wind velocity or direction.

Flow Control

Actively or passively modifying a flow to achieve a desired aerodynamic outcome.

Transient Force

A force that changes rapidly over time rather than settling into steady behavior.

Unsteady Aerodynamics

Aerodynamics where time-dependent effects are central to the flow and forces.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects fluid dynamics, control, reduced-order models (simplified versions of complex flows), and data-driven analysis.
  • It complements turbulent-flow and aerodynamic-autoencoder lectures by focusing on control under severe sudden changes.
  • It also links to robust decision-making because the system must react under conditions outside the expected design range.

Practical Caution

A controller trained or designed for moderate unsteadiness may fail under extreme gusts. Validation must include the rare high-amplitude events that drive risk.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that when disturbances are large, aerodynamics becomes a fast state-estimation and control problem, not just a steady-flow prediction problem.

Talk 64 · Harvard University / Google Research

Michael Brenner: Scientific Uses Of Automatic Differentiation

This lecture argues that one of the biggest scientific opportunities from the ML revolution is not machine learning itself, but automatic differentiation plus large-scale optimization as a new way to formulate and solve scientific problems.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Many scientific problems are optimization problems hidden inside physics: solve a PDE, design a material, find an unstable flow state, tune a kinetic pathway, infer developmental rules, or design a nonequilibrium protocol. Traditionally, each domain needs hand-derived gradients, specialized adjoints, or brute-force search. That limits the size and flexibility of the problems scientists can attack.

First-Principles Model

Automatic differentiation turns a computation into a differentiable object. If a simulator, loss function, or scientific protocol is written as a differentiable program, gradients can be propagated through it and used for large-scale optimization. This makes optimization a general scientific language: propose an objective, encode the physical computation, differentiate it, and search for designs, hidden states, or governing structures.

Key Claims

  • The tools underlying ML, especially automatic differentiation, can be more scientifically important than ML models themselves.
  • Automatic differentiation enables large-scale optimization for scientific discovery.
  • AD can support new algorithms for solving PDEs.
  • The same framework can design energy landscapes and kinetic pathways for self-assembly.
  • Optimization can discover unstable solutions that organize fluid instabilities.
  • AD-based scientific computation can extend to organismal development, nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, fluid rheology, and protein self-assembly.

Important Terms

Automatic Differentiation

A computational technique for exactly differentiating programs by applying the chain rule through their operations.

Large-Scale Optimization

Solving optimization problems with many variables, often enabled by efficient gradient computation.

Energy Landscape

A function describing the energy of a system across configurations, shaping stability and transitions.

Kinetic Pathway

A sequence of dynamical states through which a system evolves, such as during self-assembly.

Unstable Solution

A mathematically valid state or trajectory that is not stable under perturbations but can organize nearby dynamics.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture is a conceptual backbone for differentiable programming, embedded closure learning, DPC, and inverse design talks.
  • It reframes ML infrastructure as scientific infrastructure: differentiability makes scientific workflows optimizable.
  • It also connects to equation discovery and digital twins because differentiable programs can search over mechanisms and designs.

Practical Caution

Differentiating a computation does not guarantee the objective is scientifically meaningful or numerically well-conditioned. AD gives gradients; scientists still need valid models, constraints, and validation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that differentiability changes what can be optimized. AD turns scientific computation into a design and discovery engine.

Talk 134 · University of Washington

Jingwei Hu: Structure-Preserving Particle Method for Collisional Plasmas

This lecture shows a particle method for collisional plasmas that keeps conservation laws and entropy dissipation intact.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

The Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau equation (a model that tracks how particles distribute in space and speed, their electromagnetic fields, and how Coulomb collisions between charged particles reshape that distribution) describes plasma transport and collisions, but particle simulation methods must preserve physical invariants—quantities that should stay constant or change only in certain ways. A method that breaks mass, momentum, energy, or entropy behavior produces plasma dynamics that violate the physics.

First-Principles Model

The method starts from a variational formulation (a way to write the collision equations as an optimization principle) of the Landau collision operator and regularizes it (smooths out mathematical singularities) to get a particle discretization. Collisional effects become a deterministic effective force that works alongside classical PIC (particle-in-cell, a standard plasma simulation where particles evolve while sending charge/current to a grid that computes fields). The discretization conserves mass, momentum, and energy while dissipating entropy—the disorder in the system monotonically increases, just as real collisions do.

Key Claims

  • The Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau equation is a first-principles model of plasma.
  • The new particle method models transport, electromagnetic fields, and Coulomb collisions together.
  • The method comes from regularizing the variational formulation of the Landau collision operator.
  • The discretization conserves mass, momentum, and energy.
  • It dissipates entropy, keeping the thermodynamic direction of collisions intact.
  • The deterministic effective collision force works naturally alongside standard PIC.

Important Terms

Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau Equation

A kinetic model that couples the distribution of particle velocities, electromagnetic fields, and the collisional rearrangement from Coulomb interactions.

Landau Collision Operator

An equation that models how charged particles redistribute velocities when they collide with each other.

Structure-Preserving Method

A numerical method designed to keep the invariants or physical laws of the continuous system.

Entropy Dissipation

The one-way increase of disorder in the system, consistent with how irreversible collisions always spread energy into randomness.

Particle-in-Cell

A plasma simulation method where individual particles are tracked, and the charge/current they carry is interpolated onto a grid that computes electromagnetic fields.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects structure-preserving numerics, kinetic theory, and plasma particle simulation.
  • It complements moment-enhanced PIC by focusing on collisional invariants and entropy.
  • It reinforces a core DDPS theme: numerical schemes should respect the physics that define the model.

Practical Caution

Preserving invariants is necessary but not enough. The method must also be tested for accuracy and speed on different plasma regimes: damping (how disturbances die away), instability (waves or structures that grow), and shock-like behavior (sharp transitions in density or temperature).

Takeaway

Plasma particle methods should preserve the conservation and dissipation laws of the kinetic equation they are built to solve.

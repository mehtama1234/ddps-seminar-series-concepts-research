Talk 72 · Northwestern University

Jian Cao: Physics-Based AI-Assisted Design And Control In Flexible Manufacturing

This lecture shows how hybrid physics-based and data-driven methods support design, sensing, prediction, and local control in flexible manufacturing processes such as metal additive manufacturing and rapid dieless forming.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Flexible manufacturing aims to produce three-dimensional parts without geometry-specific tooling, but the process physics is hard: properties depend on long process histories, geometric features are complex, and the design/control space is high-dimensional. Pure first-principles models can be too slow or incomplete, while purely data-driven models may miss mechanics and control constraints.

First-Principles Model

A manufacturing process maps design choices and control inputs through process mechanics into microstructure, material state, shape, and mechanical behavior. Physics supplies conservation, mechanics, and process constraints; machine learning supplies fast prediction, active sensing, and adaptive control. The control target is local and in situ: infer what is happening during the process and adjust inputs before defects become locked into the part.

Key Claims

  • Flexible manufacturing benefits from hybrid physics-based data-driven approaches.
  • Metal powder-based additive manufacturing and rapid dieless forming illustrate production without geometry-specific tooling.
  • Fundamental process mechanics, process control, and ML can be integrated to predict mechanical behavior during manufacturing.
  • Machine learning can support active sensing for effective in-situ local process control.
  • Long history-dependent properties are a central difficulty in manufacturing prediction.
  • Complex geometries and high-dimensional design spaces make purely manual or brute-force approaches impractical.

Important Terms

Flexible Manufacturing

Manufacturing methods that can produce varied geometries or products without requiring fixed geometry-specific tooling.

Additive Manufacturing

Layer-wise construction of parts, often using metal powder and laser energy in this talk's examples.

Rapid Dieless Forming

A forming process for three-dimensional parts that avoids dedicated dies for each geometry.

Active Sensing

Using learned models and measurements to decide what process information to collect during operation.

In-Situ Process Control

Control actions taken during manufacturing based on local real-time process information.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects industrial digital twins to actual manufacturing control loops.
  • It also links to active learning and sensing because the model must decide what to measure during the process.
  • It complements materials microstructure talks by showing how process history creates material behavior.

Practical Caution

A manufacturing ML model that ignores process history can miss the real source of material properties. Control needs history-aware state estimation, not just static geometry-to-property prediction.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that manufacturing SciML is closed-loop process modeling. Prediction, sensing, and control must be learned around the physics of how parts are made.

Talk 98 · Dartmouth College

Dimitrios Giannakis: Quantum Information and Data Science for Modeling Classical Dynamics

This lecture applies ideas from quantum information theory to extend operator-based methods for modeling classical dynamics.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Koopman and transfer operators can turn nonlinear dynamics into linear equations by working in spaces of observables (quantities you measure) and probability densities (how likely different states are). But traditional classical methods rely on algebras where multiplying A then B gives the same result as B then A. The lecture asks: what do we gain by representing classical dynamics through quantum ideas, where the order of multiplication matters?

First-Principles Model

A dynamical system changes states over time, but it also changes what we measure and how likely each outcome is. Koopman operators describe how the things we observe change, while transfer operators describe how the probability density changes. The talk rewrites these using multiplication operators and density operators—tools borrowed from quantum mechanics—which lets us handle data fitting and model closure in the same operator language. This approach enables numerical schemes that preserve physical meaning, like keeping densities nonnegative, and lets us build approximations from data using kernel methods.

Key Claims

  • Koopman and transfer operators let you use linear techniques on nonlinear dynamics.
  • Quantum information provides a way to generalize how you represent observables and densities when multiplication order matters.
  • Probability densities can be written as density operators, which are mathematical objects borrowed from quantum mechanics.
  • Data fitting and dynamical closure fit into the same operator-based language.
  • Keeping densities and states nonnegative is crucial because they must stay physically valid.
  • Kernel methods provide a way to build the required operators directly from data.

Important Terms

Koopman Operator

A linear operator that evolves observables (quantities you measure) in a nonlinear dynamical system.

Transfer Operator

An operator that evolves probability densities (how likely different states are) through dynamics.

Density Operator

An operator that represents a statistical state; borrowed from quantum mechanics and used here to describe classical distributions.

Non-Commutative Algebra

A set of mathematical rules where the order of multiplication matters—if you multiply A then B, you may not get the same answer as B then A.

Positivity-Preserving Approximation

A numerical method that keeps densities and state representations nonnegative so they stay physically meaningful.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture extends the operator-based approach across Koopman analysis, model closure, and kernel-based learning.
  • It bridges physics-informed machine learning with information geometry and quantum-inspired mathematical tools.
  • It also connects to weather and climate through assimilation examples and subgrid-scale closure problems.

Practical Caution

The quantum analogy is a mathematical model, not a statement that the classical system behaves like a quantum one. The real value comes from whether the operator form preserves the right mathematical properties and makes computation faster or more accurate.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that switching how you represent observables and densities can make nonlinear dynamics appear more linear, organized, and easier to compute.

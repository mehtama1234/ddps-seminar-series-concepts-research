Talk 98 · Dartmouth College

Dimitrios Giannakis: Quantum Information and Data Science for Modeling Classical Dynamics

This lecture uses ideas from quantum information to generalize operator-theoretic modeling of classical dynamics.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Koopman and transfer-operator methods linearize nonlinear dynamics in spaces of observables and densities, but classical formulations usually rely on commutative algebras. The lecture asks what is gained by representing classical dynamics through the richer language of quantum states and non-commutative operator algebras.

First-Principles Model

A dynamical system evolves states, but it also evolves observables and probability densities. Koopman operators act on observables, while transfer operators act on densities. The talk reformulates these objects using multiplication operators and density operators, analogous to quantum information. This lets data assimilation and closure be written in a common operator-theoretic framework, with structure-preserving numerical schemes such as positivity-preserving approximation and kernel-based implementation.

Key Claims

  • Koopman and transfer operators enable linear methods for nonlinear dynamics.
  • Quantum information provides a non-commutative generalization of classical observable and density representations.
  • Probability densities can be represented as density operators or quantum-state-like objects.
  • Data assimilation and dynamical closure can be formulated in the same operator-theoretic language.
  • Positivity-preserving approximations are important because densities and states must remain physically meaningful.
  • Kernel methods provide a data-driven route to approximating the required operators.

Important Terms

Koopman Operator

A linear operator that evolves observables of a nonlinear dynamical system.

Transfer Operator

An operator that evolves probability densities or measures under dynamics.

Density Operator

An operator representation of a statistical state, common in quantum mechanics and used here for classical dynamics.

Non-Commutative Algebra

An algebra where the order of multiplication matters, as with many operators in quantum theory.

Positivity-Preserving Approximation

A numerical approximation that keeps densities or state representations nonnegative and physically valid.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture deepens the operator-theoretic thread across Koopman learning, closure modeling, and kernel methods.
  • It connects physics-informed machine learning with information geometry and quantum-inspired representations.
  • It also links to climate and geophysical examples through ENSO assimilation and subgrid closure.

Practical Caution

The quantum analogy is a mathematical modeling language, not a claim that the classical system is quantum. The value comes from whether the operator representation preserves useful structure and improves computation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that changing the representation of observables and densities can make nonlinear dynamics look more linear, structured, and computable.

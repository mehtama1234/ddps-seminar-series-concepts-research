Talk 52 · New York University

Benjamin Peherstorfer: Neural Galerkin Schemes With Active Learning

This lecture presents Neural Galerkin schemes that solve high-dimensional evolution equations by fitting neural-network states over time and actively selecting training samples guided by PDE residuals.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

High-dimensional time-dependent PDEs are difficult because uniform sampling of the time-space domain can be wasteful, while neural networks need informative data to realize their approximation power. Static training sets may miss moving localized structures such as waves, fronts, or coherent features.

First-Principles Model

Galerkin methods approximate a PDE solution inside a chosen trial space by enforcing residual conditions. Neural Galerkin replaces the classical trial basis with a neural-network representation whose parameters evolve sequentially in time. Instead of drawing all training points blindly, the method uses residual information to actively collect samples where the current approximation needs them most.

Key Claims

  • Estimating population loss is hard in science and engineering when informative samples are scarce.
  • Neural Galerkin schemes fit network parameters by minimizing PDE residuals sequentially over time.
  • Residual-based sampling provides a self-informed active-learning mechanism.
  • Active data collection is key to making neural approximation capabilities practical in high dimensions.
  • The approach is especially relevant for evolution equations with moving coherent features.
  • The method reframes training data generation as part of the numerical solver rather than a preprocessing step.

Important Terms

Galerkin Method

A numerical method that approximates a solution in a trial space and enforces residual orthogonality or minimization.

Neural Galerkin Scheme

A Galerkin-style PDE solver whose trial space is parameterized by a neural network.

PDE Residual

The amount by which an approximate solution violates the governing differential equation.

Active Learning

A strategy that chooses new training samples based on what would most improve the model.

Evolution Equation

A differential equation describing how a system changes over time.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects to PINNs through residual minimization, but adds a solver-centric sequential and active-learning view.
  • It also connects to ROMs and high-dimensional PDE methods because the neural trial space is an adaptive reduced representation.
  • It reinforces the series' data-generation theme: where you sample can matter as much as what architecture you use.

Practical Caution

Residual minimization can be misleading if the sampling distribution misses important regions or if the residual is small while the solution error is large. Active learning reduces this risk but does not remove the need for validation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that neural PDE solvers should adapt their samples to the dynamics. The training set is part of the numerical method.

Talk 28 · North Carolina State University

Dmitriy Anistratov: ROMs For Thermal Radiative Transfer Based On Moment Equations And POD

This talk builds smaller, faster models for radiation heat transfer by simplifying the transport equation into moment equations and using data to capture the missing pieces.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Thermal radiation heat transfer is high-dimensional (depends on position, time, direction, and photon energy), nonlinear, changes across many scales, and couples tightly to material temperature. Solving the full transport equation directly is expensive. Simple old models like diffusion or P1 approximation run fast but can miss important effects.

First-Principles Model

The method uses nonlinear projection to break down the complex Boltzmann transport equation into a simpler hierarchy of moment equations. The hard part is closure: when you project out details, you lose information that you have to recover. The low-order equations need extra quantities like the Eddington tensor (a measure of how radiation travels in different directions) to stay accurate. POD (proper orthogonal decomposition, a data-compression method) or DMD (dynamic mode decomposition, which finds repeating patterns in time series) compresses these closure terms using examples from expensive full simulations.

Key Claims

  • Thermal radiation heat transfer is a useful testing ground for extreme-energy and radiation-coupled gas dynamics problems.
  • The main cost comes from solving the high-dimensional transport equation.
  • Nonlinear projection can derive low-order moment equations for radiation intensity.
  • The Eddington tensor supplies the closure that moment systems need.
  • POD and DMD can approximate closure information across different conditions and time.
  • Removing the expensive transport equation from online calculations can bring cost down to match simple models while keeping predictive quality under control.

Important Terms

Thermal Radiative Transfer

Predicting how radiation energy moves through a material while the material heats up or cools down by absorbing or emitting that energy.

Boltzmann Transport Equation

An equation describing how radiation intensity (amount and direction of light energy) changes across space, direction, energy, and time.

Moment Equation

A simpler equation created by integrating the transport equation over some of its variables (like direction), reducing the number of unknowns.

Eddington Tensor

A closure term that captures how radiation travels in different directions; needed when you simplify a transport equation into moment form.

POD/DMD Closure

Using data-compression methods (proper orthogonal decomposition or dynamic mode decomposition) to approximate the closure terms from training simulations.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk extends reduced-model ideas beyond fluids and structures to a transport-heavy application.
  • It parallels large-eddy simulation (LES) reduction: both take a high-dimensional physics model and replace removed details with a learned or data-fitted closure.
  • It also demonstrates hybrid physics-data modeling, where the equations set the low-order structure and data fills in the missing closure.

Practical Caution

A simplified radiation model is only as good as its closure—the extra terms that stand in for what was thrown away. To compress the Eddington tensor with POD or DMD, you need training data that covers all the conditions where you'll actually use the model.

Takeaway

The central insight is that moment-based reduced models work by keeping the low-order structure of the physics and using data to learn or compress the closure terms that the projection step creates.

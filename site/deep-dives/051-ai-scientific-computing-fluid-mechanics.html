Talk 51 · Harvard University

Petros Koumoutsakos: Artificial Intelligence And Scientific Computing For Fluid Mechanics

This lecture argues for mixing AI and scientific computing carefully for complex flows, using learned reduced models and multi-agent reinforcement learning while separating real progress from hype.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Fluid mechanics combines nonlinear dynamics, structure at many scales, turbulence, flow-structure interaction (how fluids push and pull on solid objects), and control. AI methods are widely promoted as replacements for traditional simulation, but learning algorithms alone cannot be trusted for complex flows. The problem is how to combine data, numerical solvers, physical insight, and learning algorithms into systems that predict and control flows better than either piece alone.

First-Principles Model

Scientific computing provides discretized physical laws, numerical solvers, and stability constraints. AI provides flexible approximation, representation learning, and control policies. A hybrid system is one where each component does what it is structurally good at: solvers generate or constrain dynamics, learned models approximate the effective low-dimensional behavior, and reinforcement learning (learning by trial and environmental feedback) explores control strategies under physical simulation feedback.

Key Claims

  • AI has benefited from massive hardware and data growth, but fluid-mechanics claims require careful separation of what is proven from what is speculation.
  • Complex flow prediction and control should use combinations of AI and simulations rather than AI alone.
  • Learning reduced models is one hybrid approach: detailed or high-resolution simulations inform a smaller dynamical description.
  • The equation-free method is an important ancestor of learned reduced dynamics.
  • Multi-agent reinforcement learning can be combined with scientific computing for flow-structure interaction and control.
  • Successes and failures both matter because they reveal when the underlying physics structure is necessary.

Important Terms

Computing Alloy

A hybrid system that joins scientific computing, models, solvers, and machine learning into one calculation.

Effective Dynamics

A reduced description that captures how a complex system behaves at the scale you care about.

Flow-Structure Interaction

Coupled motion between a fluid and solid bodies that can move or change shape.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

A method where multiple agents learn by interacting with an environment and each other, adjusting their actions based on rewards.

Scientific Computing

Numerical methods and algorithms that solve math models of physical systems on computers.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture directly extends ideas from Kevrekidis about equation-free methods and effective dynamics into fluid mechanics.
  • It connects to later talks about surrogate acceleration because the goal is not speed alone but accurate prediction and control.
  • It also links to reinforcement learning in scientific control, where the simulation becomes the training environment.

Practical Caution

For fluids, a learned model that looks impressive on one test run can fail when conditions change, when predictions drift over long times, or when control feeds back into the system. Mixing AI and solvers must include numerical and physical checks to catch these failures early.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that AI for fluids should be built as a hybrid scientific-computing system. The right question is not AI or solvers, but which mixture is stable, accurate, and can be controlled.

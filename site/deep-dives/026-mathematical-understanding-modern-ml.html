Talk 26 · Brown University

Yeonjong Shin: A Mathematical Understanding Of Modern Machine Learning

This lecture studies machine learning mathematically by separating approximation, estimation, generalization, and optimization errors, with special attention to PINNs.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Modern ML often works through empirical tricks, but scientific ML needs robustness and reliability. PINNs and neural solvers may look promising, yet without mathematical analysis it is unclear when they actually solve the equations correctly, when they work on new data, or when gradient-based training will find a useful solution.

First-Principles Model

A learning method has several errors. Approximation error comes from the expressiveness of the model class—how much can the model shape itself to match reality. Estimation error comes from finite data, not infinite samples. Generalization error combines these statistical effects—the gap between what you learned on your data and what happens on the world. Optimization error comes from the training algorithm returning a model that is not the ideal minimizer. Scientific ML theory asks how these errors behave when physics constraints and neural networks are combined.

Key Claims

  • The success of ML depends on modeling, gradient-based optimization, data, and computing power.
  • Scientific ML adds physical laws to the standard learning setup.
  • PINNs have strong promise but need consistency and optimization theory.
  • Generalization error and optimization error are distinct and should not be conflated.
  • The talk presents convergence theory for PINNs based on Holder regularization and classical PDE analysis.
  • Mathematical understanding can motivate improved algorithms for training neural networks.

Important Terms

Approximation Error

Error caused by the best available model in the chosen function class still not matching the target.

Estimation Error

Error caused by learning from a finite sample rather than infinite data.

Generalization Error

The gap between learned model behavior and true target behavior outside the training sample.

Optimization Error

Error caused by the training algorithm failing to find the best model in the class.

PINN Consistency

A theoretical property that PINN solutions converge to the correct PDE solution under appropriate limits and assumptions.

Connections Across The Series

  • This is the theory counterpart to the Perdikaris PINN failure lecture: it asks when the method should work in principle.
  • It connects to robustness and UQ because dependable scientific ML needs error decomposition, not just benchmark accuracy.
  • It also helps interpret later operator-learning and neural-solver talks by separating expressiveness from trainability.

Practical Caution

A theorem about the ideal minimizer does not guarantee that a practical optimizer will find it. PINN reliability needs both consistency theory and optimization diagnostics.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that scientific ML needs an error budget. Approximation, data, generalization, and optimization failures are different failure modes.

Talk 82 · Georgia Institute of Technology

Peng Chen: Derivative-Informed Neural Operators

This lecture introduces derivative-informed neural operators for PDE-constrained Bayesian inference, experimental design, and stochastic optimization, where both state maps and input derivatives are needed efficiently.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Optimization and inference problems governed by large-scale PDEs with high-dimensional random parameters require many expensive PDE solves. Classical methods become prohibitive because each sampling or optimization iteration may need statistical estimates, states, derivatives, and sensitivities.

First-Principles Model

A neural operator approximates the map from parameters and controls to a PDE state. For optimization, the derivative of that map with respect to inputs is also crucial. A derivative-informed neural operator trains a reduced-basis architecture to learn both the solution operator and its derivative, reducing the number of expensive PDE solves needed while preserving sensitivity information for downstream algorithms.

Key Claims

  • Large-scale PDE optimization with high-dimensional random parameters appears in Bayesian inverse problems, optimal experimental design, and stochastic optimization.
  • Classical approaches are computationally prohibitive because statistical measures require many expensive PDE solves.
  • Derivative-informed neural operators approximate both the PDE state map and derivatives with respect to input variables.
  • Reduced-basis architectures make the operator scalable to high-dimensional problems.
  • Only a limited number of training data can achieve high accuracy for both PDE solution and optimization solution.
  • Derivative information improves the usefulness of neural operators for optimization, not just prediction.

Important Terms

Derivative-Informed Neural Operator

A neural operator trained to approximate both a PDE solution map and its input derivatives.

Bayesian Inverse Problem

An inverse problem where unknown parameters are inferred probabilistically from data.

Optimal Experimental Design

Choosing data-acquisition settings that maximize expected information or utility.

Stochastic Optimization

Optimization under uncertainty in parameters, data, or objectives.

Reduced Basis Architecture

A neural architecture that represents outputs in a lower-dimensional basis for efficiency.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture extends neural operators toward inverse problems and design, where sensitivities matter.
  • It connects to active subspaces, Bayesian calibration, and differentiable programming because all rely on derivative or sensitivity structure.
  • It also complements ROM talks by using reduced bases inside operator learning.

Practical Caution

A neural operator that predicts states accurately may still be poor for optimization if its derivatives are wrong. Sensitivity validation is essential when the surrogate drives decisions.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that optimization needs more than fast forward prediction. It needs trustworthy derivatives of the learned physics map.

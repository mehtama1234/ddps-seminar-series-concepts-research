Talk 124 · IBM T.J. Watson Research Center

Kyongmin Yeo: Reducing Data Resolution for Better Reconstruction: Super-Resolution of Navier-Stokes Flows

This lecture analyzes super-resolution for noisy Navier-Stokes observations, showing why deliberately reducing data resolution can improve reconstruction.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Physical super-resolution aims to recover high-resolution states from low-resolution observations, but noisy measurements create a tradeoff: keeping every fine observation preserves nominal resolution while also preserving noise. The problem is how to recover the ground-truth state of a 2D Navier-Stokes flow from noisy data.

First-Principles Model

The method first averages observations on a coarse grid, intentionally reducing resolution to suppress noise. It then uses a dynamic observer to reconstruct the fine-scale flow state. The first-principles idea is that reconstruction quality is not monotone in measurement resolution when observations are noisy; a coarser but cleaner signal can be better input to a physics-aware observer than a fine but noisy signal.

Key Claims

  • Super-resolution reconstructs high-resolution information from low-resolution data.
  • For physical systems, empirical success needs theoretical understanding.
  • The talk studies noisy observations of 2D Navier-Stokes flows.
  • Averaging over a coarse grid reduces noise at the cost of losing resolution.
  • A dynamic observer then reconstructs the ground-truth state.
  • The key tradeoff is resolution versus noise reduction.

Important Terms

Super-Resolution

Reconstructing high-resolution information from lower-resolution data.

Dynamic Observer

A dynamical-system estimator that uses measurements and model evolution to infer hidden state.

Navier-Stokes Flows

Fluid flows governed by the Navier-Stokes equations.

Coarse-Grid Averaging

Averaging measurements over larger spatial cells to reduce noise.

Noisy Observation

A measurement corrupted by random or systematic error.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects data assimilation, observer design, super-resolution, and fluid reconstruction.
  • It complements sensor-placement and state-estimation lectures by showing that measurement preprocessing can improve inference.
  • It also links to weak-form and denoising themes because noise handling is central to scientific ML.

Practical Caution

Reducing resolution helps only if the lost fine-scale information can be recovered by the dynamics and observer. Over-averaging can erase features the model cannot reconstruct.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that better reconstruction can come from cleaner measurements, not simply higher-resolution measurements.

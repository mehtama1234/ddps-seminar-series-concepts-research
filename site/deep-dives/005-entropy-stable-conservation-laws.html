Talk 5 · Rice University

Jesse Chan: Entropy Stable Schemes For Nonlinear Conservation Laws

This lecture explains entropy stability as a way to build high-order discretizations of nonlinear conservation laws that stay reliable near shocks, turbulence, and features too small to resolve.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

High-order numerical methods can be accurate on smooth PDE solutions but unreliable on nonlinear conservation laws. Shocks, turbulence, and under-resolved gradients can cause the solution to blow up unless the method adds filtering, limiting, or artificial viscosity. Those fixes can work, but they create a tuning tradeoff between reliability and accuracy.

First-Principles Model

A nonlinear conservation law transports conserved variables through fluxes. For many physical systems there is an entropy or energy-like quantity that should satisfy an inequality. An entropy-stable discretization is built so the semi-discrete numerical scheme respects the same entropy inequality, making stability a built-in property rather than a repair added when the scheme starts to fail.

Key Claims

  • Burgers, shallow water, Euler, Navier-Stokes, and MHD systems can be viewed through nonlinear conservation laws with entropy structure.
  • High-order DG methods are attractive because they combine geometric flexibility with high-order accuracy.
  • Under-resolved shocks and turbulence make naive high-order schemes unreliable.
  • Traditional stabilization through artificial viscosity, filtering, or slope limiting can reduce accuracy and require tuning.
  • Entropy-stable schemes use entropy variables, numerical fluxes, and discrete entropy inequalities to build reliability into the scheme.
  • The method can be extended beyond collocation-style methods toward modal DG and reduced-order modeling.

Important Terms

Nonlinear Conservation Law

A PDE expressing that conserved quantities change through fluxes (flow) across boundaries.

Entropy Inequality

A mathematical inequality for an energy-like quantity (one that increases with disorder, never decreases) that physically valid solutions must satisfy.

Entropy Variables

Variables obtained by taking the derivative (rate of change) of the entropy with respect to the conservative quantities.

Discontinuous Galerkin Method

A high-order finite-element-style discretization that represents solutions separately in each region and connects regions through numerical fluxes.

Artificial Viscosity

Added numerical diffusion used to stabilize features too small to resolve, often at the cost of accuracy.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture adds the numerical-analysis side of reliability to the ML-heavy talks: physical validity depends on discretization structure too.
  • It connects to fluid simulation and reduced-order modeling because learned or reduced models inherit stability issues from the equations they approximate.
  • It is a useful counterweight to learned surrogates without validation: before learning the solution, understand what the numerical scheme must preserve.

Practical Caution

Do not evaluate a learned fluid surrogate only by short-horizon error. If the underlying conservation-law structure or entropy behavior is wrong, under-resolved shocks and turbulence can create unstable or incorrect rollouts that violate conservation.

Takeaway

Entropy stability is the idea that reliability should be built from the physics of the PDE, not added after the scheme starts failing.

Talk 60 · CWI, Dutch National Institute for Mathematics and Computer Science

Benjamin Sanderse: Structure-Preserving Learning Of Embedded Discrete Closure Models

This lecture explains how neural closure models for PDEs should be learned while embedded inside discretized solvers, with structure preservation such as kinetic-energy conservation to avoid instability.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Closure models represent the effect of unresolved small scales on resolved large scales in LES (Large Eddy Simulation), ROMs (Reduced Order Models), and other simplified descriptions. Neural networks can fit closures accurately when trained in isolation, but once inserted into a numerical PDE solver they can become unstable because the training environment differs from where they actually operate.

First-Principles Model

A closure is not a standalone prediction task; it is a piece of a time-stepping numerical method. Learning it outside the solver optimizes the wrong situation. Embedded differentiable training places the closure inside the discrete PDE solver during learning so gradients show how closure errors affect future states. Adding structure-preserving forms, such as kinetic energy conservation, provides stability guarantees even when errors occur.

Key Claims

  • The closure problem is a fundamental challenge in fluid flow and reduced-order modeling.
  • Neural networks can construct highly accurate closure models, but directly inserting them into physics models is prone to numerical instability.
  • Instability arises partly because the training environment does not match the prediction environment.
  • Closure models should be learned while embedded in the discretized PDE solver.
  • Differentiable programming enables gradients through the solver but creates adjoint and time-integration challenges.
  • Preserving kinetic energy conservation can provide stability guarantees.

Important Terms

Closure Model

A model that captures the effect of unresolved small scales on resolved large-scale variables.

Embedded Learning

Training a learned component while it sits inside the numerical solver where it will be used.

Discrete Closure

A closure designed for the discretized PDE system rather than only the continuous equation.

Adjoint Problem

The backward sensitivity calculation used to compute gradients through a solver or time-stepping process.

Kinetic Energy Conservation

A structure-preserving property that prevents artificial energy growth in certain fluid models.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture directly connects to reduced-order model closures, Large Eddy Simulation, differentiable programming, and structure-preserving learning.
  • It also clarifies a recurring failure mode: a neural component trained in isolation may fail when coupled to physics.
  • It complements talks on PINN failures by focusing on solver-embedded stability rather than residual loss alone.

Practical Caution

Do not train a closure as a standalone supervised prediction model and assume it will remain stable when running forward in time. The closure must be tested and preferably trained in the numerical environment where it will operate.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that closure learning is coupled numerical modeling. Stability comes from embedding the learned object in the solver and preserving the right invariants.

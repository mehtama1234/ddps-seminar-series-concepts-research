Talk 90 · University of Michigan

Krishna Garikipati: Fokker-Planck Inverse Reinforcement Learning

This lecture connects inverse reinforcement learning with Fokker-Planck mean-field dynamics to infer both reward and transition functions from observed stochastic trajectories.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Inverse reinforcement learning tries to infer a reward function from observed agent behavior, but conventional IRL usually assumes a known transition function. In many scientific and biological systems, such as cancer cell dynamics, the transition dynamics are not directly accessible and must be inferred along with reward.

First-Principles Model

An MDP describes states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards, and policies. If agent dynamics in state-action space are modeled by SDEs, the corresponding probability density evolves by a Fokker-Planck equation. The lecture proposes an isomorphism between time-discrete Fokker-Planck dynamics and MDP structure, enabling FP-IRL to infer transition, reward, and policy from observed trajectories using variational system identification.

Key Claims

  • IRL estimates an unknown reward function from observed agent trajectories.
  • Most IRL methods require a transition function that is known or estimated beforehand.
  • Unknown transition dynamics make scientific IRL significantly harder.
  • SDE dynamics in state-action space can be represented at the mean-field level by the Fokker-Planck equation.
  • A time-discrete Fokker-Planck to MDP isomorphism can support physics-aware IRL.
  • FP-IRL can simultaneously infer transition and reward functions from observed trajectories.

Important Terms

Inverse Reinforcement Learning

Inferring the reward function that explains observed behavior.

Markov Decision Process

A framework with states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards, and policies.

Fokker-Planck Equation

A PDE describing the time evolution of a probability density for stochastic dynamics.

Transition Function

The probability rule for moving from one state to another under an action.

Variational System Identification

Estimating system functions by optimizing a variational or weak-form objective.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture links stochastic dynamics, inverse problems, and reinforcement learning in a scientific setting.
  • It complements Fokker-Planck, SDE, and stochastic model reduction themes elsewhere in the series.
  • It also illustrates a broader motif: using physics structure to make a learning problem identifiable.

Practical Caution

Inferring reward from behavior is inherently ambiguous. Physics constraints can reduce ambiguity, but the inferred reward should not be interpreted causally without domain validation.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that behavior inference needs both objective and dynamics. Fokker-Planck structure supplies the missing dynamics side of IRL.

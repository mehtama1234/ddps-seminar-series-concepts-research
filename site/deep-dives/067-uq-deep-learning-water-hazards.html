Talk 67 · University of Manchester

Ajay Harish: Uncertainty Quantification And Deep Learning For Water-Hazard Prediction

This lecture applies uncertainty quantification and deep learning to storm-surge and water-hazard prediction, where flow, debris, structure, and infrastructure uncertainties all affect damage assessment.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Storm surges, tsunamis, and coastal flooding can impose severe loads on urban infrastructure. The impact is not just plain water height; it depends on uncertain initiation conditions, wave-surge interactions, floating debris, sediment, material properties, and structural response. Damage assessment must quantify these uncertainties for decision-making.

First-Principles Model

A water-hazard model maps uncertain hazard inputs and uncertain structural properties into uncertain damage outcomes. Bayesian calibration estimates uncertain parameters from data; neural networks and surrogate models accelerate repeated evaluations; UQ propagates uncertainty to risk and damage metrics. The goal is not only a forecast, but a quantified distribution of possible impacts on infrastructure.

Key Claims

  • Storm surge is the difference between observed and regular sea level caused by a storm system.
  • Surge combined with waves and high tides can greatly raise mean water levels and flood coastal areas.
  • Urban infrastructure impact modeling is complicated by uncertainty in both flow and structures.
  • Uncertainty sources include initiation conditions, floating debris, sediments, and material properties.
  • Bayesian calibration and neural networks can help characterize and quantify extreme-event impacts.
  • Water-hazard prediction needs damage assessment, not only hydrodynamic prediction.

Important Terms

Storm Surge

An abnormal rise in sea level caused by storm winds and pressure, above regular tide levels.

Water Hazard

A damaging water-driven event such as storm surge, tsunami, flood, or wave impact.

Damage Assessment

Estimating the likely physical or economic damage caused by a hazard event.

Bayesian Calibration

Updating uncertain model parameters using observed data and prior information.

Urban Infrastructure Risk

The vulnerability of buildings, roads, utilities, and coastal assets to hazard impacts.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture is an applied UQ case study that complements uncertainty propagation and SHM talks.
  • It connects to digital twins because resilient infrastructure decisions need fast calibrated hazard models.
  • It also links to surrogate modeling: extreme-event UQ requires many model evaluations under uncertainty.

Practical Caution

A deterministic flood map can hide the true risk. Hazard decisions should account for uncertainty in the event, environment, debris, structures, and model calibration.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that hazard prediction is uncertainty propagation into decisions. Deep learning is useful when it helps quantify risk faster and more completely.

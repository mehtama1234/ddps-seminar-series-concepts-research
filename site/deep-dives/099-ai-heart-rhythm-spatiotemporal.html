Talk 99 · University of California, San Francisco

Jan Christoph: Predicting Heart Rhythm Disorders from Spatio-Temporal Imaging Data Using AI

This lecture explains how AI can infer hidden cardiac electrical wave dynamics from observable mechanical imaging data.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Heart rhythm disorders arise from abnormal electrical waves moving through cardiac tissue, but current imaging cannot easily resolve full three-dimensional electrophysiological dynamics inside the heart wall. Clinicians and scientists need a way to infer dangerous hidden electrical patterns from measurements that are easier to obtain.

First-Principles Model

Cardiac cells are electrically excitable nonlinear oscillators. Their coordinated excitation creates reaction-diffusion waves, and those electrical waves trigger mechanical contraction and deformation. If electrical activity leaves a measurable mechanical signature, then a model can learn the inverse map from high-speed fluorescence or ultrasound imaging of deformation back to electrical dynamics. Ex vivo isolated-heart experiments provide paired training data where the hidden electrical state and visible mechanical motion can both be measured.

Key Claims

  • Heart rhythm disorders can be understood as abnormal nonlinear wave activity in cardiac tissue.
  • Electrical excitation and mechanical deformation are coupled, so mechanics can contain information about electrophysiology.
  • AI models can learn to predict electrical dynamics from spatiotemporal imaging data.
  • High-speed fluorescence and ultrasound imaging provide complementary data sources.
  • Ex vivo isolated-heart training enables supervised learning with paired electrical and mechanical measurements.
  • The long-term goal is better prediction and understanding of arrhythmia mechanisms.

Important Terms

Electrophysiology

The study of electrical activity in biological tissue, here cardiac tissue.

Reaction-Diffusion System

A system where local reactions and spatial diffusion combine to create propagating patterns or waves.

Spatiotemporal Imaging

Imaging that captures both spatial structure and time evolution.

Ultrasound Imaging

A medical imaging modality that uses sound waves to observe tissue motion and structure.

Ex Vivo Training

Training data collection using organs or tissue outside the living body under controlled experimental conditions.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects physics-informed learning, inverse problems, and biomedical imaging.
  • It mirrors broader DDPS themes where inaccessible states are inferred from observable proxy measurements.
  • It also links nonlinear waves and reaction-diffusion dynamics to real clinical prediction problems.

Practical Caution

A model trained ex vivo may not transfer directly to in vivo clinical settings. Differences in tissue conditions, imaging quality, patient variability, and interventions must be validated carefully.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that coupled physics can make an invisible biological state inferable from a visible proxy signal.

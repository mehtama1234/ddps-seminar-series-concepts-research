Talk 100 · University of California San Diego

Boris Kramer: Structure-Preserving Learning of High-Dimensional Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Systems

This lecture explains why reduced models of mechanical systems should preserve Lagrangian and Hamiltonian structure instead of only matching past data.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

High-dimensional mechanical systems are expensive to simulate, but naive learned reductions can break energy conservation, momentum conservation, symmetry, or stability. Such breaks may seem tiny over short time windows and then add up into poor predictions over long horizons.

First-Principles Model

Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics express how systems evolve through structured quantities like energy, momentum, and generalized coordinates (the minimal variables needed to describe the system's position). A structure-preserving learning pipeline first shrinks the dimension and then learns the motion in coordinates that keep the same mechanical form. By requiring the reduced model to follow this structure, the learner needs fewer examples and builds more stable and accurate predictions over long horizons than a model that just copies numbers without respecting the structure.

Key Claims

  • Many scientific and engineering systems are naturally modeled by Lagrangian or Hamiltonian mechanics.
  • Quantities such as momentum, energy, and vorticity are not just measurements after the fact; they are woven into how the system works.
  • Structure-preserving reduced models can be applied to structural mechanics, soft robotics, and wave motion.
  • Preserving symmetry or positive definiteness (matrices that point in consistent directions) can improve accuracy over long prediction horizons.
  • Structured methods can need less training data than unstructured alternatives.
  • The goal is not just small error on past data but dynamics that stay stable and predictable far into the future.

Important Terms

Lagrangian Mechanics

A way of writing how systems move based on kinetic energy (motion) minus potential energy (stored) and the idea that nature takes paths that make the total action (a sum of energies over time) stationary.

Hamiltonian Mechanics

A way of writing how systems move based on total energy and how the system's state (position and velocity) evolves in a space where position and momentum are treated symmetrically.

Structure-Preserving ROM

A reduced-order model (a smaller approximation) designed to keep the physical or geometric structure of the original system intact.

Long-Time Prediction

Making guesses about what happens over periods where tiny structural errors add up into large failures in what actually occurs.

Positive Definiteness

A property of matrices or operators (mathematical objects that transform data) that is often tied to stability, energy, and whether a problem has a well-defined solution.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture ties together geometric mechanics (the math of shapes and symmetries), reduced models, learning operators (functions that transform whole functions), and scientific machine learning.
  • It complements thinking about symplectic structure (a way that preserves certain properties) and physics-informed learning by putting conservation and structure at the center.
  • It reinforces the seminar-wide theme that building in what you already know about the problem can reduce how much data you need to learn.

Practical Caution

Structure preservation only helps if the structure you choose actually matches the system and how you split time into steps. Using the wrong mechanical form can create false confidence in predictions.

Takeaway

The core lesson in mechanics is that respecting the shape of the equations matters more than fitting the next state alone.

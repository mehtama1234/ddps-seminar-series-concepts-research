Talk 100 · University of California San Diego

Boris Kramer: Structure-Preserving Learning of High-Dimensional Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Systems

This lecture shows why reduced models of mechanical systems should preserve Lagrangian and Hamiltonian structure rather than only fit trajectories.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

High-dimensional mechanical systems are expensive to simulate, but naive learned reductions can violate energy, momentum, symmetry, or stability. Such violations may look small over short horizons and then accumulate into poor long-term predictions.

First-Principles Model

Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics encode dynamics through structured quantities such as energy, momentum, and generalized coordinates. A structure-preserving learning pipeline first reduces dimension and then learns dynamics in coordinates that maintain the relevant mechanical form. By constraining the reduced model to respect this structure, the learner needs fewer examples and obtains more stable, robust, long-horizon predictions than a purely black-box fit.

Key Claims

  • Many scientific and engineering systems are naturally modeled by Lagrangian or Hamiltonian mechanics.
  • Quantities such as momentum, energy, and vorticity are not just diagnostics; they are part of the system's structure.
  • Structure-preserving ROMs can be applied to structural mechanics, soft robotics, and wave propagation.
  • Preserving symmetry or positive definiteness can improve long-term predictive accuracy.
  • Structured methods can require less training data than non-structured alternatives.
  • The target is not just low reconstruction error but stable predictive dynamics.

Important Terms

Lagrangian Mechanics

A formulation of mechanics based on kinetic minus potential energy and variational principles.

Hamiltonian Mechanics

A formulation of mechanics based on energy and phase-space evolution.

Structure-Preserving ROM

A reduced-order model designed to retain the physical or geometric structure of the original system.

Long-Time Prediction

Prediction over horizons where small structural errors can accumulate into large qualitative failures.

Positive Definiteness

A matrix or operator property often tied to stability, energy, and well-posedness.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects geometric mechanics, ROMs, operator learning, and scientific machine learning.
  • It complements symplectic and physics-informed learning ideas by making conservation and structure central.
  • It also reinforces the course-wide theme that inductive bias can reduce data requirements.

Practical Caution

Structure preservation helps only if the chosen structure matches the system and discretization. Imposing the wrong mechanical form can create false confidence.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that in mechanics, respecting the geometry of the equations can matter more than fitting the next state.

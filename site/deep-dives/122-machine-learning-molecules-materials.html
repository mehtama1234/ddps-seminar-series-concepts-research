Talk 122 · University of Amsterdam

Max Welling: Machine Learning for Molecules and Materials

This lecture surveys how machine learning is reshaping molecular and materials science through force fields, density-functional modeling, and PDE-based scientific applications.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Natural-science problems such as molecular simulation and materials discovery need accurate models of interactions across scales. Traditional physics can be expensive, while generic ML may ignore symmetries, conservation laws, and physical structure.

First-Principles Model

Machine learning for molecules and materials uses physical inductive biases to learn energy landscapes, forces, electronic structure approximations, and continuum models. ML force fields learn fast approximations to quantum or atomistic calculations. Classical density-functional theory and PDE modeling provide other places where learned components can accelerate scientific prediction while retaining mathematical structure.

Key Claims

  • AI has become a major application domain in the natural sciences, beyond text and images.
  • ML has had major impact in molecular and materials modeling.
  • ML force fields are a central example of learned scientific models.
  • Classical DFT and PDE modeling are important scientific ML application areas.
  • Mathematics, physics, and neuroscience continue to inspire better ML algorithms.
  • Scientific AI may help address large societal challenges such as climate change.

Important Terms

ML Force Field

A learned model that predicts molecular energies and forces for fast simulation.

Density Functional Theory

A physics framework for modeling many-particle systems through density rather than full wavefunctions.

Molecular Simulation

Computational simulation of molecules and their interactions.

Materials Discovery

Searching for materials with desired properties using computation, experiments, and ML.

Physical Inductive Bias

A model design constraint derived from physical principles such as symmetry or conservation.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk connects scientific foundation models, geometric deep learning, physics-aware architectures, and materials discovery.
  • It complements lectures on molecules, PDEs, and neural operators by framing them as part of a broader science-AI transition.
  • It also links to the data-progress theme because high-quality scientific data and simulation labels shape model capability.

Practical Caution

Scientific ML models for molecules and materials must respect symmetries and physical constraints. High benchmark accuracy is not enough if the model fails on out-of-distribution chemistries or materials regimes.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that science ML succeeds when learned approximations are shaped by the geometry and laws of the physical system.

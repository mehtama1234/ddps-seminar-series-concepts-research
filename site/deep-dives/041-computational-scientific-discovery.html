Pat Langley: Computational Scientific Discovery

This lecture presents scientific discovery as heuristic search through spaces of communicable laws and models, guided by data, scientific formalisms, and background knowledge.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Mainstream machine learning often optimizes predictive accuracy without producing scientific theories that researchers can communicate, inspect, and reuse. Scientific discovery requires more than fitting curves: it requires searching a structured hypothesis space for laws or models that explain phenomena in a formal language scientists understand.

First-Principles Model

A discovery system needs three ingredients: observations to explain, a representational space of possible laws or models, and search heuristics that move through that space. The central constraint is communicability: the result should be a law, equation, qualitative mechanism, or process model that can be stated in an established scientific formalism. Background knowledge narrows the search and makes the candidate models interpretable.

Key Claims

  • Computational discovery can be framed as heuristic search over candidate laws, hypotheses, or models.
  • Scientific formalisms distinguish discovery systems from generic data mining because the output must be a communicable theory-like object.
  • Equation discovery searches for numeric laws, but model discovery must also capture qualitative structure and mechanisms.
  • Qualitative explanatory models can be more important than parameter values because they encode causal or process structure.
  • Inductive process modeling combines multivariate time series with background knowledge to infer process-based differential equation models.
  • Domain knowledge is not a nuisance variable; it is what makes search tractable and the result scientifically meaningful.

Important Terms

Heuristic Search

A guided exploration of a large hypothesis space using rules or scores that prefer promising candidates.

Communicable Law

A discovered relationship stated in a compact scientific form, such as an equation or process model, rather than an opaque predictor.

Equation Discovery

Searching for mathematical equations that fit observed data and express an underlying law.

Qualitative Model

A model that explains mechanisms or structural relationships even when exact numeric parameters are secondary.

Inductive Process Modeling

Learning process-based dynamical models from time series using background knowledge and differential equation structure.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects directly to SINDy, symbolic regression, Koopman methods, and Miles Cranmer's discussion of scientific debt.
  • It offers a philosophical bridge between data-driven modeling and traditional scientific explanation.
  • It also clarifies why interpretable surrogate models matter: a predictor is not automatically a scientific discovery.

Practical Caution

A symbolic expression is not automatically a discovered law. If the hypothesis space, background knowledge, or search heuristic is poorly chosen, the system can produce compact but meaningless equations.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that scientific ML should search in the language of science. Data constrains the search, but formalisms and background knowledge make the result communicable.

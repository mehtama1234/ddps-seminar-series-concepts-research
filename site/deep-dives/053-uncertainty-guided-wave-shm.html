Talk 53 · University of Florida

Ishan Khurjekar: Uncertainty-Aware Guided Wave Structural Health Monitoring

This lecture explains how ensemble learning and probabilistic methods can make guided-wave structural health monitoring more robust to uncertainty from temperature, loading, and model mismatch.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Guided-wave SHM systems detect and localize damage by comparing acoustic wave signals across sensor arrays. Real structures experience temperature, loading, and environmental variations that change wave propagation, so damage-like signal changes may arise even when the structure is healthy. Vanilla neural networks can be overconfident under these shifts.

First-Principles Model

A guided-wave monitoring system sends waves through a structure and records received signals. Damage changes those signals, but nuisance variables such as temperature also change them. Uncertainty-aware learning treats detection and localization as probabilistic inference: ensembles, variational autoencoders, adversarial perturbations, and anomaly detection estimate both predictions and confidence under variation.

Key Claims

  • Structural health monitoring is important for buildings, bridges, aircraft, oil rigs, and other infrastructure.
  • Guided waves are useful for damage detection and localization because sensor arrays can transmit and receive acoustic waves.
  • Temperature and loading variations create uncertainty by changing wave propagation even without damage.
  • Deep learning methods need uncertainty awareness because ordinary networks can be brittle or overconfident.
  • Ensembles of variational autoencoders can improve robustness to temperature variation.
  • Adversarial training on simulated perturbations can improve robustness to wave-number uncertainty.

Important Terms

Structural Health Monitoring

Sensing and analysis methods for detecting, locating, and characterizing damage in structures.

Guided Wave

An acoustic wave constrained by a structure, often used to probe damage over distances.

Damage Localization

Estimating where damage is located in a structure.

Ensemble Learning

Combining multiple models to improve robustness and estimate uncertainty.

Variational Autoencoder

A probabilistic autoencoder that learns a latent distribution and can be used for anomaly detection.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects to uncertainty propagation and robust surrogate modeling through engineering decision risk.
  • It also links to active learning and data augmentation because environmental variation must be represented during training.
  • It is a concrete applied example of why uncertainty estimates are operationally important.

Practical Caution

Damage detection is safety-critical. A model must distinguish damage from environmental shift and must express uncertainty when the input lies outside the training distribution.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that monitoring is not just classification. It is inference under environmental variation, and uncertainty is part of the output.

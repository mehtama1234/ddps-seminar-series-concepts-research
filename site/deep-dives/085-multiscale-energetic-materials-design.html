Talk 85 · University of Iowa

H. S. Udaykumar: Data-Driven Multi-Scale Simulations For Energetic Materials

This lecture describes data-driven multiscale simulation for energetic materials, using machine-learning closures, surrogate models, deep learning, transfer learning, and multifidelity strategies to build structure-property-performance design loops.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Energetic material sensitivity and performance depend on physics at nano, micro, meso, and macro scales. Atomistic simulations must inform mesoscale models, and mesoscale models must provide closures for macroscale response. Directly resolving all scales in practical design loops is impossible.

First-Principles Model

Materials-by-design requires a structure-property-performance map: the link from material structure to properties to final performance. Multiscale simulation works upward: atomistic mechanisms inform mesoscale localization, mesoscale ensembles define closures (how to represent unresolved small-scale effects in the larger-scale model), and macroscale models predict performance. Machine learning supplies data-driven closures and surrogates that upscale unresolved localization physics and enable optimization over microstructures.

Key Claims

  • Energetic material sensitivity requires upscaling physics from nano-, micro-, and meso-scales to macro-scale response.
  • Atomistic simulations must inform mesoscale models; mesoscale models must provide closures (learned models of unresolved effects) for macroscale simulations.
  • Machine-learning algorithms can provide data-driven closures for unresolved subgrid localization physics.
  • Materials-by-design depends on structure-property-performance relationships.
  • Surrogate modeling with ensembles of mesoscale calculations is one route to structure-property-performance design.
  • Deep learning, transfer learning, and multifidelity modeling (combining simulations of different accuracy and cost) can accelerate learning across related energetic materials.

Important Terms

Energetic Material

A material that stores and releases chemical energy rapidly, used in propellants, explosives, and related systems.

Scale Bridging

Passing information from simulations at one physical scale to models at a larger scale.

Data-Driven Closure

A learned model that represents unresolved small-scale effects in a larger-scale simulation.

Structure-Property-Performance

The chain from material structure to material properties to final performance.

Multifidelity Modeling

Combining data or simulations of different accuracy levels and computational costs.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects materials microstructure machine learning with multiscale closure learning.
  • It complements industrial digital-twin and manufacturing talks by focusing on design loops for materials.
  • It reinforces the closure theme: unresolved physics must be summarized accurately at each step up for higher-scale prediction.

Practical Caution

Transferring knowledge between energetic materials is useful but risky. Similar material families can still differ in sensitivity mechanisms, so transfer learning needs validation across all scales.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that materials-by-design is a multiscale closure problem: you must represent unresolved small-scale physics accurately at each step up. Machine learning is most valuable when it carries the right unresolved physics upward.

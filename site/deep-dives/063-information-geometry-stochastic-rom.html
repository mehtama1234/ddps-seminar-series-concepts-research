Talk 63 · University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

Zorin Mitran: Data-Driven Information Geometry For Stochastic Model Reduction

This lecture extends reduced-order modeling from flat straight subspaces to curved statistical manifolds, using data-driven information geometry for systems with randomness and variation.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Standard ROMs often project data onto linear subspaces using Euclidean least squares. For stochastic systems, what we care about are probability distributions, and their natural geometry may be curved rather than flat. In systems far from equilibrium, we may not even know a clean parametric distribution family to start with.

First-Principles Model

Information geometry treats families of probability distributions as manifolds with metrics and curved paths (geodesics). Instead of projecting vectors onto a flat linear subspace, stochastic model reduction projects or transports distributions along curved statistical manifolds. When no parametric family is given, we can build local normal distributions from the data using SVD, and their envelope forms a data-driven statistical manifold.

Key Claims

  • Standard ROMs project onto subspaces and minimize Euclidean distance in flat linear spaces.
  • Stochastic systems can be modeled on manifolds of probability distributions.
  • Information geometry studies statistical manifolds of parametrized probability distributions.
  • For systems far from equilibrium, an explicit parametrized distribution may not be available.
  • A data-driven statistical manifold can be built from envelopes of local normal distributions extracted from SVD of the data.
  • Reduced models are obtained by moving along geodesics, where paths are curved rather than straight Euclidean lines.

Important Terms

Information Geometry

The study of probability distributions as geometric objects; how probability distributions sit on curves and surfaces.

Statistical Manifold

A curved surface whose points each represent one probability distribution.

Geodesic

The shortest or most natural path between two points on a curved surface; generalizes the idea of a straight line.

Local Normal Distribution

A bell-curve (Gaussian) approximation built around small neighborhoods in the data.

Stochastic Model Reduction

Reducing the complexity of models whose states or outputs include randomness and variation.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture broadens the ROM theme from deterministic state reduction to probabilistic distribution reduction.
  • It connects to uncertainty quantification because model states are treated as distributions rather than single point values.
  • It also links to manifold learning talks: the reduced object may sit on a curved learned geometry rather than flat space.

Practical Caution

A Euclidean projection can distort the statistical structure of probabilities. For stochastic systems, the choice of geometry affects the reduced model, so the manifold we construct must match how distributions actually behave in the data.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that stochastic reduction depends on geometry. If what you are modeling are distributions, the reduced space should respect how those distributions sit and move.

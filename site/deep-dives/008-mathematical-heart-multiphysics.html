Talk 8 · EPFL / Politecnico di Milano

Alfio Quarteroni: The Mathematical Heart

This lecture treats the heart as a coupled multiphysics, multiscale simulation problem spanning electrophysiology, mechanics, blood flow, valves, circulation, and coronary perfusion.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Heart function is not a single PDE or a single simulator. Electrical activation drives contraction; contraction deforms tissue; deformation moves blood; valves open and close; coronary flow perfuses the myocardium; and the whole organ couples to external circulation. Each subsystem has different scales, meshes, equations, and numerical requirements.

First-Principles Model

A computational heart model starts from geometry, meshes, and core cardiac physics modules. Electrophysiology models electrical propagation through the myocardium. Active and passive mechanics model contraction and tissue deformation. Fluid dynamics models blood in chambers and vessels. Circulation and perfusion models provide boundary and network coupling. The system-level model is the coupling of these modules.

Key Claims

  • First-principles mathematical models can describe the electrical, mechanical, and fluid-dynamical processes of the heart.
  • The heart is a classical multiphysics problem with multiscale behavior in space and time.
  • Patient-specific geometry can come from CT, MRI, atlas models, or CAD-like solid models.
  • Different meshes may be needed for myocardium electrophysiology/mechanics and chamber blood flow.
  • Core cardiac models include electrophysiology, active mechanics, passive mechanics, fluid dynamics, valve motion, and coronary perfusion.
  • Large finite-element and reduced-order libraries are needed because full models are computationally heavy and uncertainty matters.

Important Terms

Electrophysiology

The modeling of electrical activation and propagation through heart tissue.

Active Mechanics

Cell-level contractile mechanics that generate force in response to electrical activation.

Passive Mechanics

Organ-level tissue deformation due to material elasticity and loading.

Fluid-Structure Interaction

Coupling between blood flow and deforming cardiac walls or valves.

Multiscale Perfusion

Modeling blood supply through large coronary vessels and many smaller intramyocardial vessels using different-scale descriptions.

Connections Across The Series

  • This is the playlist's clean example of a hybrid multiphysics simulator where data-driven methods must respect several coupled physical modules.
  • It connects to ROMs because patient-specific studies and uncertainty quantification need faster surrogates of expensive heart simulations.
  • It connects to differentiable simulation and inverse problems because clinical use often asks for parameter estimation from patient data.

Practical Caution

A model of one cardiac subsystem can be correct but clinically misleading if the coupling is wrong. The hard part is not just solving electrophysiology, mechanics, or flow separately; it is making their time scales, boundary conditions, and feedback loops consistent.

Takeaway

The heart lecture shows what first-principles scientific ML must eventually handle: not a single learned model, but a coupled computational physiology stack.

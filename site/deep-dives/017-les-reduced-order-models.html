Talk 17 · Virginia Tech

Traian Iliescu: Large Eddy Simulation Reduced Order Models

This lecture explains LES-ROMs as reduced-order models for turbulent flows that directly handle unresolved scales by applying ROM filtering and adding models for the effects of missing scales.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Turbulent flows contain many scales of motion that interact with each other. A reduced-order model (a simplified version that captures only the biggest structures) can represent only the largest patterns, so the small-scale motion that was ignored can feed back and change how the large scales evolve, making predictions unstable or wrong. Standard reduced-order models do not automatically solve this problem of accounting for the missing scales.

First-Principles Model

Large eddy simulation divides the flow into two parts using a mathematical filter: the large scales you can track, and the small scales you skip. You then add an extra term that models how the small scales affect the large ones. LES-ROM applies the same idea to reduced-order models: use filters to divide which patterns the model tracks, evolve only the tracked patterns, and add models for how the untracked patterns influence the motion.

Key Claims

  • Reduced-order modeling for turbulence is still an open problem with no single agreed approach.
  • Galerkin reduced-order models express the solution as a sum of basis functions (computed from data) and project the governing equations onto those functions.
  • When you filter nonlinear equations, the small scales you filtered out create an extra term that must be estimated or modeled.
  • Models for the missing effects can be either functional (adding a dissipation-like term) or structural (altering the equations themselves).
  • Practical tricks like pressure stabilization may lack theoretical elegance but are needed to make real calculations work.
  • Scaling laws for models of missing effects may be specific to each problem rather than working across all Reynolds numbers and flow types.

Important Terms

Large Eddy Simulation

A simulation method that tracks large flow patterns directly and adds a model for how untracked small-scale motion affects the tracked patterns.

ROM Filter

A mathematical tool in a reduced-order model that defines which flow scales the model can represent.

Closure Problem

The challenge of estimating how the small-scale motion you cannot track affects the large-scale motion you can track.

Eddy Viscosity

A model that adds damping to represent the mixing effect of small-scale turbulent motion on large-scale flow.

Data-Driven Variational Multiscale ROM

A method that combines multiscale mathematical theory with information learned from simulated or measured flow data to model the effects of small scales.

Connections Across The Series

  • This connects to entropy-stable fluid schemes, interpretable machine learning for fluids, and reduced-order models around the shared problem of accounting for motion at scales you cannot resolve.
  • It is a central warning for neural surrogates of turbulent flow: the untracked small scales still have real effects on the tracked large scales, even if the model ignores them.
  • It also explains why testing a turbulent surrogate model over long prediction windows matters — the missing small scales accumulate errors over time.

Practical Caution

Do not expect one set of numbers or a simple scaling rule to work when the Reynolds number (a measure of flow speed), geometry, or flow conditions change. Turbulent reduced-order models must be tuned and tested for the specific type of flow you care about.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that turbulence reduced-order models are inherently filtered models. If you ignore the small scales, the simplified model is not just smaller in size; it is missing real physics that affects what happens.

Talk 95 · BNZ Energy Inc.

Michael Glinsky: A Physics-Based ROM Capturing The Topology Of Dynamical Manifolds

This lecture presents a physics-based reduced-order model (a fast approximation of expensive simulations) inspired by scattering transforms, renormalization ideas, and projections that preserve the geometric structure of dynamical manifolds (the low-dimensional surfaces on which dynamics evolve).

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

High-energy-density physics simulations like pulsed power liner implosions are expensive and highly nonlinear, yet practical work needs fast surrogates. A model that only interpolates state vectors (numbers describing the system at one moment) may miss the manifold topology (the qualitative shape of the low-dimensional surface) and scale-dependent structure of how the dynamics evolve.

First-Principles Model

The proposed Heisenberg Scattering Transformation applies a structured deep convolutional transform inspired by Mallat's scattering transform (a wavelet-based method that builds stable hierarchical representations) and a Hamiltonian generating functional (a physics-based mathematical object encoding how the system evolves). It projects dynamics into a renormalization or S-matrix basis (coordinates where scales of physics appear naturally) where the evolution stays confined to a low-dimensional complex linear subspace (a flat space of lower dimension). PCA (Principal Component Analysis, finding the main directions in data) identifies this subspace, and an encoder-decoder neural network quantifies topology through an analytic function (a function smooth enough that calculus works on it) on that space.

Key Claims

  • The approach is inspired by Mallat's scattering transformation (a wavelet-based hierarchical method) and a canonical Heisenberg-style perspective on how dynamics work.
  • A logarithmic generating functional of the Hamiltonian (a physics-based mathematical tool) operates on the system state or statistical distribution.
  • The transform projects dynamics onto a renormalization or scattering-matrix basis (coordinates where different scales of physics appear naturally).
  • The low-dimensional complex linear subspace can be identified with PCA (Principal Component Analysis).
  • Topology is quantified using a neural network with decoder/encoder structure that approximates an analytic function (a smooth, well-behaved mathematical function) on the subspace.
  • The method is applied as a fast surrogate for 2D pulsed power liner implosions, including MagLIF (Magnetized Liner Inertial Fusion).

Important Terms

Scattering Transformation

A structured wavelet-based convolutional transform that builds stable hierarchical representations by repeated filtering at different scales.

Heisenberg Scattering Transformation

This talk's proposed physics-based transform for representing dynamics in a renormalization or scattering basis where scales appear naturally.

Renormalization Basis

A set of coordinates where the structure of physics at different scales (how solutions look across small and large distances) appears naturally.

Dynamical Manifold Topology

The qualitative geometric structure of the low-dimensional surface on which the system's evolution happens, including how it twists and folds.

MagLIF

Magnetized Liner Inertial Fusion; a pulsed-power approach to fusion where an imploding metal cylinder compresses fuel at extreme density.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects manifold learning (finding low-dimensional representations of data), reduced-order models (fast approximations), topology (geometric structure), and physics-based transforms.
  • It complements earlier work on charting dynamics and information geometry (the geometry of probability distributions) by showing that the reduced space has real geometric structure, not just coordinates.
  • It also links to high-energy-density surrogate modeling (creating fast approximations of expensive simulations), where preserving structure matters for predictions to stay accurate.

Practical Caution

Topology-aware transforms are effective but specialized. The assumed transform structure must match the actual physics; otherwise the model can preserve the wrong geometric features and fail in realistic scenarios.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that some models must preserve dynamical topology (the actual geometric shape where dynamics live) and scale structure, not just minimize projection error (the gap between the original and simplified versions).

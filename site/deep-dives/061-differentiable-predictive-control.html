Talk 61 · Pacific Northwest National Laboratory

Jan Drgona: Differentiable Programming For Modeling And Control Of Dynamical Systems

This lecture presents differentiable predictive control as a way to merge model predictive control, differentiable dynamical models, scientific machine learning, and constrained offline policy optimization.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Classical model predictive control can handle constraints and dynamics but may be computationally expensive online. Reinforcement learning can optimize policies but may be data hungry and weak at hard constraints. Energy and engineering systems need scalable, data-efficient, constraint-aware control policies that can run on limited hardware.

First-Principles Model

Differentiable predictive control treats a controller as a policy optimized through differentiable models of system dynamics and constraints. It borrows MPC's structure: predict future trajectories, optimize objectives, and enforce constraints. But it moves much of the optimization offline by training policies through differentiable programming, using domain-aware models and constraint penalties or modules.

Key Claims

  • Differentiable programming provides a useful perspective on optimal control of dynamical systems.
  • Differentiable predictive control integrates classical MPC with scientific machine learning.
  • DPC uses domain-aware differentiable dynamical models for offline policy optimization.
  • The method can handle nonlinear constraints during policy training.
  • Case studies include building control and dynamic economic dispatch.
  • Embedded demonstrations show potential computational and memory efficiency for control-as-a-service settings.

Important Terms

Differentiable Predictive Control

A model-based policy optimization method that trains controllers through differentiable predictive models and constraints.

Model Predictive Control

A control strategy that repeatedly optimizes future actions over a prediction horizon subject to dynamics and constraints.

Offline Policy Optimization

Training a control policy before deployment using models, simulations, or datasets.

Nonlinear Constraint

A restriction on states, actions, or outputs expressed through a nonlinear function.

Control As A Service

A deployment model where control algorithms are packaged for reusable or embedded operational use.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects differentiable programming with the control side of the DDPS playlist.
  • It complements RL and flow-control talks by emphasizing model-based constrained policy optimization.
  • It also links to digital twins because deployed control needs fast differentiable models tied to physical systems.

Practical Caution

Offline-optimized policies can fail if the differentiable model is wrong or constraints are only softly enforced. Real deployment needs robustness checks and fallback safety handling.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that differentiability turns control design into trainable computation, but the controller remains only as trustworthy as its model, constraints, and deployment checks.

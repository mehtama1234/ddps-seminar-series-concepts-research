Talk 56 · Siemens Technology

Dirk Hartmann: Machine Learning And Physics-Based Simulations For Industrial Digital Twins

This lecture shows industrial digital twins as hybrid systems where machine learning and physics-based simulation must work together closely to reach the accuracy, speed, and automation that real decisions need.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Industrial assets, products, systems, and infrastructure need digital models that are accurate enough for decisions and fast enough for design, engineering, and operations. Pure high-fidelity simulation is often too slow and demands manual expert effort to set up, while pure machine learning can give wrong answers outside the range of data it learned from. Scaling digital twins across many assets also hits a wall: manual expert modeling effort is expensive and takes time.

First-Principles Model

A digital twin is a computer representation tied to a real asset or process. In industry, it must answer operational questions inside hard constraints: speed, accuracy, ease of upkeep, and cost to deploy. Physics-based models provide structure that makes sense when data runs out; machine learning supplies fast predictions, automatic calibration, and adaptation to new data. What wins in practice is the hybrid workflow—neither pure simulation nor pure machine learning alone gets the job done.

Key Claims

  • Linking the digital and real world demands digital models that are fast enough and accurate enough.
  • Only by tightly joining machine learning and physics-based methods can you reach the speed and accuracy required.
  • Hybrid digital twins are necessary across design, engineering, and operations for complex industrial systems.
  • Industrial deployment asks for the right balance between accuracy and speed rather than the highest accuracy at any cost.
  • Manual expert effort is the main bottleneck stopping faster production of more digital twins.
  • Research should be judged on what matters in the full industrial lifecycle, not only on academic test scores.

Important Terms

Digital Twin

A computer model of a real asset, product, system, or infrastructure that you use to predict or guide what happens.

Hybrid Digital Twin

A digital twin that combines physics-based simulation (rules from natural laws) with machine learning (learning patterns from data).

Industrial Deployment

Putting a method to real use in production with hard limits on cost, speed, maintenance, and who is responsible when things go wrong.

Accuracy-Speed Tradeoff

The practical choice between how exact a model is and how fast it runs.

Expert-Effort Bottleneck

The limit you hit when you need scarce experts to manually build, tune, or fix models, slowing everything down.

Connections Across The Series

  • This talk grounds the series' hybrid physics-machine-learning theme in a concrete industrial systems problem.
  • It ties to surrogate modeling, reduced-order models, active learning, and uncertainty because digital twins need all of them when deployed.
  • It also shows why interpretability and automation matter: they determine whether you can build and maintain many twins at scale.

Practical Caution

An impressive demo twin is not the same as an industrial digital twin that works in real production. Industrial use demands full integration across the system's lifetime, testing against real assets, ways to update the model when things change, and clear responsibility when model predictions guide decisions.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that industrial scientific machine learning is constrained engineering: solve real decisions with real limits. The model that wins is the one that solves the decision problem with enough physics, enough data, and enough automation to deploy and keep running.

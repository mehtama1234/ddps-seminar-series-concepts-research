Talk 78 · University of California, Santa Barbara

Paul Atzberger: Generative Machine Learning For Data-Driven Modeling And Reductions

This lecture covers machine-learning models that create compact representations of complex dynamical systems, using geometric VAEs (a type of neural-network encoder that preserves shape and structure) to capture physically meaningful patterns and stochastic dynamic GANs (two-network systems trained competitively to model random time-evolving processes) to encode randomness in dynamics.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Simulations of soft materials, complex fluids, and biological systems involve many length and time scales plus random fluctuations. Reduced models need to capture nonlinear dynamics and account for randomness, but standard machine-learning models that compress data into hidden layers may ignore geometric structure, smoothness properties, conservation laws, and the numerical stability needed to run simulations over many steps.

First-Principles Model

A generative reduction learns a simulator by extracting patterns from observations or detailed simulations. Geometric VAEs add built-in rules so that the hidden representation respects shape, curvature, and physical laws. SDYN-GANs learn random dynamics as stochastic differential equations (rules for how systems evolve when subject to random forces), including the smooth drift term, the noise scale, and numerical methods that keep multi-step simulation stable. The result is not just compressed data; it is a learned dynamical model you can run forward in time.

Key Claims

  • Machine learning creates opportunities to build reduced-order models and multiscale models of nonlinear scientific simulations.
  • Physical principles and existing scientific knowledge should be added as built-in biases that guide what the model learns.
  • Geometric variational autoencoders can learn representations that preserve shape, topology, smoothness, and adherence to physical laws.
  • Geometric VAEs can build reductions of high-dimensional time-evolving systems and nonlinear partial-differential equations.
  • SDYN-GANs learn random dynamical models from data collected from stochastic systems.
  • SDYN-GAN models use stochastic differential equations and stable numerical methods for multi-step simulation.

Important Terms

Generative Model

A system that learns to create samples or time sequences resembling the actual data.

Geometric Variational Autoencoder

A neural-network encoder that learns compressed representations while preserving shape, topology, smoothness, and physical structure.

Stochastic Dynamic GAN

A two-network system where one generates and one criticizes random dynamical representations, trained competitively.

Stochastic Differential Equation

A rule for how a system evolves when subject to random forces; includes smooth drift plus random noise.

Multi-Step Stochastic Integrator

A numerical method that advances random dynamics forward multiple time steps while maintaining accuracy and stability.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects machine learning with reduced-order models, stochastic model reduction, and learning curved spaces from data.
  • It builds on information-geometry and charting-dynamics talks by focusing on structure in learned hidden spaces.
  • It also relates to uncertainty quantification because random dynamics require probabilistic reduced models, not just deterministic approximations.

Practical Caution

A generative model can produce trajectories that look plausible while violating conservation laws or breaking down numerically. Scientific reductions need structural constraints built in, not just visual or statistical similarity.

Takeaway

The core lesson: generative machine learning for science should learn structured dynamics, not just generate samples. The hidden representation must enable simulation.

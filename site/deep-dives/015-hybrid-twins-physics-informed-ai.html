Talk 15 · ENSAM Institute of Technology / ESI Group

Francisco Chinesta: Empowering Hybrid Twins From Physics-Informed Artificial Intelligence

A hybrid twin combines physics-based models with learned models that predict what the physics model misses, correcting the gap between simulation and reality.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

A physics model is only an approximation of reality. Real systems behave under real loading histories that a pure physics model cannot capture fully. Industrial users increasingly need twins that represent both the known physics and the unmodeled deviation between what the simulation predicts and what actual measurements show. Pure physics may be incomplete; pure data without physical constraints may be unstructured and unreliable.

First-Principles Model

The core idea is this equation: reality equals known physics plus the unknown gap. A hybrid twin keeps the physics-based model for the known part and learns a data-driven model that predicts the gap between predictions and measurements. When you combine the physics model and the gap model, the virtual twin gets closer to the real system and can support both diagnosis (figuring out what is wrong now) and prognosis (predicting future behavior).

Key Claims

  • Industrial modeling is shifting from selling products to guaranteeing performance under real usage.
  • A physics model is an approximation, so mismatch between prediction and measurement should be modeled directly.
  • Hybrid twins combine physics-based simulation with a learned model that predicts the gap.
  • The learned gap model should respect physical rules, including conservation laws when relevant.
  • Once the twin matches reality better, it can support diagnosis from data and prediction of future system behavior.
  • Model reduction, regression, and machine learning are related tools for building accurate approximations in this hybrid approach.

Important Terms

Hybrid Twin

A digital twin that combines mechanistic simulation with learned models that correct or fill gaps in the physics.

Model Gap

The difference between what a physics model predicts and what measurements show the real system actually does.

Diagnosis

Using current observations to infer the present state or discover a defect in a system.

Prognosis

Predicting future system behavior or failure based on the updated and corrected twin.

Thermodynamic Consistency

A rule that learned material or process models must obey the basic laws of thermodynamics and not violate them.

Connections Across The Series

  • This is the clearest DDPS direction for hybrid physics-ML systems: learn the missing part instead of learning the whole system from raw data alone.
  • It connects to fast reduced-order models because these lean physics models often become the core of a real-time twin.
  • It also connects to uncertainty and control because diagnosis and prognosis require the twin to stay aligned with actual observations.

Practical Caution

A digital twin is not just a dashboard or a trained surrogate model. If it does not separate known physics from learned corrections, you cannot tell when the model is predicting outside the range of its evidence, which breaks trust in the results.

Takeaway

The core lesson is that hybrid twins are gap models wrapped around physics: keep what you already know, learn what you do not, and update the twin to match reality.

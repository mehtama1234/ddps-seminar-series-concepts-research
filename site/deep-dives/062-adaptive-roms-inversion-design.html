Talk 62 · Virginia Tech

Eric de Sturler: Cheap And Robust Adaptive Reduced Order Models For Nonlinear Inversion And Design

This lecture explains how randomization and adaptive reduced-order models can reduce the cost of nonlinear inverse and PDE-constrained optimization problems while checking that the stand-in model stays accurate.

YouTube source · Talk page

Core Problem

Nonlinear inverse problems and design optimization often need many repeated large linear, nonlinear, and adjoint solves. Smaller models built from a family of full models (parametric ROMs) can cut cost, but building and running them can still be expensive, and they often have no cheap way to check if they are giving right answers during optimization.

First-Principles Model

Using a stand-in model for optimization only works if the optimizer knows when the stand-in is trustworthy. Randomization can cheaply build reduced bases or check errors and gaps in how well the objective got better. Adaptive ROMs update the smaller model when checking shows it no longer gives right answers, and trust-region logic (an optimization method that takes steps only where the model is believed to be accurate) keeps steps the size the surrogate says is safe.

Key Claims

  • Nonlinear inverse and PDE-constrained optimization problems need many repeated solves.
  • Newton-type methods also need adjoint or transpose-system solves.
  • Smaller models built to approximate a full model across different parameters can greatly cut computation.
  • Computing smaller models online can itself be expensive for these problems.
  • Smaller models built across parameters often have no cheap way to check error.
  • Randomization can help compute smaller models cheaply and check accuracy or how much the objective improved for trust-region methods.

Important Terms

Nonlinear Inverse Problem

A problem of estimating unknown parameters or structures from observations when the relationship between the parameters and what you see is not linear.

PDE-Constrained Optimization

Optimization where the things you are adjusting must obey a differential equation model.

Parametric Reduced-Order Model

A smaller model built to stand in for a full model across a range of different parameter settings.

Randomization

Using randomized numerical methods to cut cost, check error, or pick informative subsets or directions.

Trust-Region Method

An optimization method that limits steps to areas where the model is believed to be accurate.

Connections Across The Series

  • This lecture connects smaller models to inverse design and optimization rather than only running simulations forward.
  • It also ties to checking and controlling errors because using stand-in models requires ways to know if they are reliable.
  • It adds to science and machine learning in industry by asking how smaller models can be made cheap and reliable enough for repeated design loops.

Practical Caution

A reduced model can speed up optimization but steer it the wrong way if its errors are not being watched. Trust-region checks or randomized error tests are part of the algorithm, not optional add-ons you can skip.

Takeaway

The first-principles lesson is that optimization needs trustworthy cheap models, not just cheap models. The stand-in must know when it is safe to guide the next step.

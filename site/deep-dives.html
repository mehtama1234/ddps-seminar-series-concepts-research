Talk 1 · University of Pennsylvania

Paris Perdikaris: When And Why PINNs Fail To Train

This whole series is about getting computers to predict physical behavior by learning from examples instead of only hand-written math. One popular way is to let a learning program (a program with millions of dials it tunes by trial and error until its answers look right) also receive the laws of physics as it learns. This talk is about why that approach, called a PINN, so often refuses to learn well: the trial-and-error tuning gets stuck because some of the physics rules 'shout' much louder than others, so the program never lands on an answer that respects all of them.

Talk 2 · Courant Institute / NYU

Donsub Rim: Distilling Nonlinear Shock Waves

This whole series is about getting computers to predict how physical things behave by learning from examples. One common trick is to use a learning program (a huge network of dials that tunes itself by trial and error until it predicts correctly) to build a small, fast version of a big simulator. This talk focuses on why that fails for one specific type of problem: when something moves or a shock wave travels across the space — linear tricks don't work because motion makes the snapshots messy and high-dimensional, but the actual motion itself is simple. This lecture shows how to find the right low-dimensional description by tracking how things move through space instead of memorizing field values.

Hybrid Twins

Hybrid twins combine mechanistic simulation with learned components and live data to represent, predict, and update physical systems.

Ordinary Problem

Pure simulation may miss unknown effects, while pure data models may fail outside observed regimes.

Naive Picture

The naive view is that a neural network can simply replace a simulator once enough data is available.

Why It Fails

Physical simulation problems mix equations, data, discretization, stability, uncertainty, and scientific validity. A generic ML benchmark can hide failures that matter in the real system.

First-Principles Move

A useful twin should use physics for structure, data for correction, and online updates to stay aligned with the real system.

What Breaks Without It

Without this concept, it is easy to build a model that looks accurate on examples but fails under new parameters, longer time horizons, or scientific constraints.

Summit Role

This theme organizes DDPS seminars related to hybrid twins and gives us a first-pass route for later deep dives.

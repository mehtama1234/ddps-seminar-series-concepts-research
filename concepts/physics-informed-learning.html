data-driven physical simulation

Physics-Informed Learning

Physics-informed learning trains models while constraining them with known governing equations, conservation laws, boundary conditions, or residual penalties.

Ordinary Problem

Pure black-box models can fit data while violating physics, especially when measurements are sparse or extrapolation matters.

Naive Picture

The naive view is that a neural network can simply replace a simulator once enough data is available.

Why It Fails

Physical simulation problems mix equations, data, discretization, stability, uncertainty, and scientific validity. A generic ML benchmark can hide failures that matter in the real system.

First-Principles Move

If the physical law is known, learning should search among functions that are consistent with that law rather than treating every data point as unconstrained supervision.

What Breaks Without It

Without this concept, it is easy to build a model that looks accurate on examples but fails under new parameters, longer time horizons, or scientific constraints.

Summit Role

This theme organizes DDPS seminars related to physics-informed learning and gives us a first-pass route for later deep dives.
